# Card style samples (2026-10-17, approved by Devon)

The two lines a shopper reads in shop mode, and nothing else:
- **Line 1, what to get** (`decision`, ≤10 words): the label words found on the shelf, and the food.
  Both wordings when the shelf uses two.
- **Line 2, why** (first surviving `buying_tips` entry, ≤~80 chars): the one reason that keeps a
  shopper from settling for the near-miss. A why, never an instruction; never repeats line 1.
- Feed first for animal foods: what the animal ate outranks organic and pasture words.
- Kristy's voice: a calm, natural guide beside you. Second person, no first person, no slogans,
  no spec fragments. Fuller reasoning moves to `why`; every fact traces to the entry (claim lock).

| Card | Line 1 | Line 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Beef | 100% grass-fed or grass-finished beef. | "Grass-fed" alone can mean a grain finish; grass to the end makes leaner beef. |
| Ground beef | An organ blend with each organ's percentage, or 100% grass-fed ground beef. | A few percent liver and heart still tastes like regular ground beef. |
| Steak | 100% grass-fed or grass-finished steak, not mechanically tenderized. | How it was raised matters more than the cut; Prime and Choice only grade marbling. |
| Eggs | Soy-free, corn-free eggs from hens on pasture. | Most hens eat corn and soy, organic ones too, and her feed becomes the egg. |
| Milk | Trusted raw milk from a farm, or low-temp pasteurized 100% grass-fed. | Every bit of heat changes milk; 100% grass-fed means grass and hay, never grain. |
| Cheese | Block cheese with a short list: milk, cultures, salt, enzymes. | Bagged shreds are coated in starch to keep them apart, which is why they melt poorly. |
