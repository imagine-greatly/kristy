From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type of clarified butter from India



For other uses, see [Ghee (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee_(disambiguation) "Ghee (disambiguation)").

**Ghee** ([Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi"): घी, IPA: [[ɡʰiː]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu "Help:IPA/Hindi and Urdu")) is a type of [clarified butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarified_butter "Clarified butter"), originating in the [Indian Subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent").[[1]](#cite_note-ghee-1) It is a widely used butter for cooking especially in [Indian cuisines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine "Indian cuisine") and in [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") religious rituals.[[1]](#cite_note-ghee-1) Typically made from [cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") butter or [buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo "Water buffalo") butter,[[1]](#cite_note-ghee-1) ghee is favoured for its nutty flavour and high [smoke point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_point "Smoke point") allowing it to be heated at high temperatures without burning.[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2)

Description
-----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Description")]

Ghee is typically prepared by [simmering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmering "Simmering") [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter "Butter"), skimming any impurities from the surface, then pouring and retaining the clear liquid fat while discarding the solid residue that settles at the bottom.[[1]](#cite_note-ghee-1)[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2)[Spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice "Spice") can be added for flavour. The texture, colour, and taste of ghee depend on the quality of the butter, the milk used in the process, and the duration of boiling.[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2)

Etymology
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Etymology")]

The word *ghee* is borrowed from the [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") word घी (*ghī*), which comes from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language "Sanskrit language"): घृत (**ghṛta-**, IPA: [[ɡʱr̩tɐ]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Sanskrit "Help:IPA/Sanskrit")) 'clarified butter'; traditionally derived from the root घृ, *ghṛ-*, 'to sprinkle',[[3]](#cite_note-3) and so [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate "Cognate") with the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") word χριστός (*khristós*, 'rubbed, anointed'), from which the English word *[Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ "Christ")* is derived.[[4]](#cite_note-4)

In the [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages") spoken in South India, ghee is referred to by terms likely inherited from [Proto-Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Dravidian "Proto-Dravidian") *\*ney* 'oil, ghee': [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") நெய் (*ney*), [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam") നെയ് (*neyŭ*), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language "Telugu language") నెయ్యి (*neyyi*), [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada "Kannada") ನೆಯ್ಯಿ (*neyyi*). See [Reconstruction:Proto-Dravidian/ney](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ "wikt:").

Religious use
-------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Religious use")]

Traditionally, ghee is made from [bovine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine "Bovine") milk, either [cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") or [water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo "Water buffalo"), and has been used in rituals since the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period"). It is a sacred requirement in [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic "Vedic") *[yajña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagna "Yagna")* and *[homa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homa_(ritual) "Homa (ritual)")* (fire rituals), through the medium of [Agni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agni "Agni") (fire) to offer oblations to various deities (See: [Yajurveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajurveda "Yajurveda")).

Fire rituals are utilised for ceremonies such as marriage and funerals. Ghee is required in Vedic worship of *[mūrtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murti "Murti")* (divine deities), with *[aarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarti "Aarti")* (offering of ghee lamp) called *[diyā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diya_(light) "Diya (light)")* or *dīpa* and for *Pañcāmṛta* ([Panchamruta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchamruta "Panchamruta")) where ghee along with [mishri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_candy#Misri "Rock candy"), [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey "Honey"), [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk "Milk"), and *[dahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahi_(curd) "Dahi (curd)")* (curd) is used for bathing the deities on the appearance day of [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna "Krishna") on [Janmashtami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janmashtami "Janmashtami"), *Śiva* ([Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva "Shiva")) on *Mahā-śivarātrī* ([Maha Shivaratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Shivaratri "Maha Shivaratri")). There is a [hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn "Hymn") to ghee.[[5]](#cite_note-5) In the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")*, the *[Kaurava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaurava "Kaurava")* were born from pots of ghee.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

Ghee is also used in *[bhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhang "Bhang")* in order to heat the [cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_(drug) "Cannabis (drug)") to cause [decarboxylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decarboxylation "Decarboxylation"), making the drink [psychoactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive "Psychoactive").[[7]](#cite_note-Gottlieb07-7)[[8]](#cite_note-Drake018-8)

In [Buddhist scripture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), stages of dairy production are used as metaphors for stages of enlightenment. The highest-stage product, [sarpir-maṇḍa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarpir-ma%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Da "Sarpir-maṇḍa"), is theorised to be ghee or clarified butter.[[9]](#cite_note-9)

In [ancient Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Mesopotamia "Ancient Mesopotamia"), ghee was used in the ritual of [Mîs-pî](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%AEs-p%C3%AE "Mîs-pî").[[10]](#cite_note-10)[[11]](#cite_note-11)

Culinary uses
-------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Culinary uses")]

Ghee is common in [cuisines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_cuisine "South Asian cuisine") from the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"), including traditional rice preparations (such as *[biryani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biryani "Biryani")*).[[1]](#cite_note-ghee-1) In [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra "Maharashtra"), polis or [Indian flatbreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_bread "Indian bread") are accompanied with ghee. In [Rajasthan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan "Rajasthan"), ghee often accompanies *[baati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baati "Baati")*. All over [North India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India "North India"), *[roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti "Roti")* is served with ghee. In [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka "Karnataka") and [Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_Nadu "Tamil Nadu"), ghee is provided alongside [dosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dosa_(food) "Dosa (food)") and [kesari bhath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesari_bhath "Kesari bhath"). In [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal "Bengal") (both [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal") and [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh")) and [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat "Gujarat"), *[khichdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khichdi "Khichdi")* is a traditional evening meal of rice with [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil "Lentil"), cooked in a [curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry "Curry") made from dahi (curd), [cumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumin "Cumin") seeds, [curry leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_tree "Curry tree"), [cornflour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_starch "Corn starch"), [turmeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmeric "Turmeric"), [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic "Garlic"), salt and ghee. It is also an ingredient in *[kadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadhi "Kadhi")* and [Indian sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_sweets "Indian sweets"), such as *[Mysore pak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_pak "Mysore pak"),* and varieties of *[halva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halva "Halva")* and *[laddu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laddu "Laddu")*. [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine "Indian cuisine") restaurants typically incorporate large amounts of ghee, sometimes brushing *[naan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naan "Naan")* and *roti* with it, either during preparation or just before serving. In the state of [Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha "Odisha") ghee is widely used in [Odia dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Odisha "Cuisine of Odisha") such as *khechedi* and *dalma*. The [sattvic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattvic_diet "Sattvic diet") food prepared in most temples in Odisha has ghee as a major ingredient in its culinary tradition. Ghee is used in [South Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Indian_cuisine "South Indian cuisine") for tempering curries and in the preparation of rice dishes and sweets. North Indians also add ghee to rice before eating it with pickles, dal and curries. North Indians are among the biggest consumers of ghee. Vegetarian dishes of [Andhra Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh "Andhra Pradesh") especially use ghee for the preparation of savoury and sweet dishes alike. Ghee is important to traditional [North Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_cuisine "North Indian cuisine"), with *[parathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratha "Paratha")*, *[daals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dal "Dal")* and curries often using ghee instead of oil for a richer taste. The type of ghee, in terms of animal source, tends to vary with the dish; for example, ghee prepared from cow's milk ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"): গাওয়া ঘী, gaoa ghi) is traditional with rice, *roti* or as a finishing drizzle atop a curry or *dal*, whereas buffalo milk ghee is more typical for general cooking purposes. Traditionally, ghee ([Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language "Bhojpuri language"): 𑂐𑂲𑂇, gheeu) in the Bhojpuri region is widely used along with mustard oil. Litti is usually served after dipping in ghee.

Ghee is a useful [fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil "Cooking oil") for [deep frying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_fry "Deep fry") because its [smoke point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_point "Smoke point") (where its molecules begin to break down) is 250 °C (482 °F), which is well above typical cooking temperatures of around 200 °C (392 °F) and above that of most vegetable oils.[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2)

Flavour
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Flavour")]

The main flavour components of ghee are [carbonyls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonyls "Carbonyls"), [free fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_fatty_acids "Free fatty acids"), [lactones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactone "Lactone"), and [alcohols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_(chemistry) "Alcohol (chemistry)").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Along with the flavour of milk fat, the ripening of the butter and the temperature at which it is [clarified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarified_butter "Clarified butter") also affect the flavour. For example, ghee produced at 100 °C (212 °F) or lower has a milder flavour, whereas 120 °C (248 °F) results in a strong flavour.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Differences from clarified butter
---------------------------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Differences from clarified butter")]

The production of ghee differs slightly from that of clarified butter. The process of creating clarified butter is complete once the water is evaporated and the fat (clarified butter) is separated from the [milk solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_solids "Milk solids").[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2) However, the production of ghee includes simmering the butter, which makes it nutty-tasting and aromatic.[[12]](#cite_note-Landis-12)[[13]](#cite_note-13)[[14]](#cite_note-:1-14)[[15]](#cite_note-15)

A traditional recipe for ghee is to boil raw milk and let it cool to 43 °C (109 °F). After leaving it covered at room temperature for around 12 hours, add a bit of dahi (yogurt) to it and leave it overnight. This makes more yogurt. This is churned with water, to obtain cultured butter, which is used to simmer into ghee.[[16]](#cite_note-16)

Nutrition
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Nutrition")]

Ghee is 99.5% [fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat "Fat"), 0.2% water, 0.3% [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_(nutrient) "Protein (nutrient)"), and contains no [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate") (table). The fat composition includes 62% of [saturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat "Saturated fat"), 29% of [monounsaturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monounsaturated_fat "Monounsaturated fat"), and 4% of [polyunsaturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyunsaturated_fat "Polyunsaturated fat") (table). In a reference amount of 100 g (3.5 oz), ghee supplies 876 [calories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie "Calorie"), 256 mg of [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol"), [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A "Vitamin A") (93% of the [Daily Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Value "Daily Value"), DV), and [vitamin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_E "Vitamin E") (19% DV), with no other [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient") in significant content (table).

The [British Nutrition Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nutrition_Foundation "British Nutrition Foundation"), [Heart and Stroke Foundation of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_and_Stroke_Foundation_of_Canada "Heart and Stroke Foundation of Canada") and [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization "World Health Organization") advise people to limit or eliminate ghee in food preparation due to its high saturated fat content, using instead plant-based oils high in unsaturated fat, such as [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed_oil "Rapeseed oil"), [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil "Olive oil"), or [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower_oil "Sunflower oil") oils and spreads.[[18]](#cite_note-18)[[19]](#cite_note-19)[[20]](#cite_note-20)

-------------------

There are five common methods through which ghee is prepared. Industrial preparation on the other hand is done by using "white butter", usually sourced from other dairies and contractors.[[21]](#cite_note-Priyanka-21)

[![In the cream butter method, cream separated from milk must be churned to produced butter. The butter then undergoes heat clarification.](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Cream_to_get_clarified_butter_home_made.Ghee.jpg/250px-Cream_to_get_clarified_butter_home_made.Ghee.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Cream_to_get_clarified_butter_home_made.Ghee.jpg)

In the cream butter method, cream is separated from milk and churned to produce butter. The butter then undergoes heat clarification.

### Milk butter

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Milk butter")]

Sour [raw milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_milk "Raw milk") is churned into butter. The butter is boiled in an open pan to allow the water to evaporate. The hot ghee is transferred and stored.

### Direct cream

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Direct cream")]

Fresh cream, [cultured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sour_cream "Sour cream") or washed [cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream "Cream") is directly converted to ghee. This method requires a long heating time and produces a caramelised flavour.

### Cream butter

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Cream butter")]

Milk is separated into cream, which is then [churned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churning_(butter) "Churning (butter)") into butter. The butter undergoes heat clarification to produce ghee.

### Pre-stratification

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Pre-stratification")]

This method is suitable for large quantities of butter. Butter is melted at 80–85 °C (176–185 °F) for 30 minutes. Layers of protein particles, fat and [buttermilk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buttermilk "Buttermilk") are induced. The buttermilk is drained out. The remaining layers of fat are heated to a temperature of 110 °C (230 °F) to remove moisture and develop flavour.[[22]](#cite_note-22)

Packaging
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghee&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Packaging")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Food_Fair_%282025%29_11.jpg/250px-Food_Fair_%282025%29_11.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Food_Fair_(2025)_11.jpg)

Ghee packaged in jars

Ghee is generally packaged in airtight glass jars.[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2) The containers should be stored away from direct sunlight which can produce moisture inside the jar, reducing ghee quality and [shelf life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelf_life "Shelf life").[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2) Sealed jar storage is recommended at 4 °C (39 °F) or colder.[[2]](#cite_note-safety-2)
