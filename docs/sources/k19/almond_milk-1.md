**Almond milk** is a plant-based [milk substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_substitute "Milk substitute") with a watery texture and nutty flavor manufactured from [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond "Almond"),[[1]](#cite_note-1) although some types or brands are flavored in imitation of [cow's milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk "Milk").[[2]](#cite_note-2) It does not contain [cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol "Cholesterol") or [lactose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose "Lactose") and is low in [saturated fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_fat "Saturated fat"). Almond milk is often consumed by those who are [lactose-intolerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose_intolerance "Lactose intolerance") and others, such as [vegans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism"), who do not consume [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_products "Dairy products").

Commercial almond milk comes in sweetened, unsweetened, vanilla and chocolate flavors, and is usually [fortified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_fortification "Food fortification") with [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient") as a [processed food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-processed_food "Ultra-processed food").

Almond milk can also be made at home using a [blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_(appliance) "Blender (appliance)"), almonds and water.[[3]](#cite_note-pros-3)[[4]](#cite_note-4)

Global almond milk sales in 2018 were US$5.8 billion, growing at 14% per year, and forecast to be a $13 billion global market by 2025.[[5]](#cite_note-vn-5)

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Almond_milk&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

The earliest recipes for almond milk date back to at least the 13th century [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"), as it was listed in [Muhammad bin Hasan al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_bin_Hasan_al-Baghdadi "Muhammad bin Hasan al-Baghdadi")’s book *Kitāb al-Ṭabīḫ* (كتاب الطبيخ; *The Book of Dishes*), written in 1226. Almond milk was used as a substitute for animal milk in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") in areas that followed [Catholic fasting laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting_and_abstinence_in_the_Catholic_Church "Fasting and abstinence in the Catholic Church"). Some recipes for almond milk have used wine in the process.[[6]](#cite_note-6) Historian [Carolyn Walker Bynum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_Walker_Bynum "Carolyn Walker Bynum") notes that:

> ... Medieval cookbooks suggest that the aristocracy observed fasting strictly, if legalistically. Meat-day and fish-day recipes were not separated in medieval recipe collections, as they were in later, better-organized cookbooks. But the most basic dishes were given in fast-day as well as
> ordinary-day versions. For example, a thin split-pea puree, sometimes enriched with fish stock or almond milk (produced by simmering ground almonds in water), replaced meat broth on fast days; and almond milk was a general (and expensive) substitute for cow's milk.[[7]](#cite_note-Bynum-7)

Almond milk's popularity as a dairy substitute continued throughout history, going well into modern times. It is even mentioned under the secondary definition of "milk" in [Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Johnson "Samuel Johnson")'s *[Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_the_English_Language "A Dictionary of the English Language")* in 1755, alongside [pistachio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistachio "Pistachio") milk.[[8]](#cite_note-8)

Commerce
--------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Almond_milk&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Commerce")]

In the United States, almond milk remained a niche health food item until the early 2000s, when its popularity began to increase. In 2011 alone, almond milk sales increased by 79%.[[9]](#cite_note-9) In 2013, it surpassed [soy milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_milk "Soy milk") as the most popular plant-based milk in the US.[[10]](#cite_note-soy_milk_fades-10) As of 2014 it comprised 60 percent of plant-milk sales and 4.1 percent of total milk sales in the US.[[11]](#cite_note-11): 2–3

Manufacturers and distributors of animal milk have advocated that plant-based milk not be labelled as "milk". They complain that consumers may be confused between the two, and that plant-based milks are not necessarily as nutritious in terms of vitamins and minerals. In the case of the United States, the FDA said in 2023 that plant-based milk could be labeled as milk, but that producers should note the nutritional differences from regular milk.[[12]](#cite_note-12) European Union regulations require "milk" to be used for animals only, except coconut milk. (See [Plant milk § Labeling and terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_milk#Labeling_and_terminology "Plant milk").)[[13]](#cite_note-13)

Within the Italian regions of [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily "Sicily"), [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia "Apulia"), [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria "Calabria"), and [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania "Campania"), almond milk is a [protected traditional agricultural product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodotti_agroalimentari_tradizionali "Prodotti agroalimentari tradizionali").[[14]](#cite_note-14)

Production
----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Almond_milk&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Production")]

The general production method involves soaking and grinding almonds in an excess of water. A milky white liquid is obtained after filtering the almond pulp (flesh). Almond milk can also be made by adding water to [almond butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond_butter "Almond butter"). In commercial production, almond milk is homogenised with high pressure and pasteurised for greater stability and shelf life.[[24]](#cite_note-27)

Almond milk can be stored in the fridge in an air-tight container (preferably a glass container) for about 4–5 days. However, certain factors, such as the temperature of the refrigerator, sterilization of the blender or storing jar, and surface cleanliness, can decide how many days you can keep it fresh. Storing homemade almond milk for more than a week may reduce its nutrition or even make it unhealthy.[[25]](#cite_note-28)

In July 2015, a [class action lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_action_lawsuit "Class action lawsuit") was filed in New York City against two American manufacturers, [Blue Diamond Growers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Diamond_Growers "Blue Diamond Growers") and [White Wave Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Wave_Foods "White Wave Foods"), for false advertising regarding the small quantity of almonds (only 2%) contained in the final product.[[26]](#cite_note-forbes-29)[[27]](#cite_note-lawsuit-30)[[28]](#cite_note-31)[[29]](#cite_note-32) In October 2015, a judge denied the plaintiff's request for an [injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injunction "Injunction").[[30]](#cite_note-33)

Almond production in California is concentrated mainly in the [Central Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Valley_(California) "Central Valley (California)"),[[32]](#cite_note-bi-35) where the mild climate, rich soil, and abundant sunshine and water supply make for ideal growing conditions. Due to the [persistent droughts in California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_in_California "Droughts in California") in the early 21st century, it became more difficult to raise almonds in a sustainable manner.[[33]](#cite_note-npr-36)[[34]](#cite_note-bloom-37)

Almond sustainability is challenged because of the high amount of water needed to grow almonds: a single glass of almond milk requires roughly 74 litres (16 imp gal; 20 US gal) of water to produce.[[31]](#cite_note-Briggs-34) Among plant-based milks, almond milk requires substantially more water during the growing and production stages than soy, rice or oat milk (graph).[[35]](#cite_note-Holmes-38)[[31]](#cite_note-Briggs-34) Dairy milk requires more water to produce than almond milk (graph).[[31]](#cite_note-Briggs-34) In 2014, California produced 42.3 billion pounds of cow's milk and only 2.14 billion pounds of almond milk.[[36]](#cite_note-39)[[37]](#cite_note-40)

Sustainability strategies implemented by the Almond Board of California and almond farmers include:[[34]](#cite_note-bloom-37)[[38]](#cite_note-abc2018-41)[[39]](#cite_note-42)

* tree and [soil health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_health "Soil health"), and other farming practices
* minimizing dust production during the harvest
* [bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee "Bee") health
* [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation "Irrigation") guidelines for farmers
* food safety
* use of waste [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass "Biomass") as coproducts with a goal to achieve zero waste
* use of [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy "Solar energy") during processing
* job development
* support of scientific research to investigate the potential health benefits of consuming almonds
* international education about sustainability practices
