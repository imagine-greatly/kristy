**Soy milk** (or **soymilk**), also known as **soya milk**, is a [plant-based milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_milk "Plant milk") produced by soaking and grinding [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean "Soybean"), boiling the mixture, and filtering out remaining particulates. It is a [stable emulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emulsion#Appearance_and_properties "Emulsion") of oil, water, and protein. Its original form is an intermediate product of the manufacture of [tofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofu "Tofu"). Originating in China,[[3]](#cite_note-3) it became a common beverage in Europe and North America in the latter half of the 20th century, especially as production techniques were developed to give it a taste and consistency more closely resembling that of [dairy milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_milk "Dairy milk"). Soy milk may be used as a substitute for dairy milk by individuals who are [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegan "Vegan") or [lactose-intolerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose-intolerant "Lactose-intolerant") or have a [milk allergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_allergy "Milk allergy").

Soy milk is also used in making imitation dairy products such as [soy yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_yogurt "Soy yogurt"), [soy cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cream "Plant cream"), soy [kefir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir "Kefir"), and soy-based [cheese analogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_analogue "Cheese analogue").[[4]](#cite_note-bw-4)[[5]](#cite_note-bim-5) It is also used as an ingredient for making [milkshakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkshake "Milkshake"), [pancakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancake "Pancake"), [smoothies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothie "Smoothie"), bread, [mayonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayonnaise "Mayonnaise"), and [baked goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking "Baking").[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6)

Names
-----

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Names")]

In some parts of China, the term 豆浆 *[dòujiāng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%B2uji%C4%81ng "Dòujiāng")* (lit. "bean [broth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broth "Broth")") is used for the traditional watery liquid, used locally as a drink or broth, produced as an intermediate product in the production of tofu,[[7]](#cite_note-7)[[8]](#cite_note-8) while store-bought products that are designed to imitate the flavor and consistency of dairy milk (and may contain a mixture of dairy and soy) are more often known as 豆奶 *dòunǎi* ("bean milk").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Naming in the EU

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Naming in the EU")]

In the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union"), "milk" by law refers exclusively to "the normal mammary secretion obtained from one or more milkings without either addition thereto or extraction therefrom".[[9]](#cite_note-9) Only [cow's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle "Cattle") milk is allowed to be named "milk" on packaging, and any other milks must state the name of the respective animal: for example, "[goat milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat_milk "Goat milk")" or "[sheep milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_milk "Sheep milk")". There are exceptions for traditional products such as coconut milk.[[10]](#cite_note-10) The usage of the term "soy milk" became the subject of a 2017 court case before the [Court of Justice of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union "Court of Justice of the European Union") after a German [consumer protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_protection "Consumer protection") group filed an [unfair competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfair_competition "Unfair competition") complaint about a company describing its soy and tofu products as 'milk' or 'cheese'. The Court of Justice ruled that such designations may not be used for purely plant-based products and that additions indicating the plant origin of the products (*soy* milk) does not influence that prohibition.[[11]](#cite_note-11)

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: History")]

The earliest record of soybean milk is on a stone slab of the [Eastern Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han_dynasty "Eastern Han dynasty") unearthed in China, on which is engraved a description of the making of soy milk in ancient kitchens.

A tofu broth (*doufujiang*) c. 1365 was used during the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire "Mongol Empire") [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty "Yuan dynasty").[[1]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20135_&_23%E2%80%934-1)[[2]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20149_&_127-2) As *[doujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doujiang "Doujiang")*, this drink remains a common watery form of soy milk in China, usually prepared from fresh soybeans. The [compendium of Materia Medica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium_of_Materia_Medica "Compendium of Materia Medica"), which was completed in 1578, also has an evaluation of soymilk. Its use increased during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty "Qing dynasty"), apparently due to the discovery that gently heating *doujiang* for at least 90 minutes [hydrolyzed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolyze "Hydrolyze") or helped to break down its undesirable [raffinose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffinose "Raffinose") and [stachyose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stachyose "Stachyose"), [oligosaccharides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligosaccharide "Oligosaccharide"), which can cause [flatulence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatulence "Flatulence") and digestive pain among [lactose-intolerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactose-intolerant "Lactose-intolerant") adults.[[12]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi201323%E2%80%934-12)[[13]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuang200852-13) By the 18th century, it was common enough that [street vendors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_vendor "Street vendor") were hawking it;[[14]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi201329-14) in the 19th, it was also common to take a cup to [tofu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofu "Tofu") shops to get hot, fresh *doujiang* for breakfast. It was already often paired with [youtiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youtiao "Youtiao"), which was dipped into it.[[15]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20135_&_33-15) The process was industrialized in early [Republican China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_(1912%E2%80%931949) "Republic of China (1912–1949)"). By 1929, two Shanghai factories were selling over 1000 bottles a day, and another in Beijing was almost as productive itself.[[16]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20136-16) Following disruption from the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War") and the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War "Chinese Civil War"), soy milk began to be marketed in soft drink-like fashion in Hong Kong, Singapore, and Japan in the 1950s.[[17]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20137%E2%80%938-17)

Soymilk was mentioned in various European letters from China beginning in the 17th century.[[18]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20135-18) "Soy milk" entered the English language (as "soy-bean milk") in an 1897 [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA "USDA") report.[[19]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTELangworthy1897-19)[[20]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi2009174-20)[Li Yuying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Shizeng "Li Shizeng") established Caséo-Sojaïne, the first soy milk "dairy", in [Colombes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombes "Colombes"), France, in 1910; he received the first British and American patents for soy milk manufacturing in 1912 and 1913.[[16]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20136-16) J.A. Chard began production of "Soy Lac" in New York City, United States, in 1917.[[16]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20136-16) Harry W. Miller—an American businessman forced to relocate his factory from Shanghai owing to [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War "Second Sino-Japanese War")—was similarly compelled by the US Department of Agriculture and the US dairy industry to use the term "Soya Lac" rather than "soy milk".[[16]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20136-16)[John Harvey Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harvey_Kellogg "John Harvey Kellogg") had been working with what he called "soymilk" at his [Battle Creek Sanitarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Creek_Sanitarium "Battle Creek Sanitarium") since 1930, but was similarly compelled to market his [acidophilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidophilus "Acidophilus")-enriched beverage as "Soygal" when it began commercial production in 1942.[[21]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi2004-21)

A string of 40 court cases against Rich Products between 1949 and 1974 finally established that non-dairy "milks" and imitation dairy products were "a new and distinct food", rather than inferior and illegal knock-offs.[[16]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20136-16)[Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University "Cornell University") researchers established the [enzyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme "Enzyme") [lipoxygenase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoxygenase "Lipoxygenase") as responsible for the "[beany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean "Bean")" flavor of soy milk made in 1966; the same research established a process for reducing or eliminating the bean flavor from commercial products.[[22]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20138-22)[[23]](#cite_note-23) With [Tetra Pak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetra_Pak "Tetra Pak") [cartons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carton "Carton") extending soy milk's [shelf life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelf_life "Shelf life"), Hong Kong-based [Vitasoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitasoy "Vitasoy") reintroduced the product to the US market in 1980 and brought it to 20 other countries within a few years.[[22]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20138-22)[Alpro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpro "Alpro") similarly began production in [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium") in 1980, quickly becoming Europe's leading producer.[[22]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20138-22) New production technology and techniques led to soy beverages with an appreciably more milk-like flavor and consistency in the mid-1980s.[[24]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShurtleffAoyagi20138%E2%80%939-24)

Preparation
-----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Preparation")]

Soy milk is made from whole soybeans or full-fat soy flour.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25) The dry beans are soaked in water for a minimum of three hours up to overnight depending on the temperature of the water. The rehydrated beans then undergo wet grinding with enough added water to give the desired solids content to the final product which has a protein content of 1–4%, depending on the method of production.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25) The ratio of water to beans on a weight basis is 10:1 for traditional soy milk.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25) The resulting [slurry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry "Slurry") or purée is brought to a boil to improve its taste properties (see "Soy odor" below), to inactivate soybean [trypsin inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trypsin_inhibitor "Trypsin inhibitor") proteins, and to sterilize the product.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25)[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6) Heating at or near the boiling point is continued for a period of time, 15–20 minutes, followed by the removal of insoluble residues ([soy pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_pulp "Soy pulp") fiber) by straining/filtration.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25)

Processing requires the use of an [anti-foaming agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-foaming_agent "Anti-foaming agent") or natural defoamer during the boiling step. Bringing filtered soy milk to a boil avoids the problem of foaming. It is generally opaque, white or off-white in color, and approximately the same consistency as cow's milk.[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25) Quality attributes during preparation include [germination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germination "Germination") time for the beans used, [acidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidity "Acidity"), total [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein") and [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate"), [phytic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytic_acid "Phytic acid") content, and [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity "Viscosity").[[25]](#cite_note-jiang-25) Raw soy milk may be sweetened, flavored, and [fortified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_fortification "Food fortification") with [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient").[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6) Once fully processed, soy milk products are typically sold in [plastic bottles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_bottle "Plastic bottle") or [plastic-coated cartons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic-coated_paper "Plastic-coated paper"), such as [tetrapaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetra_Pak "Tetra Pak").[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6)

### Soy odor

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Soy odor")]

Traditional East Asian soymilk has a "beany" odor, partly of [hexanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexanal "Hexanal"), considered disagreeable by most Westerners. This is caused by the [lipooxygenase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipooxygenase "Lipooxygenase") (LOX) in the soy oxidizing the fat in the beans. Rehydrating the beans allows the reaction to proceed with the oxygen gas dissolved in soaking water.[[26]](#cite_note-26) To eliminate the odor, one can either disable the LOX enzyme with heat or remove the oxygen dissolved in the water. The former can be achieved by soaking beans in hot water (a "hot grind"), skipping the soak entirely, or [blanching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanching_(cooking) "Blanching (cooking)") the soy in water or steam first.[[27]](#cite_note-27) The latter can be achieved by a variety of chemical means, such as adding glucose and [glucose oxidase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_oxidase "Glucose oxidase") to consume the oxygen.[[28]](#cite_note-28) The soybean cultivar also influences the odor[[29]](#cite_note-29) and a mutant cultivar lacking LOX completely has been produced.[[30]](#cite_note-30)

The issue and preference of soy odor also affects products made from soymilk, especially tofu. See [Tofu § Flavor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tofu#Flavor "Tofu").

Commerce
--------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Commerce")]

With soybean production increasing worldwide during the early 21st century,[[4]](#cite_note-bw-4) and consumer interest in plant milks growing from demand in Asia, Europe, and the United States,[[4]](#cite_note-bw-4)[[5]](#cite_note-bim-5)[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6) soy milk became the second-most consumed plant milk (after [almond milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond_milk "Almond milk")) by 2019.[[31]](#cite_note-clara-31)[[32]](#cite_note-lucas-32) Soy milk sales declined in the United States during 2018–19,[[5]](#cite_note-bim-5)[[32]](#cite_note-lucas-32) mainly due to the rising popularity of almond milk and loss of market share to the successful introduction of [oat milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat_milk "Oat milk").[[33]](#cite_note-vp-33)

Based on the latest data from Grand View Research, the soy milk market reached USD 5.51 billion in 2024 and is estimated to reach USD 7.4 billion by 2030. The estimated CAGR is 5.1% from 2025 to 2030.[[34]](#cite_note-34) Growth in consumption was due mainly to expanding the flavors of sweetened soy milks and uses in desserts, whereas unsweetened soy milk was being used particularly in Asia-Pacific countries as an ingredient in [snacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snack "Snack") and various prepared foods.[[35]](#cite_note-grand-35)

### Nutrition

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Nutrition")]

A [cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_(unit) "Cup (unit)") (243 mL) serving of a generic unsweetened commercial nutrient-fortified brand of soy milk provides 80 [calories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calories "Calories") from 4 g of [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrates "Carbohydrates") (including 1 g of sugar), 4 g of fat and 7 g of [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein "Protein").[[36]](#cite_note-:0-36) This processed soy milk contains appreciable levels of [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A "Vitamin A"), [B vitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_vitamins "B vitamins"), and [vitamin D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D "Vitamin D") in a range of 10 to 45% of the [Daily Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Value "Daily Value"), with [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium "Calcium") and [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium "Magnesium") also in significant content.[[36]](#cite_note-:0-36)

It has a [glycemic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index "Glycemic index") of 34±4.[[37]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAtkinsonFoster-PowellBrand-Miller2008-37) For [protein quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_quality "Protein quality"), one study gave soya milk a [Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestible_Indispensable_Amino_Acid_Score "Digestible Indispensable Amino Acid Score") (DIAAS) of 78% for infants, 99% for young children, and 117% for older children, adolescents, and adults, with the [limiting amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limiting_amino_acid "Limiting amino acid") for those groups being [leucine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leucine "Leucine"), [lysine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysine "Lysine"), and [valine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valine "Valine") respectively.[[38]](#cite_note-38) A DIAAS of 100% or more is considered to be an excellent/high protein quality source.[[39]](#cite_note-39)

#### Phytic acid

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Soy_milk&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Phytic acid")]

Soybeans, and soy milk in particular, contain [phytic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytic_acid "Phytic acid"),[[47]](#cite_note-50) which may act as a [chelating agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelation "Chelation") and inhibit mineral absorption, especially for diets already low in minerals.[[48]](#cite_note-51)[[49]](#cite_note-52) However, dietary intake of phytic acid may help reduce the risk of developing colon cancer.[[50]](#cite_note-53)[[51]](#cite_note-54)

Soy milk is a common beverage in [East Asian cuisines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_cuisine "East Asian cuisine").

* In [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine "Chinese cuisine"), "sweet" soy milk is made by adding [cane sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cane_sugar "Cane sugar") or [simple syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_syrup "Simple syrup"). "Salty" or "savory" soy milk is often combined with chopped [pickled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickled "Pickled") [mustard greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_greens "Mustard greens"), [dried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried "Dried") [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp "Shrimp"), *[youtiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youtiao "Youtiao")* [croutons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crouton "Crouton"), chopped [spring onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_onion "Spring onion"), [cilantro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilantro "Cilantro"), [pork floss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rousong "Rousong"), and/or [shallots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallot "Shallot"), along with vinegar, [sesame oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_oil "Sesame oil"), [soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce "Soy sauce"), and/or [chili oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_oil "Chili oil"). Both are traditional breakfast foods, served hot or cold [depending on the season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine "Traditional Chinese medicine") or personal preference. At breakfast, it is often accompanied by [starchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch "Starch") [carbohydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate")-rich foods like *[mantou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantou "Mantou")* (a thick, fluffy kind of roll or bun), *[youtiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youtiao "Youtiao")* (deep-fried dough sticks), and *[shaobing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaobing "Shaobing")* (sesame flatbread).
* [Japanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cuisine "Japanese cuisine") uses soy milk to make *[yuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuba_(food) "Yuba (food)")* and as an occasional [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_soup "Base soup") for *[nabemono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabemono "Nabemono")*.
* In [Korean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine "Korean cuisine"), soy milk is used as the broth for making *[kongguksu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongguksu "Kongguksu")*, a cold [noodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noodle "Noodle") [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup "Soup") eaten mostly in summer.
* In [Taiwanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_cuisine "Taiwanese cuisine"), soy milk is a popular breakfast item, often served with Chinese donuts (you tiao).

In many countries, soy milk is used in [vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veganism "Veganism") and vegetarian food products and as a substitute replacement for cow's milk in many recipes.[[31]](#cite_note-clara-31)[[4]](#cite_note-bw-4) Soy milk is also used in making imitation dairy products such as [soy yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_yogurt "Soy yogurt"), [soy cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_cream "Plant cream"), soy [kefir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefir "Kefir") and soy-based [cheese analogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_analogue "Cheese analogue").[[4]](#cite_note-bw-4)[[5]](#cite_note-bim-5) It is also used as an ingredient for making [milkshakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkshake "Milkshake"), [pancakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancake "Pancake"), [smoothies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoothie "Smoothie"), bread, [mayonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayonnaise "Mayonnaise"), and [baked goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking "Baking").[[6]](#cite_note-bharti-6)

Ecological effects
------------------

Using [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean "Soybean") to make milk instead of raising cows is [ecologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology "Ecology") advantageous.[[52]](#cite_note-Holmes-55)[[53]](#cite_note-56) Cows require much more energy to produce milk, since the farmer must feed the animal, which can consume up to 24 kilograms (53 lb) of food in dry matter basis and 90 to 180 litres (24 to 48 US gal) of water a day, producing an average of 40 kilograms (88 lb) of milk a day. [Legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume "Legume"), including the soybean plant, also replenish the [nitrogen content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_fixation#Legume_family "Nitrogen fixation") of the soil in which they are grown.[[54]](#cite_note-57)

The cultivation of soybeans in South America is a cause of [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation "Deforestation")[[55]](#cite_note-wwf-deforestation-58) (specifically in the Amazon rainforest) and a range of other large-scale environmental harm.[[56]](#cite_note-wwf-59) However, the majority of soybean cultivation worldwide, especially in South America where cattle farming is widespread, is intended for [livestock fodder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_feed "Animal feed") rather than soy milk production.[[55]](#cite_note-wwf-deforestation-58)
