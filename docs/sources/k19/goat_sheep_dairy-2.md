From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dairy product

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Sheepparlour.jpg/250px-Sheepparlour.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sheepparlour.jpg)

Lacaune dairy sheep in rotary parlour, [Aveyron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveyron "Aveyron"), France

**Sheep milk** is the [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk "Milk") of [domestic sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep"). It is commonly used to make cultured [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product "Dairy product"), such as cheese. Some of the most popular sheep cheeses include [feta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feta "Feta") (Greece), [pecorino romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_romano "Pecorino romano") (Italy), [Roquefort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquefort "Roquefort") (France) and [Manchego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchego "Manchego") (Spain).

Sheep breeds
------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Sheep_milk&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Sheep breeds")]

Specialized dairy breeds of sheep yield more milk than other breeds. Common dairy breeds include:

* [East Friesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Friesian_sheep "East Friesian sheep") (Germany)
* [Sarda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarda_sheep "Sarda sheep") (Italy)
* [Lacaune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacaune_(sheep) "Lacaune (sheep)") (France)
* [British Milk Sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Milk_(sheep) "British Milk (sheep)") (UK)
* [Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chios_sheep "Chios sheep") (Greece)
* [Awassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awassi_sheep "Awassi sheep") (Syria)
* [Assaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assaf_sheep "Assaf sheep") (Israel)
* [Zwartbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwartbles_sheep "Zwartbles sheep") (Friesland, Netherlands)

In the U.S., the most common dairy breeds are the East Friesian and the Lacaune.[[1]](#cite_note-:0-1) Meat or wool breeds do not produce as much milk as dairy breeds, but may produce enough for small amounts of cheese and other products.

Milk production period
----------------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Sheep_milk&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Milk production period")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/World_Emissions_Intensity_Of_Agricultural_Commodities_%282021%29.svg/250px-World_Emissions_Intensity_Of_Agricultural_Commodities_%282021%29.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:World_Emissions_Intensity_Of_Agricultural_Commodities_(2021).svg)

Sheep milk has the third highest CO2 emissions intensity of any agricultural commodity.

Female sheep (ewes) do not produce milk constantly. Instead, they produce milk during the 80–100 days after lambing.[[2]](#cite_note-2) Sheep naturally breed in the fall, which means that a majority of lambs are born in the winter or early spring. Milk production decreases and eventually stops when lambs are weaned or the days are shorter.[[1]](#cite_note-:0-1) Milk cannot be produced year-round.[[3]](#cite_note-3) Through the use of [controlled internal drug release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_internal_drug_release "Controlled internal drug release") (CIDR), ewes can be bred out of season. CIDR drugs contain [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone "Progesterone"), which is slowly released into the bloodstream, bringing the animal into [estrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrous_cycle "Estrous cycle").[[4]](#cite_note-4) In this way, ewes can be bred at different times throughout the year, providing farms with a year-round supply of milk.

Meat and wool breeds of sheep lactate for 90–150 days, while dairy breeds can lactate for 120–240 days. Dairy sheep can produce higher yields of milk per ewe per year. Dairy sheep can produce 400–1,100 lb (180–500 kg) of milk per year while other sheep produce 100–200 lb (45–91 kg) of milk per year. Crossbred ewes produce 300–650 lb (140–290 kg) of milk per year.[[1]](#cite_note-:0-1)

Products made from sheep's milk
-------------------------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Sheep_milk&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Products made from sheep's milk")]

[Sheep milk cheeses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_milk_cheese "Sheep milk cheese") include the [Kashkaval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashkaval "Kashkaval") of [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Roquefort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquefort "Roquefort") of France, [Manchego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchego "Manchego") of Spain; [Serra da Estrela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_da_Estrela_cheese "Serra da Estrela cheese") from [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal "Portugal"); [pecorino Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Romano "Pecorino Romano") (the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language "Italian language") word for sheep is *pecora*) and [pecorino Sardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecorino_Sardo "Pecorino Sardo") of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy"); [Pag cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pag_cheese "Pag cheese") of Croatia; [Ġbejna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%A0bejna "Ġbejna") of Malta; and [Gomolya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomolya?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gomolya (page does not exist)") of Hungary; and [Bryndza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza "Bryndza") (*Slovenská bryndza* from [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia "Slovakia"), [brânza de burduf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%A2nza_de_burduf "Brânza de burduf") from [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania "Romania") and *[Bryndza Podhalańska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryndza_Podhala%C5%84ska "Bryndza Podhalańska")* from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland "Poland")).

A byproduct of making bryndza is the drink [Žinčica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDin%C4%8Dica "Žinčica") which is drunk in Poland and Slovakia.

[Whey cheeses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whey_cheese "Whey cheese") are also made from sheep's milk: various [ricottas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricotta "Ricotta") of Italy (but the best-known are made from buffalo milk); [anthotyros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthotyros "Anthotyros"), [mizithra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizithra "Mizithra"), [manouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manouri "Manouri"), and [xynomizithra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xynomizithra "Xynomizithra") of Greece (often with goat milk mixed in); various [requeijão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requeij%C3%A3o_Serra_da_Estrela "Requeijão Serra da Estrela") in Portugal.

In Greece, [yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt "Yogurt") is often made from sheep's milk.

-----------------------

Sheep's milk is exceptionally high in fat and conjugated linoleic acid (CLA) and has a high level of solids,[[6]](#cite_note-6) as compared to other kinds of milk, making it very suitable for cheese-making. In particular, sheep's milk produces much more cheese than the same amount of cow's milk.[[7]](#cite_note-7)
