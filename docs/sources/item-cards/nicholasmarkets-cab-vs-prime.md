# Certified Angus Beef vs. USDA Prime: What's the Difference?

Standing at the meat counter, “Prime,” “Choice,” and “Certified Angus Beef” can all sound like they mean the same thing: good beef. They don’t. One is a government grade. The other is a brand with its own rulebook — and it’s the standard our butcher shop is built around.

### Key Takeaways:

- How the USDA grades beef, and what “Prime” actually measures
- What it takes for beef to earn the Certified Angus Beef® (CAB) name
- Why CAB can outperform a USDA grade alone
- How to shop for CAB cuts at your local Nicholas Markets butcher shop

## What Is USDA Beef Grading?

USDA grading is a quality score, not a certification. A federal grader looks at marbling (the flecks of fat within the muscle) and the maturity of the animal, then sorts the carcass into a tier — Prime, Choice, Select, and so on. Prime sits at the top of that scale because it typically shows the most abundant marbling, which usually means more tenderness and flavor.

That’s it, though. USDA grading doesn’t look at breed, doesn’t set a maximum carcass size, and doesn’t screen for visual appeal or marbling texture. It’s one measurement, applied broadly across every type of cattle in the country.

### What Makes Certified Angus Beef Different?

Certified Angus Beef isn’t a grade, it’s a brand with ten specific quality checkpoints that beef has to clear before it can carry the name. A carcass is evaluated by an independent USDA grader for these standards at the same time it’s evaluated for a USDA grade, but passing USDA grading alone doesn’t earn the CAB name.

Some of the specifications that matter most:

- Modest or higher marbling: the flecks of fat that drive flavor and juiciness
- Medium to fine marbling texture: coarse marbling is rejected, even if the amount is technically high enough
- A-maturity cattle, harvested young for better color, texture, and tenderness
- A defined ribeye area, so cuts are consistent from one package to the next
- A capped carcass weight and fat thickness, keeping portions and trim in a usable range
- Visual appeal standards, including freedom from surface defects

Put together, these checkpoints exist for one reason: consistency. A USDA Prime grade tells you an animal had strong marbling. Ten CAB specifications tell you the marbling, the texture, the size, and the appearance all meet a bar built specifically for flavor and tenderness, cut after cut.

## USDA Grade vs. CAB Standard: A Side-by-Side Look

| | USDA Grading Alone | Certified Angus Beef |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it measures | Marbling amount + maturity | 10 specifications: marbling amount, marbling texture, maturity, ribeye size, carcass weight, fat thickness, and appearance |
| Consistency between cuts | Varies within a grade | Built into the standard |
| Applies to | Any breed of cattle | Angus-influenced cattle meeting the brand’s criteria |
| Extra screening | None beyond the grade | Independent evaluation against all 10 specs |

This is why a single USDA grade and a full CAB certification aren’t really the same kind of claim — one is a snapshot measurement, the other is a full quality profile.

## Certified Angus Beef (FAQ)

Is Certified Angus Beef better than USDA Prime?

They’re not directly comparable, since one is a grade and one is a brand standard. CAB requires cattle to clear marbling, texture, maturity, size, and appearance checkpoints — a more complete profile than a USDA grade alone provides.

Does Certified Angus Beef come in different tiers?

Yes. The CAB family includes multiple tiers built on top of the base 10 specifications, so shoppers looking for an even higher marbling threshold have options within the brand.
