**Apple cider vinegar**, or **cider vinegar**, is a [vinegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinegar "Vinegar") made from [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider "Cider"),[[3]](#cite_note-ns-3) and used in salad dressings, [marinades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinade "Marinade"), [vinaigrettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinaigrette "Vinaigrette"), [food preservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_preservative "Food preservative"), and [chutneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chutney "Chutney").[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4) First, apples are crushed[[5]](#cite_note-5) and pressed for juice.[[6]](#cite_note-6) The apple juice is then [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation "Ethanol fermentation") by [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast") which converts the [sugars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar "Sugar") in the juice to [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol "Ethanol"). In a second fermentation step, the ethanol is converted into [acetic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetic_acid "Acetic acid") by [acetic acid-forming bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetic_acid_bacteria "Acetic acid bacteria") (*[Acetobacter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetobacter "Acetobacter")* species), yielding cider vinegar.[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4) The acetic acid, together with the [malic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malic_acid "Malic acid") naturally present in apple juice, contribute to the sour taste of this vinegar.[[3]](#cite_note-ns-3)

There is no high-quality clinical evidence that regular consumption of apple cider vinegar helps to maintain or lose [body weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_weight "Body weight"),[[7]](#cite_note-mayo-7) or is effective to manage [blood glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_glucose "Blood glucose") and [lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperlipidemia "Hyperlipidemia") levels.[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Apple_cider_vinegar&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Vinegar has historically been made with whatever sort of foods are available, and vinegars using fruits commonly available in the North Atlantic became popular in the early nineteenth century.[[8]](#cite_note-8) One 1810 cookbook includes recipes for apple cider vinegar, [gooseberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gooseberry "Gooseberry") vinegar, and [raisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin "Raisin") vinegar.[[9]](#cite_note-9)

Production
----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Apple_cider_vinegar&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Production")]

Apples are washed, crushed, and pressed to extract the juice.[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)[[10]](#cite_note-downing-10)[Autochthonous or inoculated yeasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider#Yeast "Cider"), mainly [Saccharomyces cerevisiae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saccharomyces_cerevisiae "Saccharomyces cerevisiae"), initiate [alcoholic fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation "Ethanol fermentation"), converting the sugars in the juice into ethanol and producing [apple cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_cider "Apple cider"). The apple cider is then [inoculated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoculation "Inoculation") with either a pure [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiological_culture "Microbiological culture") of acetic acid bacteria or a proportion of ['mother vinegar'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_of_vinegar "Mother of vinegar"), resulting in a secondary acetic fermentation which then converts the ethanol in the cider to [acetic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetic_acid "Acetic acid"), yielding apple cider vinegar.[[3]](#cite_note-ns-3)[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)[[10]](#cite_note-downing-10) The "mother" is a cellulose-rich biofilm of acetic bacteria that may remain in unfiltered vinegar.[[11]](#cite_note-11)

Nutrition
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Apple_cider_vinegar&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Nutrition")]

Apple cider vinegar is 94% water and 5% acetic acid with 1% [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate") and no [fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat "Fat") or [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_(nutrient) "Protein (nutrient)"). In a 100 gram reference amount, it provides 90 kilojoules (22 kcal) of energy, with negligible content of [micronutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient "Micronutrient").[[12]](#cite_note-fdc-usda-12)

Health effects
--------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Apple_cider_vinegar&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Health effects")]

Evidence for apple cider vinegar having any health effect is poor, such as for [weight loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_loss "Weight loss"), [glycemic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_control "Glycemic control") or [skin infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_infection "Skin infection").[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)[[3]](#cite_note-ns-3)[[13]](#cite_note-sr2020-13) Its use is not [recommended for any therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indication_(medicine) "Indication (medicine)") in [medical guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_guidelines "Medical guidelines") of major public health organizations or [regulatory agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_agency "Regulatory agency").[[14]](#cite_note-NCPC-14)

Safety concerns
---------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Apple_cider_vinegar&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Safety concerns")]

See also: [Acetic acid (medical use)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetic_acid_(medical_use) "Acetic acid (medical use)")

Moderate consumption of apple cider vinegar is safe, particularly if it is diluted, and the chance of [side effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_effect "Side effect") when it is consumed as directed and in the recommended amounts appears to be low.[[13]](#cite_note-sr2020-13) Reported [adverse effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_effect "Adverse effect") include [esophageal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophagus "Esophagus") damage due to incomplete swallowing of apple cider vinegar tablets, [tooth enamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tooth_enamel "Tooth enamel") damage due to swallowing excessive quantities of apple cider vinegar, and increased frequency of [burping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burp "Burp"), [flatulence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatulence "Flatulence"), and [bowel movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowel_movement "Bowel movement"). Consumption of vinegar can increase [dentin hypersensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentin_hypersensitivity "Dentin hypersensitivity").[[13]](#cite_note-sr2020-13) Irritation and redness are common when the eyes come into contact with vinegar, and [corneal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornea "Cornea") injury can occur.[[14]](#cite_note-NCPC-14) Using vinegar as a [topical medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topical_medication "Topical medication"), ear cleaning solution, or [eye wash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_wash "Eye wash") is hazardous.[[14]](#cite_note-NCPC-14) Although small amounts of apple cider vinegar may be used as a [food flavoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_flavoring "Food flavoring"),[[14]](#cite_note-NCPC-14) it may be unsafe for use by [pregnant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy "Pregnancy") and [breastfeeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breastfeeding "Breastfeeding") women and by children.[[3]](#cite_note-ns-3) Different commercial brands of apple cider vinegar were found to have inconsistent acid levels, with some contaminated by [molds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mold_(fungus) "Mold (fungus)") and [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast").[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)

If used as a [homemade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft "Handicraft") [cleaning agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleaning_agent "Cleaning agent"), apple cider vinegar, like any kind of vinegar, should not be mixed with [chlorine bleach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine_bleach "Chlorine bleach"), the combination of which may release [chlorine gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine_gas "Chlorine gas") and irritate airways, eyes, nose and throat.[[14]](#cite_note-NCPC-14)

People with [allergies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy "Allergy") to apples may experience [allergic reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergic_reaction "Allergic reaction") to apple cider vinegar.[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4)[Topical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topical_medication "Topical medication") use of apple cider vinegar to treat [skin diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_disease "Skin disease") may cause burns.[[4]](#cite_note-drugs-4) The use of apple cider vinegar may cause [untoward interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_interaction "Drug interaction") with [prescription drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug "Prescription drug"), such as [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") or [diuretics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diuretic "Diuretic").[[7]](#cite_note-mayo-7)
