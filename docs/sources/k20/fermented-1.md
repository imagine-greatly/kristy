Converting carbohydrates to alcohol or acids using anaerobic microorganisms

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Tomb_of_Nakht_%2813%29.jpg/500px-Tomb_of_Nakht_%2813%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Nakht_(13).jpg)

[Grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape "Grape") being [trodden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape_treading "Grape treading") to extract the juice and made into wine in storage jars. Tomb of [Nakht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakht "Nakht"), [18th dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_dynasty "18th dynasty"), [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Egypt "Thebes, Egypt"), [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt").

[![Sourdough starter from overhead](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Sourdough_starter.jpg/250px-Sourdough_starter.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sourdough_starter.jpg)

Sourdough starter.

In [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing "Food processing"), **fermentation** is the conversion of [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate "Carbohydrate") to [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_(chemistry) "Alcohol (chemistry)") or [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compound "Organic compound") acids using [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism "Microorganism")—[yeasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast") or [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria")—without an oxidizing agent being used in the reaction. [Fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation "Fermentation") usually implies that the action of microorganisms is desired.[[1]](#cite_note-1) The science of fermentation is known as [zymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zymology "Zymology") or zymurgy.

The term "fermentation" sometimes refers specifically to the chemical conversion of [sugars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar "Sugar") into [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol "Ethanol"), producing alcoholic drinks such as [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine "Wine"), [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer"), and [cider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cider "Cider"). However, similar processes take place in the [leavening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavening "Leavening") of [bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread "Bread") (CO2 produced by yeast activity), and in the preservation of sour foods with the production of [lactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid "Lactic acid"), such as in [sauerkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkraut "Sauerkraut") and [yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt "Yogurt").

Other widely consumed fermented foods include [vinegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinegar "Vinegar"), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive "Olive"), and [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese "Cheese"). More localized foods prepared by fermentation may also be based on beans, grain, vegetables, fruit, honey, dairy products, and fish.

### Brewing and winemaking

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Fermentation_in_food_processing&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Brewing and winemaking")]

Main articles: [Brewing § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing#History "Brewing"), and [History of wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_wine "History of wine")

See also: [History of beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_beer "History of beer")

Natural fermentation predates human history. Since ancient times humans have exploited fermentation, most likely having unintentionally discovered the process. To store excess foods, humans placed the items in a container which were probably later forgotten, and over time yeast and bacteria started to grow.[[2]](#cite_note-2) The earliest archaeological evidence of fermentation is the 13,000-year-old residue of beer, with the consistency of gruel, found in a cave near [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa "Haifa"), Israel.[[3]](#cite_note-3) Another early alcoholic drink, made from fruit, rice, and honey, dates from 7000 to 6600 BC in the [Neolithic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_China "Neolithic China") village of [Jiahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiahu "Jiahu").[[4]](#cite_note-mcgovern-4) Winemaking dates from circa 6000 BC in [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_(country) "Georgia (country)") in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus") area.[[5]](#cite_note-Ref1-5) Seven-thousand-year-old jars containing the remains of wine, now on display at the University of Pennsylvania, were excavated in the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains "Zagros Mountains") in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran").[[6]](#cite_note-Ref2-6) There is strong evidence that people were fermenting alcoholic drinks in [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon "Babylon") (ca. 3000 BC),[[7]](#cite_note-FAO-7)[ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt "Ancient Egypt") (ca. 3150 BC),[[8]](#cite_note-Cavalieri-8) pre-Hispanic Mexico (ca. 2000 BC),[[7]](#cite_note-FAO-7) and [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan "Sudan") (ca. 1500 BC).[[9]](#cite_note-Dirar-9)

### Discovery of the role of yeast

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Fermentation_in_food_processing&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Discovery of the role of yeast")]

The French chemist [Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur "Louis Pasteur") founded [zymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zymology "Zymology") in 1856 when he connected yeast to fermentation.[[10]](#cite_note-10)
In studying the conversion of sugar to [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_(chemistry) "Alcohol (chemistry)") by [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast"), Pasteur concluded that the fermentation was catalyzed by a vital force, called "[ferments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalism "Vitalism")", within the yeast cells. The "ferments" were thought to function only within living organisms. Pasteur wrote that "Alcoholic fermentation is an act correlated with the life and organization of the yeast cells, not with the death or putrefaction of the cells."[[11]](#cite_note-11)

### "Cell-free fermentation"

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Fermentation_in_food_processing&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: \"Cell-free fermentation\"")]

Nevertheless it was known that living yeast [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") were not a requirement for fermentation. In 1897 the German chemist and zymologist [Eduard Buchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Buchner "Eduard Buchner") of [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin") found that sugar was fermented even when there were no living yeast cells in the mixture,[[12]](#cite_note-12) by an enzyme complex secreted by yeast that he termed *[zymase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zymase "Zymase")*.[[13]](#cite_note-13) In 1907 he received the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") for his research and discovery of "cell-free fermentation".

One year earlier in 1906 [ethanol fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fermentation "Ethanol fermentation") studies led to the early discovery of oxidized [nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotinamide_adenine_dinucleotide "Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide") (NAD+).[[14]](#cite_note-14)[*[verification needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]

Uses
----

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Fermentation_in_food_processing&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Uses")]

Further information: [Microbes in human culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbes_in_human_culture "Microbes in human culture") and [List of microorganisms used in food and beverage preparation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_microorganisms_used_in_food_and_beverage_preparation "List of microorganisms used in food and beverage preparation")

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Beer_and_bread.jpg/250px-Beer_and_bread.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Beer_and_bread.jpg)

Beer and bread, two major uses of fermentation in food

The byproducts of food fermentation are alcohol, preservative [organic acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_acid "Organic acid"), and [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide"), all three of which have found human uses. The production of alcohol results in fruit [juices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juice "Juice") becoming [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine "Wine"), grains becoming [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer"), and foods rich in starch, such as [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato "Potato"), that are then distilled into spirits like [gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin "Gin") and [vodka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodka "Vodka"). The production of carbon dioxide is used to [leaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavening_agent "Leavening agent") bread. The production of organic acids is exploited to preserve and flavor vegetables and dairy products.[[15]](#cite_note-HuiMeunier-Goddik2004-15)

Food fermentation serves five main purposes: to enrich the diet through development of a diversity of flavors, aromas, and textures in food substrates; To preserve substantial amounts of food through [lactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid "Lactic acid"), alcohol, [acetic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetic_acid "Acetic acid"), and [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline "Alkaline")[[16]](#cite_note-16) fermentations; to enrich food substrates with protein, [essential amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_amino_acid "Essential amino acid"), and vitamins; to eliminate [antinutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antinutrient "Antinutrient"); and to reduce cooking time and the associated use of fuel.[[17]](#cite_note-Steinkraus-17)

Beverages produced through fermentation have likely been universally associated with ceremonies and festivals. There is some understanding of how they have been consumed in such contexts, derived from the construction of drinkware and the residue contained therein.[[18]](#cite_note-18)

Risks
-----

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Fermentation_in_food_processing&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Risks")]

See also: [Pickling § Possible health hazards of pickled vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickling#Possible_health_hazards_of_pickled_vegetables "Pickling")

[Sterilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterilization_(microbiology) "Sterilization (microbiology)") is an important factor to consider during the fermentation of foods. Failing to completely remove any [microbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbes "Microbes") from equipment and storing vessels may result in the multiplication of harmful organisms within the ferment, potentially increasing the risks of food borne illnesses such as botulism. However, botulism in vegetable ferments is only possible when not properly canned. The production of off smells and discoloration may be indications that harmful bacteria may have been introduced to the food.

[Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") has witnessed a steady increase of cases of [botulism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botulism "Botulism") since 1985.[[19]](#cite_note-Alaska-19) It has more cases of botulism than any other state in the United States of America. This is caused by the traditional [Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Natives "Alaska Natives") practice of allowing animal products such as whole fish, fish heads, [walrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walrus "Walrus"), [sea lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_lion "Sea lion"), and [whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale "Whale") flippers, [beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver "Beaver") tails, seal oil, and birds, to ferment for an extended period of time before being consumed. The risk is exacerbated when a [plastic container](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_container "Plastic container") is used for this purpose instead of the old-fashioned, traditional method, a grass-lined hole, as the *[Clostridium botulinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clostridium_botulinum "Clostridium botulinum")* bacteria thrive in the anaerobic conditions created by the air-tight enclosure in plastic.[[19]](#cite_note-Alaska-19)

Research has found that fermented food contains a carcinogenic by-product, [ethyl carbamate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethyl_carbamate "Ethyl carbamate") (urethane).[[20]](#cite_note-20) "A 2009 review of the existing studies conducted across Asia concluded that regularly eating pickled vegetables roughly doubles a person's risk for [esophageal squamous cell carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer "Esophageal cancer")."[[21]](#cite_note-21)
