**Natto** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 納豆, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization "Hepburn romanization"): *nattō*) is a traditional [Japanese food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cuisine "Japanese cuisine") made from whole [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean "Soybean") that have been [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_in_food_processing "Fermentation in food processing") with [*Bacillus subtilis* var. *natto*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_subtilis "Bacillus subtilis").[[1]](#cite_note-hosking-jfood-1) It is often served as a breakfast food with rice.[[2]](#cite_note-dapress18-2) It is served with [*karashi* mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karashi "Karashi"), [soy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce "Soy sauce") or [*tare* sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tare_sauce "Tare sauce"), and sometimes [spring onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allium_fistulosum "Allium fistulosum"). Within Japan, natto is most popular in the eastern regions, including [Kantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region "Kantō region"), [Tōhoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku "Tōhoku"), and [Hokkaido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido "Hokkaido").[[3]](#cite_note-soyinfocenter2012-3)[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5)

Natto is often considered an [acquired taste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquired_taste "Acquired taste") because of its powerful smell, strong flavor, and sticky, slimy texture.[[6]](#cite_note-katz-6)[[7]](#cite_note-economist20-7)[[8]](#cite_note-buerk21-8)[[9]](#cite_note-jcentre22-9)[[10]](#cite_note-massahiro23-10) A 2009 survey revealed that 70% of the 3,827 respondents found the taste pleasant, and others who may not find the taste pleasant still eat the food out of habit.[[11]](#cite_note-navi16-11)

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Natto with sticky, slimy strands caused by fermentation with bacteria

Sources differ about the earliest origin of natto. One theory is that natto was codeveloped in multiple locations in the distant past, since it is simple to make with ingredients and tools commonly available in Japan since ancient times.[[12]](#cite_note-D_Murakhver_2012_p._1412-12)

### Legendary origins

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Legendary origins")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Natto_opening_stirring.ogv/250px--Natto_opening_stirring.ogv.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Natto_opening_stirring.ogv?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original)

Opening and stirring a container of natto

One story about the origin of natto attributes it to the samurai [Minamoto no Yoshiie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minamoto_no_Yoshiie "Minamoto no Yoshiie") (1039–1106), who was on a campaign in northeastern Japan between 1086 and 1088 AD. One day, his troops were attacked while boiling soybeans for their horses. They hurriedly packed up the beans, and did not open the straw bags until a few days later, by which time the beans had fermented. The soldiers ate it anyway, and liked the taste, so they offered some to Yoshiie, who also liked the taste.[[13]](#cite_note-:0-13)[[14]](#cite_note-ShurtleffAoyagi20122-14)

Another story involves [Prince Shotoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sh%C5%8Dtoku "Prince Shōtoku") (574–622), who is said to have wrapped the leftovers of boiled soybeans in straw bags for his horse. As people happened to eat these fermented beans and found them delicious, this type of fermented stringy beans soon gained popularity in Japan because of its unique taste and strong flavor.[[15]](#cite_note-:1-15)

### Chinese *douchi*

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Chinese douchi")]

Before natto, there was a similar dish of fermented black soybeans food in China called *shì*, *chǐ* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 豉), or *[douchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douchi "Douchi")* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"): 豆豉; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin "Pinyin"): *dòuchǐ*). These are salted, fermented and aged whole soybean seasonings or condiments invented in China and spread throughout East Asia. They are usually made from fermented soybeans and with an ample amount of salt; however, the ingredients and production methods differ in Japan. Chinese use both black and yellow soybeans to produce *douchi*. The amount of salt used also differentiates *douchi* and natto in taste and appearance.[[16]](#cite_note-:2-16)[[17]](#cite_note-:3-17)

The cultivation methods of soybeans and rice were imported from China to Japan during the [Yayoi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period "Yayoi period"), and later on, the circulation of salt began to flourish in Japan. This provided an opportunity for the production of *douchi* to become popular in Japan. Because salt was expensive at the time, it has been suggested that natto was invented by accident during the production of *douchi.*[[13]](#cite_note-:0-13)[[15]](#cite_note-:1-15)

A wooden slip was excavated in [Heijō-kyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heij%C5%8D-ky%C5%8D "Heijō-kyō"), which had the Chinese character 豉 (chǐ; soybean) written on it.[[15]](#cite_note-:1-15)[[16]](#cite_note-:2-16) The excavation of the slip is considered an evidence to support the hypothesis that the invention of natto was based on the Chinese *douchi* imported to Japan.

The Chinese character 豉 entered Japan in the 8th century. It was pronounced "kuki" until the 11th century, when natto became a new name for fermented soybeans.[[17]](#cite_note-:3-17)

### Commercialization in the Taisho period

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Commercialization in the Taisho period")]

A change in the production of natto occurred in the [Taishō period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D_period "Taishō period") (1912–1926), when researchers discovered a way to produce a natto [starter culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starter_culture "Starter culture") containing *[Bacillus subtilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_subtilis "Bacillus subtilis")* without the need for straw, thereby simplifying the commercial production of natto and enabling more consistent results.[[18]](#cite_note-18)

Appearance and consumption
--------------------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Appearance and consumption")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/JP_%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC_Japan_%E5%9C%9F%E7%89%B9%E7%94%A2_%E7%B4%8D%E8%B1%86_Natto_January_2025_R12S_02.jpg/250px-JP_%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC_Japan_%E5%9C%9F%E7%89%B9%E7%94%A2_%E7%B4%8D%E8%B1%86_Natto_January_2025_R12S_02.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:JP_%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC_Japan_%E5%9C%9F%E7%89%B9%E7%94%A2_%E7%B4%8D%E8%B1%86_Natto_January_2025_R12S_02.jpg)

Natto with strands

Natto has a distinctive odor, somewhat akin to a pungent aged cheese. Stirring natto produces many sticky strings.[[1]](#cite_note-hosking-jfood-1) The dish is eaten cold with rice, mixed with the included soy sauce or [karashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karashi "Karashi") mustard if eaten from a commercially packaged natto. Other ingredients such as [long onion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_onion "Long onion") or [kimchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimchi "Kimchi") are often added.

Natto is frequently eaten as *nattō* *gohan* (natto on rice). Natto is occasionally used in other foods, such as natto sushi (*nattōmaki*), natto toast, in [miso soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miso_soup "Miso soup"), [tamagoyaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagoyaki "Tamagoyaki"), salad, as an ingredient in [okonomiyaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okonomiyaki "Okonomiyaki"), [chahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahan_(dish) "Chahan (dish)"), or even with [spaghetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti "Spaghetti"). Sometimes soybeans are crushed before fermenting.

Many find the taste unpleasant and smelly while others relish it as a delicacy. Natto is more popular in some areas of Japan than in others. Natto is known to be popular in the eastern [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region "Kantō region"), but less popular in [Kansai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai "Kansai").[[11]](#cite_note-navi16-11)

For those who dislike the smell and texture of natto, "dried natto" and "fried natto" were developed around 1990. The smell and stickiness are reduced, making it easier to eat for those who do not like conventional natto. Another type of fermented soybeans called *mamenoka* ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 豆乃香) has also been developed by developing versions of natto bacillus varieties that result in less sticky natto.[[22]](#cite_note-22)

Production
----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Production")]

Natto is made from soybeans, typically natto soybeans. Smaller beans are preferred, as the fermentation process will be able to reach the center of the bean more easily. The beans are washed and soaked in water for 12 to 20 hours to hydrate them, and increase their size.[[23]](#cite_note-23) Next, the soybeans are [steamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steaming "Steaming") for six hours, although a [pressure cooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_cooking "Pressure cooking") may be used to reduce the time. The cooked beans are mixed with the bacterium *[Bacillus subtilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacillus_subtilis "Bacillus subtilis")*, known as *nattō-kin* in Japanese. From this point on, care must be taken to keep the ingredients away from impurities and other [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria "Bacteria"). The mixture is fermented at 40 °C (104 °F) for up to 24 hours. Afterward, the natto is cooled, then aged in a refrigerator for up to one week to allow the development of stringiness.

In natto-making facilities, these processing steps have to be done while avoiding incidents in which soybeans are touched by workers. Even though workers use *B. subtilis natto* as the starting culture, which can suppress some undesired bacterial growth, workers pay extra-close attention not to introduce [skin flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_flora "Skin flora") onto soybeans.[[24]](#cite_note-24)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/%E3%81%97%E3%81%9D%E3%81%AE%E3%82%8A_%284557647319%29.jpg/250px-%E3%81%97%E3%81%9D%E3%81%AE%E3%82%8A_%284557647319%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:%E3%81%97%E3%81%9D%E3%81%AE%E3%82%8A_(4557647319).jpg)

Natto is usually sold in small containers.

[Mass-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass-produced "Mass-produced") natto is sold in small [polystyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polystyrene "Polystyrene") containers. A typical package contains two, three, or occasionally four containers, each 40 to 50 g (1.4 to 1.8 oz). One container typically complements a small bowl of rice.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Natto odor comes from [diacetyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diacetyl "Diacetyl") and [pyrazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrazine "Pyrazine"), but if it is allowed to ferment too long, then [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia "Ammonia") is released.[[25]](#cite_note-25) The fermenting of natto develops [glutamic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamic_acid "Glutamic acid"), which is why it has an [umami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umami "Umami") flavour.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Related products
----------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Natto&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Related products")]

[![A triangle with kinema near Nepal, nattō in Japan, and thua nao in Thailand. Also, douchi in China, cheonggukjang in Korea, pe pok in Myanmar, and sieng in Laos.](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/KNT_Triangle_English.svg/250px-KNT_Triangle_English.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:KNT_Triangle_English.svg)

The *kinema*–*nattō*–*thua nao* (KNT) triangle connects *Bacillus*-fermented soybean foods across [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia "Asia").[[26]](#cite_note-26)

Many countries around Asia also produce similar traditional soybean foods fermented with *Bacillus* bacteria, such as *[shuidouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douchi "Douchi")* (水豆豉) of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine "Chinese cuisine"), *[cheonggukjang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheonggukjang "Cheonggukjang")* (청국장) of [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine "Korean cuisine"), *[thua nao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thua_nao "Thua nao")* (ถั่วเน่า) of [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_cuisine "Thai cuisine"), *[kinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinema "Kinema")* of [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_cuisine "Nepalese cuisine") and the [Himalayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya "Himalaya") regions of [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal") and [Sikkim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim"), *[tungrymbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungrymbai "Tungrymbai")* of [Meghalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghalaya "Meghalaya"), *hawaijaar* of [Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur "Manipur"), *bekang um* of [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram "Mizoram"), *[akhuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhuni "Akhuni")* of [Nagaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland "Nagaland"), and *piak* of [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh "Arunachal Pradesh"), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine "Indian cuisine").[[3]](#cite_note-soyinfocenter2012-3)[[27]](#cite_note-arorahand1-27)
