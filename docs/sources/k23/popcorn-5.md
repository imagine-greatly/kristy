Popcorn hails from America’s heartland

By [Brandon Lambert](https://share.america.gov/author/lambertb/)

6 MINUTE READ

August 3, 2026

Evidence of popped corn discovered in Peru shows people’s love affair with the light and fluffy snack dates back 6,700 years. But if you’ve enjoyed popcorn in more recent times, it likely came from the United States.

While estimates vary, U.S. popcorn production accounts for upward of [80% of popcorn seed](https://worldmetrics.org/popcorn-industry-statistics/ "Popcorn Industry Statistics | Worldmetrics") sold worldwide. Much of it comes from the Midwest, the heartland region where the world’s first popcorn popper debuted at the World’s Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893.

For decades, U.S. seed scientists have adapted popcorn to thrive in the agriculturally rich region, helping drive the snack’s evolution from a movie theater and TV-night favorite to a healthy snack enjoyed around the world.

![Man inspecting crop of corn in field in front of silos (© Brent Smith/Reuters)](https://share.america.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/155/2026/08/popcorn_snack_indiana_farmer--1024x682.jpg "Man inspecting crop of corn in field in front of silos (© Brent Smith/Reuters)")

Farmer Matt Johnson inspects crops on his family’s farm in Redkey, Indiana. (© Brent Smith/Reuters)

Dan Quinn, corn program director at Purdue University in West Lafayette, Indiana, says years of localized breeding and fertilization techniques have increased farmers’ popcorn yields and developed traits for optimal popping.

Different hybrids have been developed for microwave popping versus large commercial kettles, Quinn says. Research considers everything from moisture levels to hull thickness (the part of the seed that explodes during cooking) and focuses on both major popcorn shapes: butterfly and mushroom.

Indiana now produces 40% of America’s popcorn, more than any other U.S. state. Indiana and Purdue’s rich popcorn legacy includes Orville Redenbacher, the bow-tie-clad pitchman who refined his kernels at Purdue before launching his business from the state. He later popularized his brand with TV commercials in the 1970s and 1980s.

A few states over, in Nebraska, researchers are enhancing popcorn’s inherent nutritional qualities. The University of Nebraska and private sector partners recently developed a popcorn variety rich in an amino acid essential for healthy diets, [says David Holding](https://ianrnews.unl.edu/5050-shot-breeds-decade-popcorn-success "50/50 shot breeds a decade of popcorn success | IANR News"), a professor and researcher at the school. “This is a product that lends itself to organic production and can be marketed as a novel popcorn variety,” he says.

Aaron Whalen, sales manager at Ag Alumni Seed, which partners with Purdue University and growers in 20 countries, says the popcorn market is trending toward healthier products, as well as those that allow for adding culturally specific flavors.

![Night photo of woman buying popcorn from street vendor surrounded by steam (© B Mathur/Reuters)](https://share.america.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/155/2026/08/popcorn_snack_india_street_vendor--1024x739.jpg "Night photo of woman buying popcorn from street vendor surrounded by steam (© B Mathur/Reuters)")

A street vendor sells popcorn in New Delhi. (© B Mathur/Reuters)

Vendors in India often sell freshly popped corn and prefer butterfly seeds, Whalen says. Demand in China is for mushroom-shaped varieties, which are often sold prepopped and coated with flavorings. Either way, he says, people “recognize America, especially Indiana, has executed the research to create a higher quality seed.”

Wendy Boersema Rappel, spokesperson for the Popcorn Board, an industry group, calls popcorn “America’s most universal snack with health benefits.” Each year, Americans eat 14 billion quarts (16 billion liters) of popcorn, which includes fiber to aid digestion and antioxidants that may help protect against cancer and heart disease, according to [the Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/popcorn-benefits "7 Health Benefits of Popcorn | Cleveland Clinic").

Annual harvest festivals honor the Midwest’s rich popcorn heritage. On August 14–16, North Loup, Nebraska, will hold its 125th annual Popcorn Days with softball, street dancing and food vendors. The Indiana town where Redenbacher built his business will hold the annual Valparaiso Popcorn Festival on September 12, complete with a popcorn parade, vendors and a kids’ play zone.

As America celebrates its [250th anniversary](https://share.america.gov/tag/freedom-250/ "Freedom 250 | ShareAmerica") in 2026, the Popcorn Board’s Boersema Rappel says popcorn’s history in the U.S. follows the trajectory of the nation. “Like America, popcorn may start small but bursts big onto the scene while being perfected along the way with innovation.”
