**Granola** is a food consisting of a mix of [rolled oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolled_oats "Rolled oats"), [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_(fruit) "Nut (fruit)"), [seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_seeds "Edible seeds"), [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey "Honey") (or other [sweeteners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_substitute "Sugar substitute") such as [brown sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_sugar "Brown sugar")), and sometimes [puffed rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puffed_rice "Puffed rice") that is usually [baked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking "Baking") with [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil "Cooking oil") until crisp, toasted and golden brown, sometimes forming clumps. The mixture is stirred while baking to avoid burning and to maintain a loose [breakfast cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakfast_cereal "Breakfast cereal") consistency. [Dried fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_fruit "Dried fruit"), such as [raisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin "Raisin") and [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm "Date palm"), and [confections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery "Confectionery") such as [chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate "Chocolate") are often added.

Granola is often eaten in combination with [yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt "Yogurt"), honey, fresh fruit (such as [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana "Banana"), [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry "Strawberry") or [blueberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueberries "Blueberries")), [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk "Milk"), or even with other forms of cereal. It also serves as a topping for various [pastries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastry "Pastry"), [desserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessert "Dessert") or [ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream "Ice cream"). Granola is similar to [muesli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muesli "Muesli"), except the latter is traditionally neither sweetened nor baked.

Granola is sometimes taken when [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting "Hunting"), [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing "Fishing"), [hiking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiking "Hiking"), [camping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camping "Camping"), or [backpacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpacking_(wilderness) "Backpacking (wilderness)") because it is lightweight, high in [calories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie "Calorie"), ready-to-eat, and easy to store (properties that make it similar to [trail mix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_mix "Trail mix")). Manufacturers also add additional honey, [corn syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_syrup "Corn syrup"), or [maple syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_syrup "Maple syrup") to it and compress it into [granola bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granola_bar "Granola bar"), which are easy to carry for [packed lunches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packed_lunch "Packed lunch"), hiking, or other outdoor activities.

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Granola&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Granola_advertisement%2C_1893.png/250px-Granola_advertisement%2C_1893.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Granola_advertisement,_1893.png)

An 1893 advertisement for Kellogg's Granola

[Granula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granula "Granula") was invented in [Dansville, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dansville,_Livingston_County,_New_York "Dansville, Livingston County, New York"), by Dr. [James Caleb Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Caleb_Jackson "James Caleb Jackson") at the [Jackson Sanitarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Sanatorium "Jackson Sanatorium") in 1863.[[1]](#cite_note-1) The Jackson Sanitarium was a prominent [health spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_club "Health club") that operated into the early 20th century on the hillside overlooking Dansville. It was also known as Our Home on the Hillside; thus the company formed to sell Jackson's cereal was known as the Our Home Granula Company. Granula was composed of [Graham flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_flour "Graham flour") and was similar to an oversized form of [Grape-Nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape-Nuts "Grape-Nuts"). A similar cereal was developed by [John Harvey Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harvey_Kellogg "John Harvey Kellogg"). It was also initially known as Granula, but the name was changed to Granola to avoid legal problems with Jackson.[[2]](#cite_note-2)

The food and name were revived in the 1960s, and fruits and nuts were added to it to make it a [health food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_food "Health food") that was popular with the health and nature-oriented [hippie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie "Hippie") movement. Due to this connection, the descriptors "granola" and "crunchy-granola" have entered colloquial use as a way to label people and things associated with the movement.[[3]](#cite_note-granola_definition-3)

Another major promoter was Layton Gentry, profiled in *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")* as "Johnny Granola-Seed".[[4]](#cite_note-#CITEREF1972|Time_1972-4) In 1964, Gentry sold the rights to a granola recipe using oats, which he claimed to have invented himself, to Sovex Natural Foods for $3,000. The company was founded in 1953 in [Holly, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly,_Michigan "Holly, Michigan") by the Hurlinger family with the main purpose of producing a concentrated paste of brewer's [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeast "Yeast") and [soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce "Soy sauce") known as "Sovex". Earlier in 1964, it had been bought by John Goodbrad and moved to [Collegedale, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegedale,_Tennessee "Collegedale, Tennessee"). In 1967, Gentry bought back the rights for west of the [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains "Rocky Mountains") for $1,500 and then sold the west coast rights to Wayne Schlotthauer of Lassen Foods in [Chico, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico,_California "Chico, California"), for $18,000.[[4]](#cite_note-#CITEREF1972|Time_1972-4) Lassen was founded from a health food bakery run by Schlotthauer's father-in-law.[[5]](#cite_note-#CITEREFKlein1978|Klein_1978-5)

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Granola03242006.JPG/250px-Granola03242006.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Granola03242006.JPG)

A bowl of dry, plain granola

In 1969, during [Woodstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodstock "Woodstock"), [Lisa Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Law "Lisa Law") asked the festival organizers for $3,000 to buy, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), [rolled oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolled_oats "Rolled oats"), [bulgar wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgar_wheat "Bulgar wheat"), [wheat germ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_germ "Wheat germ"), [dried apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dried_apricots "Dried apricots"), [currants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribes "Ribes"), [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almonds "Almonds"), [soy sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soy_sauce "Soy sauce"), and [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey "Honey") to make [muesli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muesli "Muesli"). Volunteers fed circa 130,000 people with [Dixie cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_cup "Dixie cup").[[6]](#cite_note-americanhistory.si.edu/lisalaw/7-6)[[7]](#cite_note-edition.cnn.com-twih.woodstock-7)[[8]](#cite_note-smithsonianmag-woodstock-feed-8)[[9]](#cite_note-nytimes-crosswords-9)[[10]](#cite_note-woodstockpreservation-FoodLine-10)[[11]](#cite_note-newberrymagazine-woodstock-dixie-cup-11)

In 1972, an executive at [Pet Milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Incorporated "Pet Incorporated"), [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri "St. Louis, Missouri"), introduced Heartland Natural Cereal, the first major commercial granola.[[5]](#cite_note-#CITEREFKlein1978|Klein_1978-5) At almost the same time, the [Quaker Oats Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_Oats_Company "Quaker Oats Company") introduced Quaker 100% Natural Granola. Quaker was threatened with legal action by Gentry, and they subsequently changed the name of their product to [Harvest Crunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest_Crunch?action=edit&redlink=1 "Harvest Crunch (page does not exist)"). Within a year, [Kellogg's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg's "Kellogg's") had introduced its "Country Morning" granola cereal and [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills "General Mills") had introduced its "[Nature Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Valley "Nature Valley")".[[12]](#cite_note-12) In 1974, McKee Baking (later [McKee Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKee_Foods "McKee Foods")), makers of [Little Debbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Debbie "Little Debbie") snack cakes, purchased Sovex. In 1998, the company also acquired the Heartland brand and moved its manufacturing to Collegedale. In 2004, Sovex's name was changed to "Blue Planet Foods".[[13]](#cite_note-13)[[14]](#cite_note-14)[[15]](#cite_note-15)

Granola bar
-----------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Granola&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Granola bar")]

**Granola bars** (or **muesli bars**) became popular in the 1960s[[16]](#cite_note-16) as a [snack bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_(food) "Bar (food)"), similar to the traditional [flapjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flapjack_(oat_bar) "Flapjack (oat bar)") familiar in the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles "British Isles") and [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_and_Labrador "Newfoundland and Labrador"). Granola bars consist of granola mixed with [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey "Honey") or other sweetened [syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrup "Syrup"), pressed and baked into a bar shape, resulting in the production of a more convenient snack. The product is most popular in the United States,[[17]](#cite_note-17) Canada, Australia, [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"), the United Kingdom, parts of [southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe "Southern Europe"), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil "Brazil"), [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa"), and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan").

Matzo granola
-------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Granola&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Matzo granola")]

Matzo granola is a breakfast food eaten by some [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish "Jewish") people during the holiday of [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover "Passover"). It consists of broken up [matzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matzah "Matzah") pieces in place of oats. Many variations are possible by adding other ingredients.[[18]](#cite_note-18)[[19]](#cite_note-19)[[20]](#cite_note-20)

Trademark
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Granola&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Trademark")]

The names *[Granula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granula "Granula")* and *Granola* were registered [trademarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trademark "Trademark") in the late 19th century United States for foods consisting of sweetened [whole grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_grain "Whole grain") products crumbled and then baked until crisp.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The name is now a trademark only in Australia and New Zealand[[21]](#cite_note-21).[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] However, the use of the term granola in Australia was clarified in 2012 when [Sanitarium Health foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitarium_Health_and_Wellbeing_Company "Sanitarium Health and Wellbeing Company") alleged trademark infringement by the word's usage on the product labels of Irrewarra sourdough. The [Federal Court of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Court_of_Australia "Federal Court of Australia") concluded that use of the word granola had become commonplace and could not infringe Sanitarium's right to the term when used to describe the product itself, rather than as a trademark.[[22]](#cite_note-22)[*[better source needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*]
