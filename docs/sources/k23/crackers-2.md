A **cracker** is a flat, dry [baked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baking "Baking") [biscuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biscuit "Biscuit") typically made with [flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour "Flour"). Flavorings or [seasonings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasoning "Seasoning"), such as salt, [herbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb "Herb"), [seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed "Seed"), or [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese "Cheese"), may be added to the dough or sprinkled on top before or after baking.[[1]](#cite_note-Manley_2011-1) Crackers are often branded as a nutritious and convenient way to consume a [staple food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food "Staple food") or [cereal grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal_grain "Cereal grain").

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Army_and_Navy_hard_tack.jpg/250px-Army_and_Navy_hard_tack.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Army_and_Navy_hard_tack.jpg)

Reproduction of 19th-century [hardtack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack "Hardtack"), in the Army (square) and Navy (round) styles

Crackers can be eaten on their own, but can also accompany other food items such as cheese or meat slices, fruits, [dips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipping_sauce "Dipping sauce"), or soft spreads such as [jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_preserves "Fruit preserves"), butter, [peanut butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_butter "Peanut butter"), or [mousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mousse "Mousse"). Bland or mild crackers are sometimes used as a palate cleanser in food product testing or flavor testing, between samples. Crackers may also be crumbled and added to soup.[[2]](#cite_note-2) The modern cracker is somewhat similar to [nautical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical "Nautical") ship's biscuits,[[3]](#cite_note-3) military [hardtack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack "Hardtack"), [chacknels](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cracknel "wikt:cracknel"),[[4]](#cite_note-4) and [sacramental bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramental_bread "Sacramental bread"). Other early versions of the cracker can be found in ancient [flatbreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread "Flatbread"), such as [lavash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavash "Lavash"), [pita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pita "Pita"), [matzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matzah "Matzah"), [flatbrød](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbr%C3%B8d "Flatbrød"), and [crispbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crispbread "Crispbread"). Asian analogues include *[papadum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papadum "Papadum")*, *[senbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senbei "Senbei")* and *[num kreab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Num_kreab "Num kreab")*.

The characteristic holes found in many crackers are called "[docking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_docker "Roller docker")" holes. The holes are poked in the dough to stop overly large air pockets from forming in the cracker while baking.

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Cracker_(food)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]

Modern crackers are the end product of what started as a convenience food for soldiers and sailors, recorded as far back as 1190. Known as [Hardtack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack "Hardtack") or ship's biscuit, they were made out of a mixture of grain and/or legume flour and water, shaped into tablets, then baked until brick hard. The low moisture content allowed these tablets to be stored for long periods of time with no loss of nutritional value. Mixed with coffee, soup, or even sea water, the hard biscuits could be softened before consuming.[[5]](#cite_note-:0-5) They were an important part of a British sailor's diet until the introduction of canned food in the 1800s,[[6]](#cite_note-6) although the product remained a staple for soldiers during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). Not having ready access to wheat, [Confederate soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army "Confederate States Army") made a similar item using ground corn, known as “corn dodgers” or “Johnny cakes".[[7]](#cite_note-7)

In 1836, [Jonathan Dodgson Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carr's "Carr's"), who owned a mill and bakery in [Carlisle, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle,_England "Carlisle, England"), began milling wheat for his Table Water biscuits.[[8]](#cite_note-8) Made of flour and water,[[9]](#cite_note-:1-9) the recipe was derived directly from hard tack, but rolled much thinner.[[5]](#cite_note-:0-5) As a thin, crisp cracker, water biscuits were more palatable than hard tack but still suitable for long-term storage by sailors and travelers.[[10]](#cite_note-10)

Types
-----

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Cracker_(food)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Types")]

There are two major types of crackers: soda (or [saltine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltine "Saltine")), which are fermented and leavened with yeast; and snack crackers, which are, in most cases, chemically-leavened. Soda crackers normally do not contain [added sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Added_sugar "Added sugar") and the fat or shortening level is quite low.[[11]](#cite_note-11) Another type of cracker uses no leavening; examples are [matzah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matzah "Matzah"),[[12]](#cite_note-12)[water crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_crackers "Water crackers") (also known as water biscuits),[[9]](#cite_note-:1-9) and [Triscuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triscuits "Triscuits").[[13]](#cite_note-13)

Crackers come in many shapes and sizes, such as round, [rectangular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangle "Rectangle"), [triangular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle "Triangle"), or irregular. Crackers sometimes have [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese "Cheese") or [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice "Spice") as ingredients, or even [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken "Chicken") stock. Saltines and [oyster crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_cracker "Oyster cracker") are often used in or served with [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup "Soup"). [Cheese crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_cracker "Cheese cracker") are prepared using cheese as a main ingredient; commercial examples include [Cheez-It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheez-It "Cheez-It"), [Cheese Nips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheese_Nips "Cheese Nips"), and [Goldfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldfish_(cracker) "Goldfish (cracker)").

[Graham crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_cracker "Graham cracker") and [digestive biscuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestive_biscuit "Digestive biscuit") are treated more like cookies than crackers. Although they were both invented for their supposed health benefits, modern versions of both are sweet. Similarly, [animal crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_crackers "Animal crackers") are crackers in name only. Animal crackers and graham crackers may have docking holes.
