Popcorn – A Great Snack Choice
==============================

May 19, 2026  [Marlene Geiger](https://blogs.extension.iastate.edu/answerline/author/mrgeiger/)

Popcorn is a favorite snack choice for many. It is also one of America’s truly native foods. Being an important food of the Aztec Indians, an ear of popcorn discovered in a Bat Cave in New Mexico in 1948 and 1950 is thought to be 5,600 years old, according to radiocarbon tests. Not only was popcorn used for food, it also was used for ceremonial decoration.

Popcorn really caught on during the 1890s and was very popular through the Great Depression. Street vendors, pushing steam or gas-powered poppers, followed crowds at fairs, parks, and expositions to sell popcorn. During the Depression, popcorn at 5 or 10 cents a bag was one of the few luxuries struggling families could afford ([USDA](https://www.nal.usda.gov/exhibits/speccoll/exhibits/show/popcorn/popcorn-explosion)). Popcorn remains one of the most affordable and healthy snack options available. When prepared at home from kernels, the cost per serving is small (~15 cents per serving) and provides a whole grain snack.

There are so many reasons to include popcorn as part of a healthy diet. Besides being [whole grain](http://www.besthealthmag.ca/eat-well/nutrition/popcorn-power-why-grains-are-healthier-than-we-thought), it is high in fiber (1.3 grams per cup), low in calories (31 calories per one cup air popped popcorn), and rich in antioxidants. Ounce for ounce, popcorn has more protein and phosphorus than potato chips and more iron than eggs and spinach. Popcorn is [gluten free](https://www.urmc.rochester.edu/encyclopedia/content?ContentTypeID=134&ContentID=183) and when ground, can be used in place of breadcrumbs for coating. Popcorn is also a low [glycemic](https://extension.illinois.edu/diabetes/glycemic-load-and-glycemic-index) food. Of all plant foods, popcorn has one of the highest concentrations of [polyphenols](https://www.chhs.colostate.edu/krnc/monthly-blog/what-are-polyphenols-another-great-reason-to-eat-fruits-and-veggies/) which work as antioxidants helping rid the body of cell-damaging free radicals; popcorn has more polyphenols and antioxidants, up to 300 mg per serving, often exceeding those found in many fruits and vegetables ([Science Daily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/03/120325173008.htm#)).

There are many popcorn options available. The most economical and consumer controlled option is to purchase whole kernels and pop at home. Air popping is the healthiest option. Walnut, avocado or extra virgin olive oils are best when making popcorn on the stovetop. Canola oil is the next best option.  Purchasing kernels over microwave varieties avoids the use of artificial trans-fats, flavors, and additives and ensures freshness. Make sure to read the labels of the pre-packaged options paying attention to the serving size, fat, and salt content; choose the most natural product without a lot of added ingredients and flavor additions. The healthiest ready-to-eat (pre-popped) bagged popcorn options focus on minimal ingredients—typically just popcorn, healthy oil, and salt.
