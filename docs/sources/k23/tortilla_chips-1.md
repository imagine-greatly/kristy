**Nixtamalization** ([/ˌnɪʃtəməlɪˈzeɪʃən, ˌnɪks-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*nish-tə-mə-lih-ZAY-shən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is a process for the preparation of [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize") (corn), or other [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal_grain "Cereal grain"), in which the grain is soaked and [cooked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking "Cooking") in an [alkaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline "Alkaline") solution, usually [limewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limewater "Limewater") (but sometimes aqueous [alkali metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkali_metal "Alkali metal") [carbonates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonate "Carbonate")),[[1]](#cite_note-ml-1) washed, and then [hulled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulling "Hulling"). The term can also refer to the removal via an alkali process of the [pericarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericarp "Pericarp") from other grains such as [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum").

Nixtamalized corn has several benefits over unprocessed grain: It is more easily ground, its [nutritional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrition "Nutrition") value is increased, [flavor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavor_(taste) "Flavor (taste)") and [aroma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aroma "Aroma") are improved, and [mycotoxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycotoxin "Mycotoxin") are reduced by up to 97–100% (for [aflatoxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflatoxin "Aflatoxin")).[[2]](#cite_note-2)[[3]](#cite_note-3)

Lime and ash are highly alkaline: the alkalinity helps the dissolution of [hemicellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicellulose "Hemicellulose"), the major glue-like component of the maize cell walls, and loosens the hulls from the kernels and softens the maize.[[4]](#cite_note-researchgate-4) The [tryptophan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryptophan "Tryptophan") in corn proteins is made more available for human absorption, thus helping to prevent [niacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niacin "Niacin") deficiency ([pellagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellagra "Pellagra")).[[5]](#cite_note-fao92-5): §5.2 Tryptophan is the metabolic precursor of endogenous niacin (Vitamin B3).

Some of the corn oil is broken down into emulsifying agents (monoglycerides and diglycerides), while bonding of the maize proteins to each other is also facilitated. The divalent [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium "Calcium") in lime acts as a cross-linking agent for protein and polysaccharide acidic side chains.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

While [cornmeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornmeal "Cornmeal") made from untreated ground maize is unable by itself to form a dough on addition of water, nixtamalized cornmeal will form a dough, called [masa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masa "Masa"). These benefits make nixtamalization a crucial preliminary step for further processing of maize into food products, and the process is employed using both traditional and industrial methods in the production of [tortillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla "Tortilla") and [tortilla chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla_chip "Tortilla chip") (but not [corn chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_chip "Corn chip")), [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale "Tamale"), [hominy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hominy "Hominy"), and many other foodstuffs.

Etymology
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Etymology")]

In the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec "Aztec") language [Nahuatl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl "Nahuatl"), the word for the product of this procedure is *nixtamalli* or *nextamalli* (pronounced [[niʃtaˈmalːi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Nahuatl "Help:IPA/Nahuatl") or [[neʃtaˈmalːi]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Nahuatl "Help:IPA/Nahuatl")), which in turn has yielded [Mexican Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Spanish "Mexican Spanish") *nixtamal* ([[nistaˈmal]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish "Help:IPA/Spanish")). The Nahuatl word is a compound of *nextli*, "ash", referring to the [lye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lye "Lye") involved in its preparation, and *tamalli*, "unformed/cooked corn dough, [tamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale "Tamale")".[[7]](#cite_note-7) The term *nixtamalization* can also be used to describe the removal of the pericarp from any grain by an alkali process, including maize, [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum "Sorghum"), and others. When the unaltered Spanish spelling *nixtamalización* is used in written English, however, it almost exclusively refers to maize.

The labels on packages of commercially sold tortillas prepared with nixtamalized maize usually list *corn treated with [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_hydroxide "Calcium hydroxide")* as an ingredient in English, while the Spanish versions list *maíz nixtamalizado*.

History
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

### Mesoamerica

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Mesoamerica")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Dried_Maize_Mote_from_Oaxaca.png/250px-Dried_Maize_Mote_from_Oaxaca.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dried_Maize_Mote_from_Oaxaca.png)

Dried, treated maize sold in [Oaxaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaxaca "Oaxaca"), Mexico ([US quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarter_(United_States_coin) "Quarter (United States coin)"), 24 mm, and [Mexican peso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_peso "Mexican peso"), 21 mm, shown for scale)

The process of nixtamalization was first developed in [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica "Mesoamerica"), where [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize "Maize") was originally cultivated. The earliest evidence is found in Guatemala's southern coast, with equipment dating from 1200 to 1500 BCE.[[8]](#cite_note-staller-8) How nixtamalization was discovered is not currently known with certainty, but it may have been through the use of hot [cooking stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_stone "Cooking stone") to boil corn in early cultures which did not have cooking vessels robust enough to put directly on fire or coals. In limestone-bedrock regions like those in Guatemala and southern Mexico, heated chunks of limestone would naturally be used, and experiments show that hot limestone makes the cooking water sufficiently alkaline to cause nixtamalization. Archaeological evidence of this conjecture has been found in southern [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah").[[9]](#cite_note-9)

The [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec "Aztec") and [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization "Maya civilization") civilizations developed nixtamalization by using slaked lime ([calcium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_hydroxide "Calcium hydroxide"), or "cal") and lye ([potassium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_hydroxide "Potassium hydroxide")) to create alkaline solutions. The [Chibcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibcha "Chibcha") people to the north of the ancient [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca "Inca") also used calcium hydroxide, while the tribes of North America used [soda ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soda_ash "Soda ash").

The nixtamalization process was very important in the early Mesoamerican diet, as there is very little [niacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_B3 "Vitamin B3") in corn and the tryptophan within is unavailable without processing.[[5]](#cite_note-fao92-5): §5.2 A population that depends on untreated maize as a [staple food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staple_food "Staple food") risks malnourishment and is more likely to develop [deficiency diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficiency_disease "Deficiency disease") such as [pellagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellagra "Pellagra"), niacin deficiency, or [kwashiorkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwashiorkor "Kwashiorkor"), the absence of certain amino acids that maize is deficient in. Maize cooked with lime or other alkali provided bioavailable niacin to Mesoamericans. Beans provided the otherwise missing amino acids required to balance maize for [complete protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_protein "Complete protein").[[5]](#cite_note-fao92-5): §8

### Spread

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Spread")]

The spread of maize cultivation in the Americas was accompanied by the adoption of the nixtamalization process. Traditional and contemporary regional cuisines (including [Maya cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_cuisine "Maya cuisine"), [Aztec cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_cuisine "Aztec cuisine"), and [Mexican cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine "Mexican cuisine")) included, and still include, foods based on nixtamalized maize.

The process has not substantially declined in usage in the [Mesoamerican region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_region "Mesoamerican region"), though there has been a decline in North America. Many Native North American tribes, such as the [Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyandot_people "Wyandot people"), no longer use the process.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] In some Mesoamerican and North American regions, dishes are still made from nixtamalized maize prepared by traditional techniques. The [Hopi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopi "Hopi") produce sodium carbonate from ashes of various native plants and trees.[[10]](#cite_note-10)[[11]](#cite_note-11) Some contemporary Maya use calcium salts in the form of ashes of burnt mussel shells or heated limestone.[[12]](#cite_note-12)

By 900 CE, nixtamalizing maize based foodways began to spread to the American Southeast, serving as the dietary basis for what would come to be called [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture "Mississippian culture") populations. The most important nixtamalzing dish was hominy, a dish of boiled maize kernels, either ground or whole, that were nixtamalized using woodash or wood ash lye, both made from the ashes of hardwood trees.[[13]](#cite_note-13) The continuation of the hominy foodway after European colonization helped reduce cases of pellagra within twentieth century Native populations.[[14]](#cite_note-14)

In the United States, European settlers rarely adopted the nixtamalization process, considering it unnecessary and not to their taste, though maize became a staple among the poor of the southern states.[[15]](#cite_note-15) This led to endemic [pellagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellagra "Pellagra") in poor populations throughout the southern US in the early 20th century.[[16]](#cite_note-16) A more varied diet and fortification of wheat flour, the other staple food, have essentially eliminated this deficiency.[[17]](#cite_note-17)

Although maize cultivation spread far beyond the Americas via [colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism"), the process of nixtamalization did not. For this reason, outbreaks of pellagra occurred in many areas of Europe, where corn often became a staple crop due to its high caloric efficiency relative to the land use required for its cultivation.[[18]](#cite_note-18) A prominent example of endemic pellagra occurred in northern Italy in the late 19th century, from which the English term pellagra derives: "pel" meaning skin and "agra" meaning rough or sour in the [Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamo "Bergamo") dialect.[[19]](#cite_note-19)

Process
-------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Process")]

### Cooking

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Cooking")]

[![](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Nixtamalized_Corn_maize_El_Salvador_recipe.jpg/250px-Nixtamalized_Corn_maize_El_Salvador_recipe.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Nixtamalized_Corn_maize_El_Salvador_recipe.jpg)

Dry maize, untreated (left) and boiled in lime (right). In this case, typical of El Salvador, 1 pound (450 g) is boiled with 1 tbsp (15 mL) of lime for 15 minutes, left to stand for a few hours, and washed with fresh water. The hulls are removed, and the kernels ground into *masa*. Exact methods vary by use and region.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In the first step of nixtamalization, kernels of dried maize are [cooked in an alkaline solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline_hydrolysis "Alkaline hydrolysis") at or near the mixture's boiling point. After cooking, the maize is steeped in the cooking liquid for a period. The length of time for which the maize is boiled and soaked varies according to local traditions and the type of food being prepared, with cooking times ranging from a few minutes to an hour, and soaking times from a few minutes to about a day.

During cooking and soaking, a number of chemical changes take place in the grains of maize. Because plant cell wall components, including [hemicellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicellulose "Hemicellulose") and [pectin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectin "Pectin"), are highly soluble in alkaline solutions, the kernels soften and their pericarps (hulls) loosen. The grain hydrates and absorbs calcium or [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium "Potassium") (depending on the alkali used) from the cooking solution. Starches swell and [gelatinize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch_gelatinization "Starch gelatinization"), and some starches disperse into the liquid. Certain chemicals[*[which?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")*] from the germ are released that allow the cooked grains to be ground more easily, yet make dough made from the grains less likely to tear and break down. Cooking changes the grain's protein matrix, which makes proteins and nutrients from the [endosperm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosperm "Endosperm") of the kernel more available to the human body.

### Extraction

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Extraction")]

After cooking, the alkaline liquid (known as **nejayote** from Nahuatl *nexayotl* - nextli 'ash' y ayotl 'liquid'), containing dissolved hull, starch, and other corn matter, is decanted and discarded (or sometimes used for making [amate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amate "Amate") bark paper).[[20]](#cite_note-20) The kernels are washed thoroughly of remaining nejayote, which has an unpleasant flavor. The [pericarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericarp "Pericarp") is then removed, leaving the [endosperm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosperm "Endosperm") of the [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_grain "Whole grain") with or without the [germ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal_germ "Cereal germ"), depending on the process.[[21]](#cite_note-21)[[22]](#cite_note-22)[[23]](#cite_note-23)[[24]](#cite_note-24)[[25]](#cite_note-25) This hulling is performed by hand, in traditional or very small-scale preparation, or mechanically, in larger scale or industrial production.

The prepared grain is called hominy, [mote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mote_(food) "Mote (food)"), or nixtamal. Nixtamal has many uses, contemporary and historic. Whole nixtamal may be used fresh or dried for later use. Whole nixtamal is used in the preparation of [pozole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozole "Pozole"), [menudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menudo_(soup) "Menudo (soup)"), and other foods. Ground fresh nixtamal is made into *masa* (nixtamal [dough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dough "Dough")) and used to make [tortillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla "Tortilla"), [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale "Tamale"), and [pupusas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pupusa "Pupusa"). Dried and ground, it is called *masa harina* or instant *masa* flour, and is reconstituted and used like *masa*.

The term hominy may refer to whole, coarsely ground, or finely ground nixtamal, or to a cooked porridge (also called [grits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grits "Grits")) prepared from any of these. [Samp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samp "Samp") is similar to grits, but a product of a different process.

### Enzymatic nixtamalization

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Enzymatic nixtamalization")]

An alternative process for use in industrial settings has been developed known as **enzymatic nixtamalization**, which uses [protease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protease "Protease") enzymes to accelerate the changes that occur in traditional nixtamalization, a technique borrowed from modern [corn wet-milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_wet-milling "Corn wet-milling"). In this process, corn or corn meal is first partially hydrated in hot water, so that enzymes can penetrate the grain, then soaked briefly (for approximately 30 minutes) at 50–60 °C (122–140 °F) in an alkaline solution containing protease enzymes. A secondary enzymatic digestion may follow to further dissolve the pericarp. The resulting nixtamal is ground with little or no washing or hulling.

By pre-soaking the maize, minimizing the alkali used to adjust the pH of the alkaline solution, reducing the cooking temperature, accelerating processing, and reusing excess processing liquids, enzymatic nixtamalization can reduce the use of energy and water, lower nejayote (alkaline wastewater) production, decrease maize lost in processing, and shorten the production time (to approximately four hours) compared to traditional nixtamalization with only a minor loss in quality.[[26]](#cite_note-26)

Impact on health
----------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Nixtamalization&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Impact on health")]

The primary nutritional benefits of nixtamalization arise from the alkaline processing involved. The processing renders the protein more digestible, allowing [tryptophan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryptophan "Tryptophan") to be absorbed by humans. Humans can convert tryptophan into [niacin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niacin "Niacin"), thus helping to prevent [pellagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pellagra "Pellagra"). Other measures of [protein quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_quality "Protein quality") are also improved.[[5]](#cite_note-fao92-5): §5.2 It was originally thought that the anti-pellagra action stems from increased availability of niacin (compared to a hemicellulose-bound form called "niacytin"),[[4]](#cite_note-researchgate-4) but multiple experiments have disproven this theory.[[5]](#cite_note-fao92-5): §5.2

Secondary benefits can arise from the grain's absorption of minerals from the alkali used or from the vessels used in preparation. These effects can increase [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium "Calcium") (by 750%, with 85% available for absorption), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron"), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper "Copper"), and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc "Zinc").[[4]](#cite_note-researchgate-4)

Nixtamalization significantly deactivates [mycotoxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycotoxin "Mycotoxin") which are putative [carcinogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogens "Carcinogens"). [Aflatoxins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflatoxin "Aflatoxin") are reduced by 90–94%. [Fumonisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumonisin "Fumonisin") are reduced by 82%.[[4]](#cite_note-researchgate-4)
