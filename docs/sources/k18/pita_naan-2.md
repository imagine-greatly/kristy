What’s Healthier: Bread vs. Pita vs. Naan?
==========================================

by
Ali Mooradian

in
[Wildgrain Blog](/blogs/news)

*By Lucy Yanckello, Ph.D.*

*[Wildgrain, opens in a new tab](https://wildgrain.com/) is the first bake-from-frozen subscription box for sourdough breads, fresh pastas, and artisanal pastries.*

Bread is a beloved carbohydrate source be it leavened with yeast, steamed, fried, or gluten free. One of the oldest staples in our diets, there are dozens of types of bread around the world with different flavors, textures, and purposes. In fact, some of the most popular types of breads, both considered flatbreads, are naan and pita. Read on to learn more about the difference between the two, and how they compare to loaf bread in flavor, texture, and nutrient profile. 

Which is healthier to eat: bread or pita or naan?
-------------------------------------------------

In contrast to loaf bread, [pita](https://wildgrain.com/blogs/news/bread-vs-tortilla-vs-pita-wrap) bread and naan are considered to be flatbreads. Pita bread is yeast-leavened and round, turning into layers when baked that create a pocket that is easily filled. Naan is also yeast leavened, and contains water (or milk), flour, salt, and the addition of butter and yogurt.  

Depending on what your nutrient needs are, bread, pita, and naan can all fit into a healthy diet. Naan and pita have many of the same benefits of other breads, including carbs, iron, and vitamin B. B vitamins are necessary to help break down and convert food into energy all while keeping hair, skin, and eyes healthy. Iron is essential for oxygen transport through the blood and preserves many vital processes in the body including immune function, body temperature, and digestion. 

All three are relatively low in fiber (around 1 to 2 grams). Because their ingredients are similar, loaf bread and pita both have very similar nutritional profiles, including similar levels of protein and fat (around 2 to 3 grams). Naan is higher in protein (around 9 grams) and fat (around 5 grams) than pita and other breads.

Is pita bread OK for weight loss? Is naan OK for weight loss? Can you still eat pita and naan when trying to lose weight?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every food, including pita bread and naan, can have a place in a healthy diet when trying to lose weight, as long as they’re enjoyed in moderation. The higher protein content in naan will allow you to feel fuller for longer, curbing cravings that cause excess snacking. On the other hand, naan has almost three times the carbohydrate content (45 grams) compared to loaf bread and pita (around 13 to 17 grams). So yes, you can still eat pita and naan when trying to lose weight, but in moderate amounts.

Is pita OK for IBS? Is naan OK for IBS?
---------------------------------------

[PitaGo to bread vs tortilla vs pita wrap](https://wildgrain.com/blogs/news/bread-vs-tortilla-vs-pita-wrap) bread and naan unfortunately are not great choices for [IBS](https://wildgrain.com/blogs/news/best-bread-ibs). Pita bread and naan are made with wheat flour, meaning they are high in FODMAP, or Fermentable, Oligosaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols. This is an acronym for a group of carbohydrates that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. This poor absorption leads to fermentation by the gut bacteria which results in the production of gas and other byproducts. This leads to Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) symptoms such as bloating, gas, and abdominal pain. 

What’s the difference between naan vs. bread? Which is healthier: bread or naan?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Naan is a flatbread, whereas [breadGo to sourdough loaf](https://wildgrain.com/products/sourdough-loaf) comes in loaves. Naan is higher in fat, protein, and carbohydrates than loaf bread. The higher protein content of naan will allow you to feel fuller for longer and curve cravings. However, the fat content of naan (from butter in the recipe) comes largely from saturated fats, which should account for less than 10% of your total daily caloric intake according to the USDA. Both naan and bread can find their place in a healthy diet in moderation. 

What’s the difference between pita vs. bread? Which is healthier: normal bread or pita?
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[PitaGo to bread vs tortilla vs pita wrap](https://wildgrain.com/blogs/news/bread-vs-tortilla-vs-pita-wrap) is a flatbread whereas [breadGo to sourdough loaf](https://wildgrain.com/products/sourdough-loaf) comes in loaves. Despite their difference in shape, pita and bread have similar ingredients - largely water, yeast, flour, and salt. Because of the similarity in ingredients, the nutrient profiles of pita and normal bread are basically identical. Both pita and loaf bread have relatively low fat, fiber, and protein content.

What’s the difference between pita vs. naan? Which is healthier: naan or pita?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Naan and [pitaGo to bread vs tortilla vs pita wrap](https://wildgrain.com/blogs/news/bread-vs-tortilla-vs-pita-wrap) are similar in that they are both flatbreads, but naan contains butter and yogurt,  whereas pita does not. As stated earlier, naan is high in fat, and specifically high in saturated fats. Pita on the other hand is considered to be a low fat food. Naan does have other benefits, including a higher protein content which will keep you fuller for longer and curb cravings. There are some pros and cons to both naan and pitas in terms of nutrient profiles, and both can be eaten in moderation in any healthy diet.

Oatmeal has quietly split into three camps: soaking oats overnight, cooking oats, and eating oats raw. Although the debate gets framed as a nutrition question, the oats themselves are identical in all three. What changes is how easily your body reaches the starch and fiber inside, and that difference shows up in blood sugar, digestion, and satiety.
What’s the difference: overnight oats vs. cooked oats vs. raw oats?
The difference comes down to heat and water. Cooked oats are heated in liquid, which swells the starch granules and breaks them open in a process called gelatinization. This gives oatmeal its soft creamy texture, and it also makes the starch quicker to digest. Overnight oats skip the heat and use time instead, softening in cold liquid over several hours without the starch fully breaking down. Raw oats get neither, so their starch content stays tightly packed. Starch that stays intact in this way is called resistant scratch because it resists digestion in your small intestine and passes through to your large intestine.
Which is better, overnight oats or cooked oats or raw oats? Is cooked oatmeal better for you than overnight oats?
Neither is better across the board, because each trades one advantage for another. The best type is ultimately the one you enjoy eating enough to eat regularly. Cooked oats are the easiest to digest, and the heat pulls more beta-glucan out of the oat. Beta-glucan is a soluble fiber that thickens into a gel in your gut. That gel binds cholesterol and carries it out of the body which is what gives cooked oats the edge on lowering cholesterol. Overnight oats keep their starch intact, so more of it arrives in the gut as resistant starch, which slows digestion and allows blood sugar to rise more gently. Raw oats hold the most resistant starch, but they are the hardest to digest because they absorb water on their way through.
Can you eat oats straight from the bag? Is it ok to eat raw oats?
Yes, you can eat oats straight from the container. Commercial oats are steamed and heat treated during processing so the oats that make it to you are not truly raw - though that step is nothing like cooking them with a liquid at home. The real issue is comfort rather than safety. Dry oats absorb water in your digestive tract, which can leave you bloated and constipated. If you like the texture, stirring dry oats into yogurt or a smoothie softens them first and avoids most of the discomfort. 
Does cooking oats destroy nutrients? Are overnight oats, cooked oats, or raw oats healthier for you?
Cooking oats does not destroy their nutrients. Fiber, protein and minerals all hold up to heat so a pot of oatmeal is nutritionally close to the dry oats it started as. What cooking changes is how easily your body can reach those nutrients, not how much of each there are. Heat makes the starch quicker to digest and pulls more of the beta-glucan out. The nutrition label reads nearly the same across all three, so the real difference is in how your body handles the meal rather than what is in it.
Does refrigerating oatmeal lower the glycemic index? Are overnight oats better for diabetics?
Yes, refrigerating oatmeal can lower its glycemic impact but how that happens depends on whether the oats were cooked first. Overnight oats deliver resistant starch that was never gelatinized to begin with, while cooked oats reform resistant starch once chilled. This is why leftover oatmeal behaves differently than a fresh pot. Either way, both land gentler on blood sugar than hot oatmeal eaten straight off the stove. Responses to the same bowl still vary a lot from person to person, so anyone building meals around a diabetes diagnosis should consult their care team.
Which is better for weight loss, overnight oats or cooked oats or raw oats?
Overnight oats have a small edge for weight management, and it comes from the milk, rather than the method. Soaking needs liquid, so most people reach for milk or yogurt instead of water, which turns a bowl of mostly carbohydrates into a source of protein. That protein works with the fiber in oats to slow digestion and keep you feeling fuller for longer. Cooked oats do the same thing if you opt for milk or yogurt over water, so the real difference is habit rather than method. Raw dry oats fall to the bottom because they are hard to eat in a filling way. Does cooking oats destroy fiber? Are overnight oats better for your gut than cooked oats?
Cooking oats does not destroy the fiber, since heat does not break fiber apart. What changes is how much of it reaches your large intestine. Cooked oats deliver the most available beta-glucan, while overnight oats deliver resistant starch as well. Both feed the good bacteria in your gut, and that fermentation produces short chain fatty acids. These compounds support gut lining and help reduce inflammation. Overnight oats have a modest advantage due to the addition of resistant starch, but cooked oats feed your microbiome perfectly well too.
Is it healthier to eat oatmeal cold or hot? 
Temperature on its own does not make oatmeal healthier. A cold bowl and a hot bowl made from the same oats gives you the same calories, protein, fiber, and minerals. What matters is whether heat was used to prepare them, not how warm they are when you eat them. Cold oats are easier to make ahead and carry with you, while hot oats feel more satisfying to eat to a lot of people. What is the healthiest milk to use for oatmeal? What is the healthiest milk to use for overnight oats?
The healthiest milk to use for oatmeal is one that you enjoy and that adds protein to your dish. Dairy milk and soy milk contain roughly 7 to 8 grams per cup and also contain calcium. Almond milk, cashew milk, and oat milk contribute very little protein. The sweetened versions of any alternative milk option can add more sugar than your toppings, so opt for the unsweetened version if you're able. Kefir and plain greek yogurt thinned with a splash of milk are worth trying too, since they add protein and live microbial cultures at the same time.
Which is the healthiest, overnight oats or cooked oats or raw oats?
Overnight oats, cooked oats, and dry oats all have their own advantages. Soaking gives you slower-digesting starch and pairs naturally with milk or yogurt in a way that rounds out your meal. Cooked oats win on digestibility and availability of beta-glucan which matters most if lowering your cholesterol is what you are after. Packing school lunches can feel like a nightly negotiation between what your kid will actually eat and what you want them to eat. Although a healthy lunch sounds like it takes planning and effort, most of the work comes down to one simple formula you repeat with different ingredients. Once you have that structure, packing lunchboxes becomes streamlined and the container comes home empty far more often than not.
What is a good healthy lunch for school? What makes a healthy school lunch?
A good school lunch has four parts working together: a whole grain, a protein source, a fruit or vegetable, and something to drink. A simple way to picture it is filling half the lunch with fruits and vegetables, a quarter with whole grain, and the last quarter with protein. That balance matters because carbohydrates on their own burn off quickly, while protein, fiber, and fat slow digestion and keep energy steady throughout the afternoon. Water and milk are the best drinks to include, since sugary beverages add calories without containing the nutrients kids need to keep focused in class. Repeating the same four categories removes the daily decision fatigue.
What are some easy ways to make healthy lunches for school? What are good healthy school lunch ideas?
The easiest healthy lunches are the ones you assemble rather than cook. Batch prep does most of the work. For example, roast a tray of chicken, boil a dozen eggs, and wash and cut produce on the weekend so everything is prepped and easy to grab from the fridge. Find a few reliable options like a sandwich on whole grain bread, a thermos of pasta or soup, a bento box of cheese and crackers, or leftovers from the night before. Frozen options that bake in minutes make the process even easier, like Wildgrain’s Slow-Fermented Margherita Pizza, which goes from the freezer to sliced and packed in about 10 minutes with 15 grams of protein in half of the pie.
Are sandwiches a healthy school lunch option for kids? 
Yes, sandwiches are a healthy school lunch option, and the bread you choose does most of the work. Whole grain bread brings fiber, B-vitamins, and minerals that refined white bread loses in processing. That fiber is what carries kids through the afternoon classes and keeps them alert. Wildgrain’s Sourdough Whole Wheat Loaf gives you 6 grams of protein and 2 grams of fiber in a single slice. Fill the sandwich with protein like turkey, egg salad, hummus, or cheese. Tuck something crunchy inside the sandwich too, like cucumber or bell pepper. Sandwiches get boring quickly, so rotating the bread and filling is what matters in terms of your kid actually eating it.
What are healthy school lunch ideas for weight loss?
Lunches built with weight in mind look almost identical to lunches built for energy and satiety, with slightly more attention paid to fiber and protein content. Vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and a protein source fill kids up on fewer calories than packaged snacks and refined carbohydrates. This tends to help reduce cravings and snacking later in the day. Swapping sugary drinks for water often makes a bigger difference than changing the food itself. Restricting a growing child’s food can work against their development, so adding filling foods usually works better than taking foods away. Any concern about a child’s weight is worth raising with their pediatrician who can look at growth over time before you make any changes to their nutritional intake.
What are healthy school lunch ideas that are high-protein?
High-protein lunches keep kids fuller and mentally steadier for longer than carbohydrate-heavy ones. Easy sources to rotate through include rotisserie chicken, turkey, hard boiled eggs, greek yogurt, cottage cheese, string cheese, hummus, edamame, and black beans. Pairing two protein sources in one lunch like string cheese alongside a turkey sandwich is a simple way to raise the protein content. Wildgrain’s Sourdough Grilled Cheese Sandwich carries 20 grams of protein and travels well in a warm thermos. Spreading protein across every meal rather than saving it for dinner tends to keep energy and blood sugar more consistent throughout the school day.
What is the healthiest allergen-friendly school lunch to pack? 
The healthiest allergen-friendly lunch is built from whole foods with labels you can actually read. Fresh fruit, vegetables, rice, potatoes, beans, and plain meats are naturally free of most major allergens which makes them a dependable base to build around. Packaged snacks are where hidden allergens normally turn up, so rechecking labels each time you buy is a habit worth keeping. Manufacturers often change formulas without warning. Many schools also have classroom-wide restrictions, so knowing your school’s policy before you plan a week of lunches saves a lot of backtracking. Keeping a short rotation of safe, already tested meals is far less stressful than trying to reinvent the wheel every morning.
What is the healthiest nut-free lunch to pack for school? How do I make nut-free nutritious school lunches?
The healthiest nut-free lunch replaces nut butter or other nut based components without losing the protein it was providing. Sunflower seed butter and soy butter spread the same way and work in most nut-free classrooms. Beyond spreads, cheese, eggs, beans, yogurt, and sliced meats all easily close the protein gap. The healthiest gluten-free lunch leans on naturally gluten free foods rather than heavily processed gluten-free substitutes. The healthiest lunch to pack is the balanced meal your child will actually eat.
