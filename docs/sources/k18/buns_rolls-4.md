A **bread roll** is a small, oblong (or round, triangular or rhomboid) individual loaf of [bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread "Bread"), usually served with a meal and eaten plain or with [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter "Butter").[[1]](#cite_note-Stein_2015-1) Rolls can be served whole or cut and filled to make a [sandwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwich "Sandwich").[[2]](#cite_note-2)

Regional variations
-------------------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Regional variations")]

### Europe

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Europe")]

Rolls are common throughout Europe, under many names, including local and dialectal ones, often [diminutives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminutive "Diminutive") of "bread".

Rolls range from white rolls made with [wheat flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat "Wheat"), to dark rolls containing mostly [rye flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye "Rye"). Many variants include spices, such as [coriander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander "Coriander") and [cumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumin "Cumin"), or nuts. Also common are bread rolls containing or garnished with whole seeds such as [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame "Sesame"), [poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poppy_seed "Poppy seed"), [pumpkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumpkin "Pumpkin") or [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower "Sunflower").[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

*French rolls* are various bread rolls with a [baguette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette "Baguette")-like crust.[[3]](#cite_note-3)[[4]](#cite_note-4)

In Germany, rolls are *Brötchen*; in Switzerland, *Brötli*. Other German names include *Rundstück* ("round piece") in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg") and [Schleswig-Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleswig-Holstein "Schleswig-Holstein");[[5]](#cite_note-5)*Weckerl* or more specific *Semmel* in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria"), [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony "Saxony") and southern [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria "Bavaria"); *Weck* and *Weckle* in much of [Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg "Baden-Württemberg"), [Franconia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franconia "Franconia") and [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland "Saarland"); *Schrippe* in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin "Berlin") and parts of [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg "Brandenburg"). Some of these names are used in other European languages as well, for example as *zsemle* in [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language "Hungarian language"), or *rundstykker* ("round pieces") in [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language "Danish language") and [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language "Norwegian language").
In the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands") they are called *broodje*.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] The *Doppelweck* or *Doppelbrötchen* is a type of bread roll originating from the Saarland consisting of two rolls joined together side-by-side before baking.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Similarly, the *[Röggelchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6ggelchen "Röggelchen")* of the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland "Rhineland") is a double rye roll. A [lye roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lye_roll "Lye roll") is a roll-shaped [pretzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretzel "Pretzel").

The Italian diminutive *[panino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panino "Panino")* means both the roll and a sandwich made from a roll. The *[Kaisersemmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_roll "Kaiser roll")* reappears in Italy as the *[Michetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michetta "Michetta")* or *Rosetta*. In [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language "Swedish language"), a bread roll is a *(frukost) bulle* '(breakfast) bun', *franskbrödbulle* 'French bread bun' or simply *fralla* 'bun', and is eaten with butter and a topping (marmalade, cheese, ham, salami) for special weekend breakfasts. In [Ticino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticino "Ticino"), Switzerland, [pane ticinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pane_ticinese "Pane ticinese") is made to separate into rolls.

*Carcaça* and *papo-seco* are two traditional Portuguese breads.[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

On the Spanish island of [Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallorca "Mallorca"), the *[llonguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llonguet "Llonguet")* is also known as *panet francès*, meaning 'small French bread' or 'French bread roll' in Catalan.

There are many regional varieties of bread rolls in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"): roll, bap, [barm cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barm_cake "Barm cake"), batch, breadcake, bun, cob, teacake and muffin.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

In Ireland, the [blaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaa "Blaa") is a specialty of [County Kilkenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Kilkenny "County Kilkenny").

### Arab world

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Arab world")]

Bread rolls are popular in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world "Arab world") and come in many varieties and names, "French-like" bread rolls exist in many forms.[[7]](#cite_note-asamr-7)

*[Eish fino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eish_fino "Eish fino")* is a [baguette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette "Baguette")-like bread roll that is popular in [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt").[[8]](#cite_note-wsjegypt-8) It is also known as *Samouli* bread ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): صامولي).[[9]](#cite_note-alaraby-9) "*Samoon*" bread ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): سمون, not to be confused with [samoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoon "Samoon") bread) is used in the Arab world for a variety of medium to high rising breads, like burger buns.[[10]](#cite_note-International_Journal_of_Nutrition-10)[[11]](#cite_note-enabBaladi-11) Other names for or varieties of bread rolls include *hamam* bread ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language "Arabic language"): حمام, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic "Romanization of Arabic"): *ḥamām*), popular in Jordan.[[7]](#cite_note-asamr-7)[[12]](#cite_note-royaNews-12)

Bread rolls are consumed in large quantities around the region, and are considered everyday items, to the extent that the price of some varieties of bread rolls is [subsidized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidy "Subsidy") in some countries, like [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"),[[12]](#cite_note-royaNews-12)[Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt "Egypt"),[[8]](#cite_note-wsjegypt-8) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria").[[11]](#cite_note-enabBaladi-11)

### Asia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Asia")]

In [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong") and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau "Macau"), *dyun faat baau* (短法包, literally "short French bread" or "short [baguette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette "Baguette")"), also known as the *zyu zai baau* (豬仔包, "piggy bun"), is used to make [pork chop buns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork_chop_bun "Pork chop bun").[[13]](#cite_note-13)

### India

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: India")]

Fried in butter and served with a mashed vegetable curry, bread rolls (pav) are a key component of the popular Mumbai street snack [pav bhaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pav_bhaji "Pav bhaji").

### Latin America

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Latin America")]

Across [Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America "Latin America"), the terms ***pan francés*** (Spanish) or ***[pão francês](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A3o_franc%C3%AAs "Pão francês")*** (Portuguese), both meaning "French bread", refer to various [baguette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette "Baguette")-like [bread rolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_roll) made with [wheat flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_flour "Wheat flour"), salt, water and [yeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker's_yeast "Baker's yeast").

#### Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay")]

In [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina "Argentina"), [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay "Paraguay"), and [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay "Uruguay"), ***pan felipe*** (Felipe bread) is a commonly eaten French bread roll.

In [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay "Uruguay"), a denser version is known as ***pan marsellés*** (Marseilles bread). The Marseille bread called *pan marsellés* in Uruguay is different from the Brazilian *[pão francês](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A3o_franc%C3%AAs "Pão francês")*. The Brazilian type is less dense and lighter and crispier. However, the Uruguayan version is made with a denser bread dough, resulting in a harder bread (similar to Italian bread), with the crust covered in maize flour before baking.

#### Bolivia, Chile, and Peru

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Bolivia, Chile, and Peru")]

In [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia "Bolivia") and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile "Chile"), the *[marraqueta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marraqueta "Marraqueta")* is sometimes called ***pan francés***. In [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru "Peru"), the term *pan francés* (or sometimes called *pan francés peruano*) is different from the marraqueta.[[14]](#cite_note-14)

#### Brazil

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Brazil")]

Main article: [Pão francês](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A3o_franc%C3%AAs "Pão francês")

The most popular bread in Brazil is the light and crispy ***[pão francês](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A3o_franc%C3%AAs "Pão francês")***. Pão francês is known by several names throughout Brazil, such as *cacetinho*, *pãozinho* (little bread), *pão de trigo* (wheat bread), pão de sal (salt bread), *pão de água* (water bread), *pão aguado* (watery bread), *careca*, and *pão Jacó* (Jacó bread). It is commonly found in delis and bakeries throughout Brazil.[[15]](#cite_note-15)[[16]](#cite_note-16)

#### Mexico and Central America

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Mexico and Central America")]

In the region of [Comarca Lagunera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca_Lagunera "Comarca Lagunera") in northeastern [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico"), *pan francés* (or sometimes called *pan francés lagunero*) is an important part of daily dining.[[17]](#cite_note-17)

In other parts of [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico "Mexico") and [Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America "Central America"), the word *pan francés* is used to refer to *[bolillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolillo "Bolillo")*.

### United States

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Bread_roll&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: United States")]
