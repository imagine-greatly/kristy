Costco Croissant Calories Nutrition and Price
=============================================

A Kirkland Signature butter croissant from the Costco bakery has **300 calories**. That’s per croissant, not per serving of something smaller, one croissant is the serving, weighing about 69 grams or roughly 2.4 ounces.

The calorie count isn’t really the interesting number, though. The saturated fat is.

Full Nutrition Breakdown
------------------------

Here’s everything on the label for one Costco butter croissant:

| Nutrient | Per croissant (69g) |
| --- | --- |
| Calories | **300** |
| Total fat | 17 to 18g |
| **Saturated fat** | **10 to 11g** |
| Trans fat | 0.5g |
| Cholesterol | 60mg |
| Sodium | 290 to 330mg |
| Total carbohydrates | 30g |
| Dietary fiber | <1g |
| Sugars | 4 to 5g |
| Protein | 6g |

Slight variations show up between sources because baking varies batch to batch, but 300 calories is the consistent figure across Costco’s own label and every nutrition database that tracks it.

The Saturated Fat Is the Real Story
-----------------------------------

Three hundred calories for a large pastry is honestly reasonable. A Costco croissant has fewer calories than most of the muffins in the same bakery case, which run 290 to 490.

The number that deserves attention is the **10 to 11 grams of saturated fat**. The FDA’s recommended daily limit is about 20 grams, which means [a single croissant burns through roughly half your day’s allowance](https://www.thetakeout.com/1831209/unhealthy-foods-avoid-costco-bakery/) before you’ve eaten anything else.

If you’re having one for breakfast, you’ve got two more meals to fit into the remaining half.

That’s the trade-off with real butter. The croissant is genuinely made with butter rather than margarine or oil, which is exactly why it tastes good and exactly why the saturated fat is where it is.

You can’t have the flavor without it. Add 290 to 330mg of sodium and less than a gram of fiber, and this is a dessert-grade pastry that happens to be shaped like breakfast.

None of that makes it a trap. It makes it a treat, and treating it as one is the whole answer.

How It Compares to the Rest of the Bakery Case
----------------------------------------------

If you’re standing in front of the bakery table deciding, the croissant is a middle-of-the-road choice rather than the indulgent one. Here’s roughly where it sits:

| Costco bakery item | Calories each |
| --- | --- |
| Chocolate chunk cookie | 210 |
| **Butter croissant** | **300** |
| Muffin (corn, low end) | 290 |
| Muffin (richer flavors) | up to 490 |
| Blueberry cheesecake croissant (whole) | 500 |
| Mini carrot cake (whole) | 760 |

A croissant beats every muffin except the lightest one, and it’s less than half a mini carrot cake. If you want the actual lowest-calorie single item, that’s a [chocolate chunk cookie at around 210](https://costcoguides.com/costco-cookies-nutrition/), which surprises people who assume a cookie is worse than a pastry.

The catch is that a croissant’s saturated fat is high relative to its calorie count, because butter is doing all the work. So the croissant wins on calories and loses on saturated fat against a comparable cookie. Pick your metric.

Costco Croissant Price
----------------------

A 12-count package of Kirkland Signature butter croissants runs about **$5.99**, which works out to roughly **50 cents per croissant**. Recent tracking confirms $5.99 as the current price across the large majority of warehouses, [down from a stretch at $6.99](https://app.warehouserunner.com/costco/1199652-butter-croissant-12-count).

That price has bounced between $5.99 and $6.99 over the past couple of years, so don’t be surprised if your warehouse is at either number. Even at the high end, 58 cents a croissant is a price no standalone bakery approaches. This is the single best value in the Costco bakery case, and it’s not close.

The croissants are baked fresh in-store daily, sold in a clear 12-count clamshell, and available year-round rather than seasonally.

The Blueberry Cheesecake Croissant
----------------------------------

Costco’s **Blueberry Caramelized Cheesecake Croissant** is a completely different product and one of the most hyped bakery items the warehouse has run lately.

It’s a six-pack for **$12.99**, roughly $2 each, with a 27-ounce total weight. Croissant dough, cheesecake filling, real blueberries, butter streusel on top, and a caramelized bottom.

![Costco blueberry cheesecake croissants](data:image/svg+xml;base64,PHN2ZyB4bWxucz0iaHR0cDovL3d3dy53My5vcmcvMjAwMC9zdmciIHdpZHRoPSIxMDI0IiBoZWlnaHQ9IjY4MyIgdmlld0JveD0iMCAwIDEwMjQgNjgzIj48cmVjdCB3aWR0aD0iMTAwJSIgaGVpZ2h0PSIxMDAlIiBzdHlsZT0iZmlsbDojY2ZkNGRiO2ZpbGwtb3BhY2l0eTogMC4xOyIvPjwvc3ZnPg==)

Now the part worth reading carefully, because it’s easy to miss. The nutrition label lists **250 calories per serving, but a serving is half a pastry.** Eat a whole one and you’re at roughly **500 calories, 28g of fat, 18g of saturated fat, and 26g of sugar.**

That’s not a knock on the product, it’s a labeling convention Costco uses across several oversized bakery items. But almost nobody eats half a croissant, so the number that matters is the doubled one. One of these is nearly two regular butter croissants’ worth of calories and close to a full day’s saturated fat.

Availability is the other catch. It’s a **rotating seasonal item that sells out fast**, having [disappeared and returned multiple times](https://parade.com/food/costco-blueberry-cheesecake-caramelized-croissant-november-2025), showing up in late 2025 and again in spring 2026, with shoppers reporting it vanishing from warehouses within days.

If you see it, that’s the moment. If your warehouse doesn’t have it, no amount of asking will conjure it.

Reheating tip that shows up repeatedly from people who’ve bought them: a few minutes in the air fryer brings the caramelized bottom back to life.

What’s Actually In Them
-----------------------

The regular butter croissants have a short, recognizable ingredient list: enriched wheat flour, butter, natural flavor, yeast, and then under 2% of sugar, eggs, nonfat dry milk, whole milk powder, wheat gluten, salt, ascorbic acid, and enzymes.

That’s a genuinely clean list for a mass-produced bakery product, no long chain of preservatives or hydrogenated oils. Real butter is the second ingredient.

**Allergens: wheat, milk, and eggs.** Like everything from the Costco bakery, these are produced on shared equipment, so anyone with a severe allergy should treat the entire department as cross-contaminated regardless of what a specific label says. There is no gluten-free or dairy-free croissant option.

Storing and Reheating
---------------------

The best-before date runs about **five days** from purchase, but realistically they start going stale around day three. There are few preservatives holding them together, which is a consequence of that clean ingredient list.

Twelve croissants is more than most households finish fresh, and the fix is the freezer. **They freeze well.** Wrap individually in foil or plastic, bag them, and pull one or two at a time. Frozen, they’ll hold for a couple of months without much loss.

For reheating, the method depends on what you want:

**For flaky**, use an oven, toaster oven, or air fryer for a few minutes. This restores the crisp exterior and is what most people prefer.

**For soft and warm**, microwave for 20 to 30 seconds. It won’t be flaky, but it’ll be pillowy and buttery, which some people actually like more.

Skip the microwave if flakiness is the point. It’s the single most common complaint, and it’s a method problem rather than a product problem.

Is It Worth Buying
------------------

At 50 cents each, yes, with clear eyes about what you’re eating. These are large, genuinely buttery croissants at a fraction of bakery pricing, and they hold up as a base for breakfast sandwiches or lunch sandwiches, which is arguably better value than eating them plain.

Just don’t let the “it’s just a croissant” framing fool you. One croissant is half your day’s saturated fat, and one blueberry cheesecake croissant is most of it.

They’re the best deal in the [Costco bakery lineup](https://costcoguides.com/costco-bakery-menu-prices/) and they’re still dessert. Buy the twelve-pack, freeze eight, and enjoy them for what they are.
