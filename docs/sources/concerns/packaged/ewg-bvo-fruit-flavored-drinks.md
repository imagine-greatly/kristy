# Beware of the chemical BVO in fruit-flavored drinks

![Glass of orange soda](https://www.ewg.org/sites/default/files/styles/wide_standard_xl/public/2023-07/BVOBlog.jpg?h=2e181f1f&itok=Fc7beNqi)

By August, food and drink companies can no longer use the chemical brominated vegetable oil, or BVO, in their products. That’s thanks to an overdue federal ban on BVO as an ingredient, in response to concerns about its potential health harms with long-term exposure.

Some beverage makers aren’t waiting for the August deadline to comply with the Food and Drug Administration’s ban. Popular soft drinks and citrus-flavored energy boosters, like Coca-Cola, Mountain Dew and Gatorade, no longer contain BVO. They previously used the chemical to improve the drinks’ taste, consistency and appearance.

[BVO](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/brominated-vegetable-oil-bvo "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) is a chemical compound and additive that combines vegetable oil, usually soybean or palm, with bromine to prevent oil from separating, stabilizing ingredients and unifying textures in beverages and some food items, like baked goods.

Health risks associated with BVO exposure include irritated skin and mucous membranes. Long-term exposure [harms](https://www.mymlc.com/health-information/faqs/b/brominated-vegetable-oil-why-is-bvo-in-my-drink/#:~:text=Health%20concerns%20about%20BVO%20stem,and%20impaired%20balance%20or%20coordination. "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) the nervous system, causing headaches, fatigue and memory loss.

Because of these concerns, some countries have banned BVO. The United Kingdom’s ban dates back to the 1970s. More than 50 years later, [the FDA finally banned it](https://www.ewg.org/news-insights/news-release/2024/07/fda-finds-brominated-vegetable-oil-no-longer-safe-use-food) in 2024. Companies have until August 2 to remove BVO from their ingredient lists.

**How BVO is used**

You’ve seen bottles of drinks way past their expiration date – they don’t look as fresh as newly purchased products. That's because some beverages settle over time, losing their consistency and carbonation.

BVO let manufacturers affordably prevent oil- and water-based ingredients from separating, which would otherwise give drinks an unappealing, cloudy appearance. Companies like PepsiCo and Coca-Cola used it for this purpose.

Cost-effective and cost-competitive BVO [alternatives](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2024/08/the-fdas-ban-on-brominated-vegetable-oil-what-fb-manufacturers-need-to-know/ "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) exist, and companies have used them in products sold in other countries. But it was still allowed for use in most of the U.S. until last year.

**The health effects of BVO consumption**

BVO exposure has been a source of concern for decades.

[Bromine](https://www.cdc.gov/chemical-emergencies/chemical-fact-sheets/bromine.html "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), a naturally occurring element that is a liquid at room temperature, is harmful when ingested in large amounts. It accumulates in the [body](https://www.cspinet.org/cspi-news/bvo-why-fda-might-finally-ban-brominated-vegetable-oil "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), impacting [fat](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D1E6D88B5E0274351607EF03DB54D72E/S0007114571000370a.pdf/bromine_content_of_human_tissue.pdf "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), the [liver](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0041008X75901805 "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), [heart](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4672864/#:~:text=In%20contrast%2C%20agents%20with%20relatively%20lower%20water%2Dsolubility%2C,tissues%20and%20organs%20such%20as%20the%20heart. "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) and [brain tissue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964729/ "(opens in a new window)")(link is external).

A 2022 [study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278691522003350?via%3Dihub "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) found that when rats are fed an amount of BVO similar to average human consumption, the level of bromine in their bodies negatively affected their hearts, lungs, fat tissue and thyroid hormones.

Moderate consumption of BVO is less likely to cause severe health harms. But there are concerns over its potential long-term effects.

**Regulating BVO**

In the late 1950s, the FDA categorized BVO as [generally recognized as safe](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-ingredients-packaging/generally-recognized-safe-gras "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), or [GRAS](https://www.federalregister.gov/citation/35-FR-1049 "(opens in a new window)")(link is external). GRAS is a regulatory loophole that lets food and beverage companies add new chemicals to their products without undergoing the FDA’s safety review process.

Congress intended the GRAS designation to apply only to ingredients widely recognized as safe, such as salt or vinegar.

The FDA’s margin of safety for BVO in processed foods and beverages was [15 parts per million](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-additives-petitions/brominated-vegetable-oil-bvo#:~:text=BVO%20is%20a%20vegetable%20oil,from%20floating%20to%20the%20top. "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), meaning products couldn’t contain more than that. But continued research revealed that BVO’s toxicity was more harmful to human health than once believed.

As new information became available, BVO use in manufacturing products declined.

The [U.K.](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/importing-drinks "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) banned BVO as an ingredient in the 1970s, followed by [India](https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FOOD_ADDITVES.pdf "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) in 1990, the [European Union](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/E-8-2015-013572_EN.html "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) in 2008 and Japan in 2010. Meanwhile, the FDA continued to permit the use of BVO in fruit-flavored drinks.

**From petition to regulation**

A Mississippi teenager’s [petition](https://www.change.org/p/powerade-if-gatorade-can-take-crazy-chemical-bvo-out-of-sports-drinks-so-can-you "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) in 2013 sparked market pressure to remove BVO from beverages.

In the face of this public sentiment, PepsiCo began to produce Gatorade without BVO. [Coca-Cola](https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2014/05/coke-to-ban-bvo/361747/#:~:text=of%20our%20partner%20.-,Coca%2DCola%20said%20on%20Monday%20that%20it%20will%20stop%20using,petition%20started%20by%20a%20teenager.&text=Some%20beverage%20manufacturers%20are%20even%20considering%20taking%20BVO%20out%20of%20their%20products. "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) soon followed suit.

A decade later, with the 2023 [California Food Safety Act](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB418 "(opens in a new window)")(link is external), the Golden State became the first in the nation to ban BVO. One year later, the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-revokes-regulation-allowing-use-brominated-vegetable-oil-bvo-food "(opens in a new window)")(link is external) [rolled back](https://www.ewg.org/news-insights/news-release/2024/07/fda-finds-brominated-vegetable-oil-no-longer-safe-use-food) its 1970 rule allowing BVO in food, setting the deadline for August to comply.

Today, companies have phased BVO out of many processed beverages, but you may still find products on store shelves that were manufactured before the ban.

Before then – and for some time after – make sure to study ingredient lists so you know whether a drink contains BVO.
