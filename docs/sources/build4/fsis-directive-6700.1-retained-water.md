Retained Water Verification - Revision 3
FSIS directive
6700.1
Series Type
6000 Series: Slaughter Inspection
Issue Date
Mar 06, 2026
Full Directive
6700.1.pdf
PURPOSE
FSIS is revising this directive to announce changes in the retained water protocol (RWP) review and approval process. As of this issuance, the Office of Policy and Program Development (OPPD) will review and approve RWPs to help finalize protocols and related data prior to the July 1, 2026, deadline for making any necessary label changes. The Office of Field Operations will continue retained water verification activities under the General Labeling task, but instead of reviewing RWPs, Inspection program personnel (IPP) are to verify that the RWP is reflective of the establishment's products and processes. IPP are to continue to document noncompliances as stated in paragraph
V. E.
This directive provides instructions to IPP for performing required verification activities for retained water in raw meat and poultry products. IPP are to inform the establishment that new and revised protocols are to be submitted through
askFSIS
.
This directive does not currently address labeling verification. IPP are not to verify labeling declarations until July 1, 2026. FSIS will reissue this directive with instructions for verifying labeling declarations prior to July 1, 2026.
NOTE
: If establishment's products do not retain water, the establishment is not required to have a retained water protocol, but they must have documentation on file to support that their process does not result in retained water in their products, as described in Section
V. C. 2
.
CANCELLATION
FSIS Directive 6700.1, Rev. 2,
Retained Water in Raw Meat and Poultry Products
, dated 11/26/25.
BACKGROUND
Raw, single-ingredient meat and poultry products that retain water from post-evisceration processing in excess of naturally occurring moisture are subject to the retained water regulations (
9 CFR 441.10
) and are to declare this retained water on the label (
9 CFR 441.10(b)
). Any establishment whose products retain water are to maintain a written RWP on file and make it available for IPP review (
9 CFR 441.10 (c)(1)
). Retained water requirements are consumer protection standards and prevent economic adulteration.
FSIS published a revised
FSIS Guideline for Retained Water
in January 2025, as announced in
90 FR 5809
. This guideline clarifies FSIS' expectations for RWPs and provides correct formulas for calculating retained water based on weights and on laboratory methods.
POST-EVISCERATION PROCESS
The following post-evisceration processes result in retained water that needs to be declared on the label:
Carcass washing with or without an antimicrobial;
Spray chilling carcasses, parts, trimmings, byproducts, bones, or other edible parts with or without an antimicrobial;
Water or ice chilling with or without an antimicrobial;
Postchill spraying with or without an antimicrobial; and
Application of any aqueous antimicrobial or processing aid.
The following post-evisceration processes do not result in retained water that needs to be declared on the label:
Scalding or flushing gastrointestinal organs to remove digestive tract contents;
Flushing livestock heads to remove digestive tract contents; and
Washing parts (i.e., to remove excess blood).
IPP are to be aware of which products are subject to
9 CFR 441.10
:
Raw, single-ingredient meat and poultry products;
Kosher products. Koshering is the process of soaking and salting product. Although Kosher products are labeled as "soaked and salted," they are not considered multi-ingredient products. Any water absorbed from koshering is not declared as retained water; and koshering steps are not included in the written RWP. However, IPP are to verify that any water retained before or after koshering, such as chilling, is declared;
Further processed product when the source material retained water.
Ground product made with source materials that retained water during processing are to also declare the retained water, unless the establishment has data to support that the product has a net loss of retained water; and
Product made with multiple source materials, each with a different retained water amount, are to declare the highest retained water percentage found in the source materials.
Giblets packaged separately or with a whole single-ingredient poultry carcass. A whole carcass with giblets is a single-ingredient product. If the giblets retain a different amount of water than the rest of the carcass, then the establishment may label for the highest amount or label for each individual product within the package;
Ritually slaughtered product that bears the mark of inspection;
Product chilled in saltwater containing 70 pounds or less of salt per 10,000 gallons of water;
Chitterlings packaged without purge; and
Ice-glazed poultry.
IPP are to be aware which products are not subject to
9 CFR 441.10
:
Multi-ingredient products.
Raw meat or poultry products used as ingredients in multi-ingredient products, e.g., raw or cooked sausage, deli meats;
Meat or poultry product with added solutions (added for flavoring or reasons other than addressing a food safety requirement), as these products are subject to different regulations (
9 CFR 317.2(e)(2)
for meat;
9 CFR 381.117(h)
for poultry). This includes basted, marinated, injected, or tumbled products, including giblets in a basted carcass; and
Product chilled in saltwater containing more than 70 to 700 pounds of salt per 10,000 gallons of water (the water and salt are to be declared on the label as ingredients).
Religious exempt product that does not bear the mark of inspection.
Chitterlings packaged with no more than 20 percent purge.
Standards of identity or composition are not affected by retained water.
Retained water in the source material is not included in formulation calculations when considering if water limits set by the standard are exceeded. For example, most uncooked sausages (
9 CFR 319.141-145
) limit water added as an ingredient to 3 percent. Any retained water in the source material would not count against that 3 percent limit. Water limits for standardized products that allow for the use of water during processing are specific to water added as an ingredient, not retained water.
Retained water in the source material is not considered a violation of the standard of identity for products that prohibit the direct addition of water during processing, such as ground beef or hamburger (
9 CFR 319.15(a) and (b)
) or dry cured products (
9 CFR 319.106
).
Retained water has no effect on subsequently cooked product or its labeling, as retained water only applies to raw products.
VERIFICATION PROCEDURES
IPP are to verify compliance with
9 CFR 441.10
at least quarterly under the routine PHIS General Labeling task in each establishment that produces raw, single-ingredient meat or poultry products.
Once during January through March;
Once during April through June;
Once during July through September; and
Once during October through December.
When performing a routine General Labeling task to verify retained water compliance, IPP are to:
Verify that the establishment does not apply aqueous solutions to its products; or
Verify that the establishment maintains data on file to support the conclusion that its products do not retain water; or
Verify that the establishment maintains its written RWP on file (
9 CFR 441.10(c)(1)
), that the establishment follows its RWP, that the analyzed data supports the retained water declaration, and that ongoing verification demonstrates retained water process control. Verify that an individual package does not exceed the retained water declaration by more than 20 percent.
EXAMPLE:
A beef slaughter establishment sprays carcasses with an antimicrobial solution before the carcasses go into the cooler. The establishment has data on file showing that the post-chill carcass weights are lower than the pre-intervention weights. These data support that the process does not retain water, and therefore the establishment does not need to develop an RWP or include a retained water statement on their label.
IPP are to verify the following requirements:
The establishment has an approved RWP available for IPP review, to include the data, analysis, and conclusion elements.
The establishment's follows the RWP as written and it addresses all the steps in the process (i.e., that the post-evisceration sampling location is after evisceration and before any water applications; that the pre-pack sampling location is after all water applications and prior to packaging; and that the establishment is not adding or removing steps from its normal process).
The establishment's RWP accounts for all the species and products the establishment produces (e.g., carcasses, parts, giblets) and that the data collection is representative of all the processes (e.g., washes, interventions, chilling, water knives, overflow sitting on ice).
IPP are to consult
askFSIS
if they have questions. IPP are to inform the establishment that new or revised RWPs are to be submitted through
askFSIS
for OPPD review.
IPP are to document an NR when:
The establishment adds water to its process, but it either does not have data to support that the products do not retain water or does not have an RWP (
9 CFR 441.10(a)
);
The establishment is missing any information in its RWP (
9 CFR 441.10(d)
);
The establishment is not following the written RWP (
9 CFR 441.10
); or
The establishment makes changes to its process and fails to evaluate and, if necessary, modify its RWP.
QUESTIONS
