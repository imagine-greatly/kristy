As a practical matter all not seasonally adjusted (NSA) CPI series are estimated as the aggregation of a set of basics. Table 1: CPI aggregate items shows the basic items which compose each aggregate item. Note that this is not necessarily the case for seasonally adjusted aggregate CPI series. For more information, see the
CPI seasonal item aggregation page
.
While all NSA CPI series are built from the bottom up through the aggregation of basic indexes, it is also true that some aggregates are subsets of other aggregates, resulting in a hierarchy called the CPI Item Aggregation Tree, which can be helpful in visualizing the relationships between different CPI series.

CPI items can be divided into several levels of complete and mutually exclusive categories which span different levels of aggregation:
Entry level items (ELIs) are the most granular, complete, and mutually exclusive breakdown of CPI items. ELIs are used internally for sampling, data collection, database organization purposes, and as basic items in the case of health insurance. They have five-character codes which illustrate which item stratum, expenditure class, and major group they belong to, as described below. More information about ELIs is available in
CPI Handbook of Methods Appendix 2: Content of CPI Entry Level Items
.
The 211 item strata are groups of one or more ELIs which cover related items and are the most granular complete and mutually exclusive breakdown of CPI items for which data is publicly available. For most items, this level is the basic level used for aggregation. These series have six-character codes of which the last four match the first four characters of the ELIs within them. For example, the item stratum
other beverage materials including tea
(SEFP02) is composed of two ELIs:
tea
(FP021) and
other beverage materials
(FP022).
The 70 expenditure classes (ECs) are groups of one or more item strata which cover related items. These series have four-letter codes which match the first four characters of item strata within them. For example, the EC
beverage materials including coffee and tea
(SEFP) is composed of two item strata:
coffee
(SEFP01) and
other beverage materials including tea
(SEFP02).
The eight major groups:
apparel
(SAA),
education and communication
(SAE),
food and beverages
(SAF),
housing
(SAH),
medical care
(SAM),
other goods and services
(SAG),
recreation
(SAR), and
transportation
(SAT). Major groups are composed all of the ECs which share the third character of the item code – so
beverage materials including coffee and tea
(SEFP) would be contained in
food and beverages
(SAF).
All items
(SA0) and
all items (old base)
(AA0) are the top-level series which combine all basic items. (Or, alternatively, the combination of all series of any level which is complete and mutually exclusive in its coverage of basic items, like ECs or major groups.)
Note that the number of basic items (243) used for the calculation of aggregate indexes is larger than the number of item strata (211) because the entry level item (ELI) level is used for the calculation of basic indexes for health insurance retained earnings (item code SEME) rather than the item stratum level.

In addition, there are also a number of CPI series which lie outside of this aggregation hierarchy. Broadly, these series fall into two categories:
Special aggregates are series which represent a group of items which may be of interest to certain users, but which are not grouped together in expenditure classes or major groups, such as
food at home
(SAF1) or
energy
(SA0E). Other special aggregate series exclude items which are included in other, similar aggregates – a prominent example of this is
all items less food and energy
(SA0L1E). Special aggregate series usually have codes which begin with SA, but this is not always the case. Exceptions to this rule
include information technology commodities
(SEEEC),
video and audio products
(SERAC), and
video and audio services
(SERAS).
Special relative calculations (SRCs) are series which represent a subset of an ELI, where that subset is of interest to users. SRCs have codes which begin with SS. Examples include
bacon and related products
(SS04011) and
rice
(SS01031).
