# Group C research: seafood and pantry label vocabulary

Date: 2026-10-29. Read/fetch only. Brands are research sources, never named in the shopper app; the deliverable is the label vocabulary.

Method and honesty notes:
- "Fetched" means the page text was returned by scrapling this run. Anything marked "discovery only" came from a search-result highlight and was NOT read in full; treat it as unverified.
- Not fetched (failed, empty, or 404): MSC standard page, Regenerative Organic Alliance pages, Non-GMO Project, USDA organic (7 CFR 205), True Source Certified site, Anson Mills, Vital Choice product pages ("not available"), Patagonia Provisions ("System maintenance"), Cornucopia scorecards (redirected, empty), Bird Friendly toolkit PDF (came back as binary, unreadable), Weston A. Price guide, Real Organic Project, and the maple grading regulation. Claims that depend on them are listed under "Unverified".
- Classification key: THIRD-PARTY (audited certification, certifier named), GOV (government-regulated term), MKTG (unregulated marketing).
- No health claims are made here.

Only three brands could be read; none printed a vessel or a catch date in fetched text.

Find on the pack, ranked:
1. MSC blue-fish seal (third-party audited wild fishery).
2. "Wild" with a named place of catch (a specific fishery/region such as Alaska, not just "USA").
3. Species named (sockeye, coho, pink, king) not just "salmon".
4. Ingredients: salmon (and salt) only; skin-on and bone-in for canned.
5. Method stated (e.g. troll, gillnet, purse seine) and no FADs for canned.

Claims that mean little: "sustainably sourced" without a seal; "sashimi-grade"; "premium"; "responsibly harvested"; "no antibiotics/GMOs" on a wild fish (nothing is given to it); a brand's own mercury-test number (own protocol, not audited, in fetched text).

Regenerative: none appears on salmon.

## 3. Shrimp

Independent source fetched: Seafood Watch shrimp guide, https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/how-to-choose-sustainable-seafood/sustainable-seafood-guides/shrimp-buying-guide. Verbatim: "Find out the type of shrimp, where it's from, and how it was caught or farmed. | Brand | Independent rating source | Verbatim label claims | Seals | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premier Catch (https://www.premiercatch.com/products/wild-gulf-shrimp) | Seafood Watch category guidance (Gulf shrimp mostly yellow) | "Wild-caught off the Louisiana coast and processed in the USA"; "All natural, with no additives, preservatives or sulfites"; "Individually frozen"; "Ingredients: Gulf shrimp, water"; "Sustainably harvested from the Gulf waters off Louisiana"; "Premier Catch offers only wild-caught seafood sourced from U.S. fisheries, never farmed." | None on the fetched page; no vessel named | Origin: specific state coast, useful; "all natural": MKTG; "sustainably harvested": MKTG; "no sulfites" is an ingredient fact, checkable on the list |

Only one shrimp brand was read in full.

Find on the pack, ranked:
1. Wild vs farmed stated, plus a named region (U.S. Pacific, BC, or Gulf/Atlantic coast).

4. Fishing method named.
5. Not "Product of Mexico" without a rating check.

Claims that mean little: "sustainably harvested"; "all natural"; "premium"; "jumbo" (a size, not quality).

Regenerative: none.

## 4. Olive oil

Independent sources fetched:
- COOC, https://cooc.com/about-the-seal/: "International standards as well as COOC regulations require that olive oil meets both chemical and sensory standards to be sold as extra virgin."; "No chemicals or extreme heat may be used during the extraction process."; panel checks the oil "has fruitiness and is free of defects."; "not milled within 24 hours of being harvested, the oil will not be top quality"; "Milling at a high temperature (over 86°F) can also negatively impact flavor and quality."; "Look for the California Olive Oil Council's Seal with the most recent harvest year to ensure you are purchasing certified extra virgin olive oil from California."
- UC Davis, https://ucfoodquality.ucdavis.edu/olive-oil/olive-oil-background: the first report "found 69 percent of the imported oils (14 brands) sampled, compared with 10 percent of the California-produced oils (five brands) sampled, failed to meet internationally accepted standards for extra virgin olive oil."; second report "73 percent (66 of 90 samples) of the five top-selling imported brands failed international sensory standards"; OOCC standards are "mandatory for California olive oil producers processing more than 5,000 gallons of olive oil per year."

| Brand | Independent rating source | Verbatim label claims | Seals | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cobram Estate (https://cobramestate.com/select-quality-olive-oil/) | UC Davis and COOC standards, which the brand cites | "Cobram Estate groves are all located in the in Sacramento Vally in Northern California."; "Cobram Estate's single harvest date ensures freshness"; "picked at peak ripeness and processed in under 6-hours"; "store all oil in nitrogen capped tanks"; "maintains acidity levels below 0.5%"; "follows the strict standards of the COOC and the OOOC"; "Always choose EVOO in dark-tinted glass bottles to reduce exposure to light. Avoid clear glass bottles and plastics." | COOC seal and OOCC standard referenced | COOC seal: THIRD-PARTY (chemistry plus blind sensory panel); OOCC: GOV-like state commission rule for large producers; harvest date, acidity: checkable facts; "peak ripeness": MKTG |

The COOC chemistry table (FFA, peroxide value, K232, K270) was found by search highlight only: unverified.

Find on the pack, ranked:
1. "Extra virgin" plus the COOC seal (or another named third-party seal) with the most recent harvest year.
2. Harvest date or harvest year (not "best by" alone).
3. One named country or region, ideally one estate or one named mill.
4. Dark glass (or tin).
5. Lab results or a lot code you can look up.

Claims that mean little: "light" or "pure" (the brand page itself calls "light" essentially a marketing term); "first cold pressed" alone; "imported from Italy" (bottled vs grown); "Product of" a blend of countries; clear bottles.

Regenerative: none appears.
