# Group B: beef, lamb/bison, milk/dairy, butter/ghee (label research, 2026-10-29)

Purpose: label vocabulary for the Kristy shopper app. Brands are the research source only; the app never names them. Every URL below was fetched in this run unless marked "not fetched". No health or treatment claims are made here.

Classification key: **CERT** = third-party audited certification. **GOV** = government-regulated term. **MKT** = unregulated marketing wording (including a brand's own seal or standard).

## Cross-cutting findings

- **USDA Organic does not mean grass-finished.** 7 CFR 205.239 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/205.239): ruminants need "management on pasture and daily grazing throughout the grazing season"; dairy needs "an average of at least 30 percent dry matter intake (DMI) from grazing throughout the grazing season"; slaughter stock may be finished in a feedlot ("The finishing period shall not exceed one-fifth (1/5) of the animal's total life or 120 days, whichever is shorter"); "Continuous total confinement of ruminants in yards, feeding pads, and feedlots is prohibited."
- **Bare "grass-fed" is not a certified claim.** Alderspring's own FAQ (https://www.alderspring.com/): "Simply 'grass fed' can often mean that the animal was raised on grass but finished on grain." and "always look for that 'grass fed and finished' or '100% grass fed.'" Force of Nature's FAQ (https://forceofnature.com/pages/faq) notes "grass" can mean hay fed in a feedlot.
- **Certified grass-fed needs a named certifier.** American Grassfed Association (https://www.americangrassfed.org/about-us/our-standards/): inspection at least every 15 months, no grain, no antibiotics or hormones. A Greener World (https://agreenerworld.org/): "The only grassfed welfare label. Guarantees 100 percent grass and forage diet on pasture or range, and high-welfare management." and "The only non-GMO welfare label."
- **Regenerative claims differ widely.** Bare "regenerative" is MKT. Regenerative Organic Certified (https://regenorganic.org/) sits on a USDA/NOP organic base with Bronze/Silver/Gold levels (548 certified farms/ranches shown). Certified Regenerative by AGW (https://agreenerworld.org/) is described as "Measuring real change in partnership to benefit soil, water, air, biodiversity, infrastructure, animal welfare and social responsibility." Land to Market (seen on Alexandre, below): "Outcomes-based, empirical verification of regeneration by measuring soil health, ground cover, water retention, biodiversity and soil carbon."
- **A badge does not equal a top independent dairy rating.** Cornucopia scorecard (https://www.cornucopia.org/scorecard/dairy/, dated Oct 23, 2026) rates the same company's EcoDairy line 1 star and its 100% Grassfed line 2 stars, and Organic Valley Grassmilk 2 stars. Top-rated dairy there is small, pasture-forward family farms.
- **Eatwild** (https://eatwild.com/find-farms.php): a directory of 623 farms with filters (Animal Welfare Approved, American Grassfed, USDA Organic and others); it rates no brands.

### Find on the pack (beef), ranked by how much it separates top from ordinary
1. A named grass-fed certifier seal (American Grassfed Association, Pasture Raised/PCO, or Certified Grassfed by A Greener World). Only this makes "grass-fed" audited.
2. "100% grass-fed AND grass-finished" (or "grass fed & finished") wording together with that seal. Bare "grass-fed" does not tell finishing.
3. USDA Organic seal, as a bonus. It does not guarantee grass finishing (up to 120 days or one fifth of life in a feedlot is allowed).
4. Real Organic Project label (higher than USDA Organic per the brand; certifier site not fetched) or an A Greener World welfare seal.
5. A named ranch or rancher and a regenerative claim tied to a certification (ROC or Certified Regenerative by AGW), not the word alone.

### Sounds good, means little (beef)
"Natural"; bare "grass-fed"; bare "regenerative"; "pasture-raised" with no certifier; "No hormones" (not allowed in any beef raising, so it differentiates little; not verified in this run); a brand's own "Standard" or "Promise" seal; "tested free from 300 chemicals" with no lab or report named; "raised organically" without the USDA Organic seal.

Lamb and bison are mostly unregulated in the US for "grass-fed" claims; the same certifier seals (AGA, PCO, AGW) are the audited route. Lamb-specific seal coverage by those certifiers was not verified in this run.

### Find on the pack (lamb/bison)
1. A named certifier seal (AGA, PCO, or Certified Grassfed by AGW) on the lamb or bison pack. None was found for the bison brands fetched.
2. "100% grass-fed and finished" / "lifetime grazed" wording.
3. USDA Organic seal (lamb).
4. Named ranch and region, with a stated rotational or regenerative grazing practice.
5. "No feedlots" stated alongside the grass-finished claim.

### Sounds good, means little
"Natural"; bare "grass-fed"; "Organically raised" without the seal; "Field harvested" (a method, not a certification); "As Nature Intended"; "Open Gate Certified" until a certifier is shown.

Cornucopia tiers (https://www.cornucopia.org/scorecard/dairy/): 5-star = "gold standard"; "diverse, small-to-medium-scale family farms. They emphasize well-managed pasture. Pasture and other forage makes up the majority of their animals' feed."; 4-star = "ample pasture... credible effort to encourage natural behaviors"; 3-star = "may have failed to answer some or all of our questions"; 2-star = "industrial-scale operations or others with outstanding questions and a lack of transparency"; 1-star = "suspected of being, or sourcing from, what we would describe as 'factory farms'". Many store brands score 10, including organic-labelled store lines and a kefir brand. 5-star examples with fluid milk: Paradise Springs, Working Cows Dairy, Hawthorne Valley Farm, Larson Farm & Creamery, Traders Point Creamery.

### Find on the pack (milk, kefir, yogurt)
1. USDA Organic seal (required on-farm grazing of at least 30 percent dry matter intake in the grazing season; no confinement-only).
2. "100% grass-fed" or "grassmilk" with a named certifier (AGA, PCO, or AGW), not the phrase alone.
3. A named small farm or farmstead on the pack, not a national brand with a generic organic line. Independent scorecards put small pasture-forward farms on top.
4. Plain, unflavored, cultured product with a short ingredient list (milk and cultures).
5. Regenerative claim only with a certification named (ROC, Certified Regenerative by AGW, or Land to Market).

### Sounds good, means little (dairy)
"Natural"; "happy cows"; "pasture raised" with no certifier; bare "regenerative"; bare "grass-fed"; "no added hormones" (a USDA-organic baseline, so little separation among organic lines); a brand's own seal (EcoDairy) or promise; "A2/A2" (a milk-protein trait, not a pasture or welfare measure); an organic seal alone, since independent ratings span 1 to 5 stars among organic brands.

1. USDA Organic seal.
2. A named small farm or creamery, farmstead where stated, with the milk source on the pack.

### Sounds good, means little

### Find on the pack (butter, ghee)
1. USDA Organic seal.
2. Grass-fed or pasture claim with a named certifier (AGA, PCO, or AGW) on the butter.
3. Named small creamery or farm and a dairy source stated; for ghee, the source butter stated.
4. Short ingredient list: cream (and salt) for butter; butter only for ghee. Cultured is a bonus.
5. Independent rating (e.g. top-tier scorecard) cannot be on a pack, so look for the farm-specific wording instead of a national brand's grass-fed headline.

### Sounds good, means little
"Made with milk from grass-fed cows" with no certifier (the claim describes the milk source, not an audited standard); "happy cows"; "ethically sourced"; "traditional"; "kettle-cooked"; "pasture raised" with no certifier; "natural".
