# Regenerative labels on US grocery packs

Research pass 2026-10-29. Read and fetch only. Nothing here is Kristy copy; the app names seals and certifiers as label terms and never names brands.

Source tags: **[F]** page fetched this run. **[S]** seen only as a search excerpt this run (not fetched; treat as unverified until fetched). **[NF]** not fetched or unreadable. Anything with no tag is the researcher's synthesis.

## 1. The ground truth

- No US federal definition. CRS (July 2025) [F]: neither "regenerative agriculture" nor "regenerative organic agriculture" is codified, and the principles are not enforceable under USDA or FDA programs.
- On meat, "regeneratively raised" is an environment-related claim reviewed by USDA FSIS. FSIS guideline [S] lists "Regeneratively Raised" and "Raised Using Regenerative Agriculture Practices" as examples and says: "FSIS has not defined specific environment-related claims in the regulations or in this guideline." It approves the claim only if the label names the entity that set the standard (or a website that states it), and for a third-party certified claim the label must carry the certifier's name, website and logo. It "strongly encourages" third-party certification. Documentation is a producer file, not an on-farm audit by FSIS.
- EWG (22 Sep 2025) [F]: "Any food package or food advertising can include regenerative claims." EWG also says the USDA allows "regeneratively raised" as a voluntary marketing claim on meat and dairy without third-party verifier visits.
- Three splits that matter on a pack:
  1. Does the seal sit on top of USDA Organic (ROC, Demeter, Real Organic) or stand apart from it (AGW Certified Regenerative, Land to Market, Regenified)?
  2. Practice-based (what the farmer does) or outcome-based (what the land shows)?
  3. Is there an on-farm audit, and are results public?
- Separate claims that get confused (grassfed.directory [F]): grass-fed is what the animal eats, pasture-raised is where it lives, regenerative is how the land is managed. A regenerative farm may still feed grain to pigs and chickens.

## 2. Seal by seal

### Regenerative Organic Certified (ROC)
- **Runs it:** Regenerative Organic Alliance, a 501(c)3 nonprofit [F regenorganic.org].
- **Audited:** Yes. Annual recertification audits at all levels; third-party auditors audit the requirements beyond existing certifications [S, ROC Framework v4.1].
- **Requires:** Three pillars: Soil Health & Land Management, Animal Welfare, Farmer & Worker Fairness [F site, S framework]. USDA Organic (or equivalent) certification is a baseline: "Participation at any level of Regenerative Organic Certified® requires that the entity is properly certified as organic by a NOP-accredited certifying body" [S]. Soil lab tests at application then every three years, in-field tests each audit [S]. CRS calls it "USDA Organic Plus" [F].
- **Levels:** Bronze, Silver, Gold. Bronze needs at least 10% of the operation's land (or revenue) certified at start, 50% by year five; Silver 50% rising to 75%; Gold 100% [S]. So a pack's level matters, and a Bronze pack can come from a farm that is mostly not yet certified.
- **Covers:** Farms and ranches, transport, slaughter, certain processing [S]. Crops, meat, dairy, eggs, fiber. Site counter [F]: 548 farms and ranches, 388 brands, 3,215 products, 22.3 million acres.
- **On pack:** A seal in Bronze, Silver or Gold, or black or white with the level written; "No other color variations are allowed" [F]. Labels need Alliance approval 30 days before print [F]. Logo artwork itself not viewed (unverified).
- **Criticism:** EWG [F] groups it with Organic and Demeter as banning synthetic fertilizer and toxic pesticides, with routine audits. Cost and paperwork burden for small farms is a common point (not verified from a fetched source). Scale figures differ between the Alliance site and Ecovia (soilpractice [F]).

### Demeter / Biodynamic
- **Runs it:** Demeter USA, "the only certifier for Biodynamic farms and products in the United States"; founded in Germany in 1928 [F demeter-usa.org].
- **Audited:** Yes, certifier inspected; EWG [F] lists it with Organic and ROC as barring synthetic fertilizer and toxic pesticides with routine audits.
- **Requires:** Whole-farm biodynamic practice that builds on organic. Demeter's own language is spiritual and cosmic ("vitality" claims); Kristy should state only the practice and never repeat that language.
- **Covers:** Crops, dairy, meat, eggs, wine and processed goods.
- **On pack:** Demeter logo (or "Biodynamic" wording). Artwork not viewed (unverified).
- **Criticism:** The cosmological content is the common objection from scientific sources (general knowledge, not from a fetched page this run).

### Real Organic Project (add-on)
- **Runs it:** Real Organic Project, "farmer-led" [F realorganicproject.org]. Free to farms [F]. Since 2018, 1,100 farms joined [F].
- **Audited:** Yes, an inspection by an approved organic certifier plus its own review [S, standards page].
- **Requires:** Already certified USDA Organic ("only certify farms that are already USDA certified organic") [S]. Soil-grown, no hydroponics; container production 1% or less of gross income; no systemic confinement of livestock; no parallel production (same crop or animal both organic and non-organic); 51% of farm sales from organic [S, apply page]. Pasture-based livestock.
- **Covers:** Produce, meat, dairy, eggs. Grain not specifically verified.
- **On pack:** "Real Organic Project" label next to the USDA Organic seal. Artwork not viewed (unverified).
- **Criticism:** It is an organic-integrity label, not a soil-outcome label; it does not itself measure soil carbon. It sued USDA over hydroponics; the court rejected the suit [F]. Not a "regenerative" word on the label, but its criteria overlap with regenerative practice.

### Certified Regenerative by A Greener World (AGW)
- **Runs it:** A Greener World (AGW); accredited by IOAS, registration number 83 [F agreenerworld.org].
- **Audited:** Yes. "AGW's independent trained auditors visit every farm in the program on an ongoing basis"; whole-farm plan reviewed by an expert panel; "measured and audited on an ongoing basis" [F].
- **Requires:** Planned practices so that "over time the soil, water, air and biodiversity are improved or maintained to the greatest extent possible" [F]. Animal side: "the only label to require audited, high-welfare production, transport and slaughter practices" [F, AGW's own claim]. Annual soil tests and approved plan [F grassfed.directory].
- **Organic:** Not required. It gives "early access to regenerative markets without being certified organic" [F]. So synthetic inputs are limited rather than banned outright; EWG [F] nonetheless puts AGW among labels that ban or limit synthetics, are practice- and outcome-based, and require on-farm audits.
- **Covers:** Meat, dairy, eggs, crops (AGW also runs Animal Welfare Approved).
- **On pack:** AGW "Certified Regenerative" mark. Artwork not viewed (unverified).
- **Criticism:** Newer and smaller than organic; less recognized by shoppers (only 24% of consumers had heard of regenerative agriculture, Consumer Reports survey cited by soilpractice [F]).

### Land to Market / Ecological Outcome Verification (Savory Institute)
- **Runs it:** Savory Institute; EOV is "an outcome-based monitoring protocol for grassland environments", deployed on over 6 million acres [F savory.global/eov].
- **Audited:** Monitoring by a trained monitor. Annually: aboveground indicators such as bare ground and biodiversity. Every five years: plant diversity, water infiltration, soil lab data (soil carbon, water-holding capacity). Feeds an Ecological Health Index [F]. Verified products "may apply to carry the coveted Land to Market Verified seal" [F]. Chain of Custody process [F landtomarket.com].
- **Requires:** An outcome (land shows regeneration), not a list of practices: "Rather than certifying management practices, EOV goes directly to the source" [F]. Not an organic label; EWG [F] says Land to Market can also be grown with synthetic fertilizers and pesticides, and that audit and test results are not public.
- **Covers:** Grazing lands only: beef, lamb, bison, goat, some wool and dairy. Not produce or grain.
- **On pack:** "Land to Market Verified" seal. Artwork not viewed (unverified). The brand directory is "coming soon" [F], so a shopper cannot yet look a product up.
- **Criticism:** EWG [F] (no public results, synthetics not barred). It does not verify animal welfare or feed on its own (inference from scope; not stated by a fetched source).

### Regenified
- **Runs it:** Regenified [F regenified.com]; "6-3-4 Verification Standard ... based on measurement of practices and outcomes" [F].
- **Audited:** Verification with measurement; EWG [F] says audit and test results are not public.
- **Requires:** "The Certified Regenified Mark says you are holding a product that comes from a farm that commits to regenerative practices and principles" [F]. Organic not required; EWG [F] says synthetics can be used. Civil Eats reported no requirement to cut synthetic fertilizer or pesticides (secondary via soilpractice [F]).
- **Covers:** Beef, chicken, pork, lamb, eggs, grains, produce and more [F].
- **On pack:** Regenified mark. Artwork not viewed (unverified).
- **Criticism:** EWG and Civil Eats points above.

### USDA Organic (the floor)
- **Runs it:** USDA National Organic Program; accredited certifiers inspect annually.
- **Requires (text fetched, Cornell LII):** 7 CFR 205.200: production must "maintain or improve the natural resources of the operation" [F]. 205.239: dairy animals must average "at least 30 percent dry matter intake (DMI) from grazing throughout the grazing season"; ruminant slaughter stock "shall be maintained on pasture for each day that the finishing period corresponds with the grazing season" [F]. No synthetic fertilizer or toxic pesticides (EWG [F]).
- **Covers:** Everything. It is the base for ROC, Demeter and Real Organic.
- **Criticism:** It does not require soil outcomes or rotation beyond the rule text; Real Organic and ROC exist because their founders say it falls short (their own claims).

### The bare word "regenerative" / "regeneratively raised"
- **Runs it:** Whoever prints it. FSIS reviews the label wording and substantiation file [S], but a producer-defined claim has no outside audit unless a certifier is named.
- **Tell:** a real claim names who defined it and a website (an asterisk to the definition) [S]. A bare word with no named standard is marketing.
- **Criticism:** EWG [F]; Center for Biological Diversity told FSIS the term lacks any consistent industry definition [S].

## 3. Ranked ladder (strongest to weakest)

Ranked on: audited on farm, synthetic inputs barred, welfare and soil both covered, public standard. This is the researcher's ordering for Devon's approval, not a sourced ranking.

| # | Rung | What the seal means (plain, no health claim) | Sits next to organic |
|---|------|------|------|
| 1 | Regenerative Organic Certified, Gold (then Silver, Bronze) | Organic farm audited for soil building, animal welfare and fair labor, at a stated level. | Stacks on organic (organic is required). |
| 2 | Real Organic Project | Organic farm that grows in real soil and keeps animals on pasture, never confined. | Stacks on organic (organic is required). |
| 2 | Demeter / Biodynamic | Organic-style whole-farm system run to a biodynamic standard. | Builds on organic; Demeter is its own certification. |
| 3 | Certified Regenerative (A Greener World) | Farm audited against a plan to keep improving soil and water, with high-welfare animal care. | Does not need organic. |
| 4 | Land to Market Verified | The grazing land itself tested and showing recovery over time. | Does not need organic. |
| 5 | Regenified | Farm measured against a stated set of regenerative practices and results. | Does not need organic. |
| 6 | USDA Organic alone | No synthetic fertilizer or toxic pesticides; animals on pasture in the grazing season. | Is the floor. Not itself a regenerative seal. |
| 7 | "Regeneratively raised" with a named standard and website | The brand's own definition, written on the pack. | Independent of organic. |
| 8 | Bare "regenerative" | No stated standard. Treat as unverified. | Independent of organic. |

Note on placement of rung 6: organic alone sits below the independent regenerative seals only for the soil claim; on synthetic inputs it is stricter than rungs 3 to 5. Devon to decide whether rungs 3 to 5 outrank plain organic or rank beside it. EWG [F] places Organic, ROC and Demeter in the "may not use synthetic" group and AGW as limiting them, with Land to Market and Regenified permitting them; that argues for AGW above, and L2M and Regenified at or below, organic for a shopper who weights inputs.

## 4. How the seals combine on a pack

- ROC, Real Organic, Demeter: found next to the USDA Organic seal. The pack should show both.
- AGW, Land to Market, Regenified: can appear with or without organic. A pasture-raised chicken that prints one of these plus a plain "regenerative" word should still be checked for the USDA Organic seal and, for chicken, the pasture claim.
- Ruminant meat: organic's pasture rule is the only legal pasture floor. Chicken and pork have no organic pasture requirement of equivalent specificity; verify with a pasture-specific seal (outside this file's scope).

## 5. Practice words top pasture brands print

Classification is the researcher's: **verifiable** means a certifier or a measurable record can confirm it; **marketing** means no standard behind it unless the pack names one. Source for what a farmer can be asked: grassfed.directory question list [F] (how often animals move to fresh pasture, how long a paddock rests, whether synthetic fertilizer, herbicide or insecticide is used, whether cover crops or no-till drills are used).

| Phrase on pack | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Rotated daily" / "moved to fresh pasture daily" | Verifiable if a named seal (AGW, ROC) audits it; otherwise marketing | Movement records exist, but only a certifier checks them. |
| "Mobile coops" / "pasture-raised in mobile coops" | Marketing unless a pasture seal is named | Describes housing, not stocking density or time on pasture. |
| "Fresh grass" | Marketing | No quantity. Grass-fed is what the animal eats; chicken and pigs also eat grain [F grassfed.directory]. |
| "Healthy soil" / "building soil" | Marketing unless soil tests are published | Only ROC (soil tests required) [S], AGW (annual soil tests) [F] and Land to Market (soil lab data every five years) [F] measure it; EWG says results are not public for L2M and Regenified [F]. |
| "No-till" / "cover crops" | Verifiable as a practice (ROC and AGW plans [S, F]) | Practice words that a plan can list; they do not guarantee fewer herbicides (FoE link, secondary [F soilpractice]). |
| "Raised without antibiotics", "no added hormones" | Separate FSIS claims needing documentation [S] | Not regenerative claims. |
| "Regeneratively raised" | Marketing unless a standard and website are named [S] | See section 2. |
