## Certifier and regulation facts used (fetched)
- 7 CFR 205.241 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/205.241): organic poultry need "year-round access to the outdoors"; "Continuous total confinement of poultry indoors is prohibited"; outdoor space at least 75 percent soil with vegetative cover; broilers may be confined the first 4 weeks; layers outdoor space "no less than one square foot for every 2.25 pounds of bird" (or 3.0 sq ft per bird); broilers 1 sq ft per 5.0 lb (or 2.0 sq ft per bird). Mobile housing is a recognised housing type. Organic does not require pasture rotation or daily moves.
- 7 CFR 205.239 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/7/205.239): organic swine "must be housed in a group" except sows at farrowing and suckling; "gestation and farrowing crates are prohibited"; "rooting materials must be provided"; year-round outdoor access; "Continuous total confinement of any animal indoors is prohibited."
- A Greener World, Animal Welfare Approved (https://agreenerworld.org/certifications/animal-welfare-approved/): its own text says it "guarantees animals are raised outdoors on pasture or range for their entire lives on an independent farm"; independent auditors visit "every farm in the program at least once a year"; ISO/IEC Guide 17065 accreditation; IOAS accredited. AGW family: Certified Animal Welfare Approved, Certified Grassfed, Certified Non-GMO, Certified Regenerative by AGW. (Certifier's own claims about itself.)
- Regenerative Organic Certified (https://regenorganic.org/): overseen by the Regenerative Organic Alliance; "meet the highest standards in the world for soil health, animal welfare, and farmworker fairness" (certifier's own words). Detailed requirements not fetched here.
- Hickory Nut Gap about page: it is an accredited Savory Hub (Savory Institute); its standards require farms to "participate in Hickory Nut Gap's EOV program" (Ecological Outcome Verification, Land to Market / Savory).

Regenerative notes: Pasturebird shows Land to Market EOV (outcome verification, Savory network), not Regenerative Organic Certified, per its own site. Greener Pastures states Regenerative Organic Certified (ROA). EOV and ROC are different programs: ROC is a practice-based certification layered on organic with animal welfare and farmworker modules (certifier's words above); EOV measures land outcomes. Neither requires the same things; the app should name the seal, not the word "regenerative".

### Find on the pack: chicken (ordered by how much it separates top from ordinary)
1. A named audited welfare seal (Certified Humane, GAP step, or AGW Animal Welfare Approved) rather than only "humanely raised".
2. "Moved to fresh pasture daily" / mobile or floorless coop wording, with a welfare or organic seal behind it. Organic law (7 CFR 205.241) allows indoor-only housing with door access and no rotation, so rotation is what organic alone does not tell you.
3. USDA Organic (government, audited): outdoor access, organic non-GMO feed, no antibiotics. Floor, not ceiling.
4. A land-outcome or regenerative seal named as a seal (Regenerative Organic Certified, Certified Regenerative by AGW, Land to Market EOV), not the bare word.
5. "No antibiotics ever" plus a seal that verifies it (organic, Certified Humane). Alone it is a self-attested label claim.
Sounds good, means little: "natural"; "humanely raised" with no seal; "pasture raised" alone (no USDA definition, per Pasturebird's own FAQ that it is loosely defined); "free range" alone; "no hormones" on chicken (hormones are not permitted in poultry; rule text not fetched this run, so mark unverified); "vegetarian fed"; "USDA inspected" (every plant has it).

### Find on the pack: eggs
1. "Pasture-raised" carried by a named seal (Certified Humane Pasture-Raised, or AGW Animal Welfare Approved) that states the outdoor area per hen. The words alone have no USDA definition.
2. Rotated pasture stated with the area per hen (the one fetched brand states 108 sq ft).
3. USDA Organic (government, audited): outdoor access, organic feed.
4. Farm name or traceability on the carton.
5. Non-GMO Project Verified or organic for feed.
Sounds good, means little: "cage-free" (indoor barn); "free range" or "all natural"; "vegetarian fed" (hens eat bugs, so it can mean no outdoor foraging); "no hormones" (not allowed in laying hens; rule text not fetched, unverified); "farm fresh". Cornucopia's lowest-scoring example is a nationally advertised "enhanced" brand.

### Find on the pack: pork
1. A named welfare seal with pasture: Animal Welfare Approved by AGW, Certified Humane Pasture-Raised, or GAP Step 4 or higher. Swine are where organic alone is weakest, because 7 CFR 205.239 allows group housing with outdoor access and temporary confinement but does not require pasture or rotation.
2. "Raised outdoors on pasture" or "rooting" with that seal behind it, and heritage breed named.
3. USDA Organic (crate bans, group housing, rooting material, year-round outdoor access are in the regulation).
4. "No antibiotics ever" backed by a seal or protocol.
5. Regenerative seal named as a seal (Regenerative Organic Certified, Certified Regenerative by AGW, EOV).
Sounds good, means little: "natural"; "humanely raised" alone; "no hormones" (not permitted in pork; unverified rule text); "vegetarian fed"; "heritage" alone; "pasture raised" alone.
