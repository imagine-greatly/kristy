# label_terms — T2, round 2, 2026-10-23

Reviewed BRIEF, locked decisions, round 1 and all 19 current cards, including their existing tips/decoded labels/questions. All round-1 URLs were reopened; failures and new-source failures appear below. Links are pages opened this run; failed opens are identified, never used as evidence. Only this report is written, so no archive files are created.

Common second-source basis for the whole/plain-food rungs across these label cards: [Nourished Kitchen](https://nourishedkitchen.com/getting-started-with-real-food/) favors whole foods, traditional preparation and avoiding flavor/color/preservative formulations; [100 Days](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/healthy-eating-defined/) independently favors whole ingredients, unsweetened organic dairy and whole grains over organic formulated snacks. Organic remains the brief's floor, not a claim that every organic recipe outranks every other food. Best versus close-second distinctions based only on producer access are traceability preferences, not demonstrated nutrient rankings. Each card below adds independent corroboration to its specific round-1 choices or explicitly identifies an unresolved claim. US label meanings are not automatically international rules; no government or mainstream health page supplies a quality judgment here.

## label_natural
- NEW FINDINGS — Independent second to round 1's Cornucopia: [Rodale FAQs](https://rodaleinstitute.org/why-organic/organic-faqs/) supports choosing organic over natural-only and asking small growers about methods; its sweeping natural-label wording must retain the meat/poultry exception.
- NEW FINDINGS — [Kresser's label investigation](https://chriskresser.com/misleading-food-labels/) adds a processing check missed in round 1: a plain-looking chicken breast can contain injected broth; read any added-solution statement and its ingredients, regardless of farm imagery.
- NEW FINDINGS — [FoodPrint shopping guide](https://foodprint.org/shopping-sustainably/) supports grower questions and checking whether market vendors actually grew the food; local availability and out-of-season displays need separate scrutiny.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1 — Two Cornucopia pages are one organization, not independent corroboration. “Minimally processed” is not an organic or pasture promise; no opened source establishes that smallness alone makes food healthier. No round-1 source failed here.
- QUESTIONS — “Does this natural chicken contain injected broth?”; “Did this stall grow this out-of-season produce?”; “Can the counter show the ingredient and farm-practice sheet for unpackaged natural meat?”
- LADDER CHANGES — Best: organic whole food + named grower and specific spray/feed answers; close second: organic plain equivalent with relevant “100% grass-fed”/“pasture-raised” details; floor: organic simple preparation; avoid: “natural,” “all natural,” “farm fresh” alone. Shelf action: locate added solutions and actual ingredients before buying.

## label_multigrain_vs_whole_grain
- NEW FINDINGS — Independent second to WAPF's preparation preference: [Nourished Kitchen](https://nourishedkitchen.com/getting-started-with-real-food/) favors sprouted grains or freshly milled grain in sourdough; this supports traditional methods, not a proven health ranking of every sprouted loaf over every other whole-grain loaf.
- NEW FINDINGS — [Real Bread Campaign](https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/sourdough/) treats genuine sourdough as a live-starter process, not sour taste; ask for final-dough fermentation and processing aids. “Sourdough powder,” acidifiers and commercial yeast require further questions; its craft definition is not a universal legal definition.
- NEW FINDINGS — [Khorasan Mills](https://www.khorasanmills.com/blogs/baking-with-ancient-grains/storing-grains-or-flour) supports choosing kernels versus flour deliberately: flour keeps less well, buy usable quantities, date/rotate stock and protect it from moisture, heat and light in a sealed food-grade container.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1 — Preserve whole composition versus intact form, but no opened source validates an exact universal soaking/fermentation duration or the proposed close-second health ranking. FAILED new sources: [Breadtopia flour page](https://breadtopia.com/store/organic-whole-grain-bread-flour/) and [Community Grains storage](https://www.communitygrains.com/how-to-store-whole-grain-flour/), both 403; search excerpts are not evidence.
- QUESTIONS — “Was the final dough fermented, or just the starter?”; “How recently was this whole-grain flour milled, and how should it be stored?”; “Does sprouted or stone-ground flour still contain the entire grain?”
- LADDER CHANGES — Best: organic whole kernels traditionally prepared, or fresh organic “100% whole grain” bread with live-starter fermentation confirmed; close second: organic sprouted whole-grain simple loaf, as a process preference; floor: organic 100% whole grain with every flour checked; avoid: enriched-flour multigrain, coloring and flavor-only sourdough claims. Ask the baker about all flours, final fermentation and milling date.
