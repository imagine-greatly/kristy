# pantryc — T2 deepen findings

Research date: 2026-10-23. Ladders apply the locked brief and decisions; they are proposed shopping preferences, not comparative health-effect claims. Producer links document ingredients or methods, not brand picks. Every cited URL was opened during this run.

## nutritional_yeast

GAPS: Keep the unfortified lead; add organic to decision/cart_pick, distinguish a seasoning from a dependable B12 source, and replace the unsupported low-temperature buying rule. Bulk-bin advice omits identity, turnover and storage.
FINDINGS: [Fortification explainer](https://www.drberg.com/blog/fortified-vs-non-fortified-nutritional-yeast) supports reading separately listed vitamins to identify fortification; both forms are inactive, and unfortified yeast should not be presented as a B12 source.
FINDINGS: [Health-food producer FAQ](https://www.marigoldhealthfoods.co.uk/faqs) documents organic unfortified flakes grown using organic beet molasses, demonstrating that organic and unfortified are separate, achievable checks.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic “unfortified/non-fortified” nutritional yeast, yeast-only ingredients, maker explains growing medium and deactivation; CLOSE SECOND organic yeast-only unfortified flakes with less production detail; FLOOR organic unfortified granules/powder meeting the same ingredient rule; AVOID added-vitamin formulas for this preference, cheese-flavored seasoning blends and assuming “organic” means unfortified.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Spice/natural-foods aisle; flakes for sprinkling, powder for blending, with no established health advantage from particle size. Choose a sealed modest pack, keep tightly closed and dry per label; do not substitute active baking yeast or assume brewer’s yeast tastes or functions identically.
SHELF STEP: Read the entire ingredient panel for added B vitamins, then verify organic and the exact product identity; ask the maker about the growth medium if absent.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Non-fortified nooch; inactive yeast; brewer’s yeast; “Does unfortified supply B12?”; “Does organic also mean no added vitamins?”; “Are flakes healthier than powder?”

## canned_coconut_milk

GAPS: “Coconut alone” wrongly excludes water; restore organic, distinguish milk/cream from sweetened cream of coconut, and add separation and opened-storage guidance. A non-BPA claim alone does not describe every lining component.
FINDINGS: [Producer’s gum-free formulation](https://store.edwardandsons.com/collections/coconut/products/native-forest-organic-unsweetened-simple-coconut-milk) documents organic coconut and water without gum; [producer handling FAQ](https://store.edwardandsons.com/pages/faq) explains natural separation, refrigeration after opening and freezing portions, with a four-day opened limit for its own milk.
FINDINGS: [Holistic coconut-milk discussion](https://chriskresser.com/3-reasons-why-coconut-milk-may-not-be-your-friend/) supports homemade milk and asking about gums/packaging, but explicitly does not establish guar gum as universally harmful.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST freshly extract milk from organic coconut meat at home; CLOSE SECOND organic unsweetened full-fat coconut milk in glass when available, coconut/water only; FLOOR that same gum-free whole milk in an intact can with lining disclosed; AVOID “lite,” sweetened cream of coconut, drink cartons/aseptic boxes and gum/emulsifier-heavy formulas under the locked preference.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: International/baking aisle; coconut cream is richer and needs recipe adjustment, while creamed coconut is ground meat. Read coconut content where stated; solid cream over watery liquid can be normal separation. Refrigerate leftovers covered, follow the actual opened limit and freeze surplus portions promptly.
SHELF STEP: Compare two full-fat cans for coconut/water only, check the lining statement and can condition, and choose a size that can be used within its opened-storage directions.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Gum-free coconut milk; coconut cream; cream of coconut; creamed coconut; “Is added water acceptable?”; “Why did the can separate?”; “Can the remainder be frozen?”

## dried_herbs

GAPS: Decision is useful, but cart_pick drops organic; why/steps dwell on legal terminology. A fully named blend can qualify, and absence of a retail irradiation symbol is insufficient as the entire process check. Add freshness, leaf form and storage.
FINDINGS: [Herbalist supplier’s selection guide](https://blog.mountainroseherbs.com/how-to-choose-the-best-herbs-and-spices) supports color/aroma checks and airtight glass away from stove heat and sunlight; [handling policy](https://mountainroseherbs.com/policies/quality-control/handling) documents an explicit no-irradiation policy and occasional low-heat steam treatment.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST fresh local organic culinary herbs in season when suitable for the dish; CLOSE SECOND recently dried organic whole/cut leaves from a small grower disclosing harvest and treatment; FLOOR sealed organic single herbs or fully named herb-only blends, non-irradiated, no fillers; AVOID irradiated herbs, stale dusty stock and blends hiding flavors, sweeteners or unnecessary additives.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Produce/herb grower for fresh, spice aisle for dried; choose leaf pieces to crumble at use rather than unnecessary powder. “Italian seasoning” is acceptable only after reading every ingredient; “smoked” needs an actual smoking method. Buy small jars and keep dry, tightly capped and away from the cooker.
SHELF STEP: Check organic, origin and pack/best-before information, then ask a bulk seller when the batch arrived and how it was treated; reject weak-smelling, faded stock when inspection is possible.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Rubbed sage; mixed herbs; Italian seasoning; freeze-dried herbs; “Are blends automatically worse?”; “Does steam-treated mean irradiated?”; “How can stale herbs be recognized?”

## tea

GAPS: Keep the locked organic loose/whole-leaf direction; cart_pick needs organic and herbal specificity. Add actual bag material, named flavor ingredients, decaffeination method and freshness; tea color alone should not become a health ranking.
FINDINGS: [Holistic tea-materials guide](https://gimmethegoodstuff.org/blogs/guides-and-product-reviews/how-to-avoid-toxins-in-tea-healthy-tea-guide) distinguishes paper seals, plastic mesh and plant-derived PLA; “compostable” does not by itself establish a plastic-free bag. Use the material explanation without importing its brand rankings.
FINDINGS: [Organic decaf producer description](https://shop.clipper-teas.com/collections/all-products/products/organic-decaf-black-tea-20-bags) documents CO2 decaffeination as a process shoppers can ask for and cool/dry storage.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic loose whole tea leaf or named organic culinary herbs from a small grower/tea merchant with harvest/origin disclosed; CLOSE SECOND those ingredients in whole-leaf bags with fully disclosed plastic-free fibers/seal; FLOOR plain organic loose broken-leaf tea or named-herb blend; AVOID “natural flavors,” instant sweetened mixes and undisclosed/plastic brewing bags.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Tea aisle or tea merchant; choose green/black/oolong by use and caffeine preference, herbal by actual plant. Bergamot oil or jasmine flowers are named additions, distinct from unspecified flavor. For decaf ask for CO2 processing; do not equate decaf with a caffeine-free herb infusion. Keep sealed away from moisture, heat and odors.
SHELF STEP: Read ingredients, organic and bag/seal composition together; choose loose leaf if the maker cannot identify the brewing material, and ask for harvest/packing date when freshness matters.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Tisane; rooibos; whole-leaf sachet; CO2 decaf; matcha; “Is a plant-based bag plastic-free?”; “Is decaf caffeine-free?”; “Does Earl Grey name its flavor ingredient?”

## vinegar

GAPS: Keep raw/aged direction but separate dressing vinegar from preservation use. Five percent alone is not a canning recipe; homemade acidity cannot be inferred from taste or pH. Balsamic starts with cooked must, so “raw” is not its quality test.
FINDINGS: [Craft vinegar maker’s acidity guide](https://supremevinegar.com/vinegar-acidities-levels-usage/) distinguishes cooking-strength bottles from canning vinegar; [fermentation explainer](https://supremevinegar.com/making-vinegar-home-essential-guide-part-basics/) distinguishes titratable acidity from pH.
FINDINGS: [Traditional balsamic producer](https://us.giusti.com/pages/traditional-balsamic-vinegar-of-modena-d-o-p) documents cooked grape must and 12-/25-year aging; [traditional shopping criteria](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) favors raw organic vinegar and aged organic balsamic.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic naturally fermented “raw/unpasteurized” cider, wine or coconut vinegar from a disclosed small producer; CLOSE SECOND organic traditionally aged balsamic with must-only or must/wine-vinegar ingredients appropriate to its style; FLOOR organic pasteurized fermented vinegar, no flavors/color; AVOID distilled vinegar as the preferred table pick, balsamic glazes with syrup/thickeners and cleaning vinegar as food.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Condiment aisle; choose unseasoned rice vinegar rather than sugar/salt-added “seasoned” vinegar. “With mother” describes sediment/culture, not measured acidity. For canning buy food vinegar with the acidity and type specified by the tested recipe, commonly 5%; never substitute homemade unknown-strength vinegar. Keep capped, cool and dark.
SHELF STEP: First identify dressing/cooking versus preservation use; read organic, fermentation/heat statement, added ingredients and printed acidity before choosing the bottle.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: ACV; vinegar with mother; seasoned rice vinegar; balsamic glaze; “Is pH the same as percent acidity?”; “Is aged balsamic raw?”; “Can this bottle replace the vinegar in a canning recipe?”

## pasta_sauce

GAPS: Keep organic/glass/olive-oil lead; cart_pick omits organic, and why is mostly ingredient-order trivia. Remove the unqualified caramel-color contaminant implication; add passata versus finished sauce, home preparation and opened storage.
FINDINGS: [Traditional Neapolitan tomato sauce](https://ouritaliantable.com/sugo-di-pomodoro/) demonstrates tomatoes, extra-virgin olive oil, garlic and basil without a sweetener, supporting a recognizably food-based sauce and a homemade option.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST simmer ripe local organic tomatoes with organic extra-virgin olive oil and herbs; CLOSE SECOND small-producer organic tomato sauce in glass, tomatoes plus named vegetables/herbs, olive oil and salt; FLOOR organic plain passata/tomato purée in glass, season at home; AVOID added sugars/syrups, seed-oil blends, flavor/color additions and added citric acid under the brief’s short-list preference.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Tomato/pasta aisle; passata is an ingredient, marinara a prepared sauce, tomato paste a concentrate requiring recipe adjustment. Oil-free plain tomatoes can qualify for home seasoning. “No added sugar” does not mean zero naturally occurring tomato sugar; “Italian-style” does not identify the grower or ingredients.
SHELF STEP: Read the whole list, including oil blends, then check seal and opened refrigeration/use directions; buy a jar sized for use or freeze surplus in suitable portions. Ask a small maker where the tomatoes were grown and whether sauce is cooked from tomatoes or reconstituted concentrate.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Passata; strained tomatoes; tomato purée; sugo; pizza sauce; “Is oil-free passata a good base?”; “Does no added sugar mean sugar-free?”; “Is tomato paste the same as pasta sauce?”

## jam

GAPS: Keep organic fruit/natural-sweetener preference; honey in cooked jam is not still raw. Add fruit quantity, no-sweetener options, pectin function and small-jar turnover. Do not turn avoidance of isolated acid additives into advice to omit required recipe acid.
FINDINGS: [Small pectin producer’s fruit-specific guide](https://pomonapectin.com/create-your-own-recipe/) shows honey-compatible, low-sweetener jam, calcium-set pectin and required lemon/lime for certain fruits; [storage FAQ](https://pomonapectin.com/faq/?include_category=how-to-use-pomonas-pectin) recommends small jars and about three weeks refrigerated after opening for its cooked recipes, not every commercial jam.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST small-batch organic crushed-fruit refrigerator/freezer spread, unsweetened where suitable, following an appropriate recipe; CLOSE SECOND organic fruit-first preserves with modest honey/maple and named lemon juice if needed; FLOOR organic fruit-first jam with a natural sweetener and simple fruit pectin; AVOID corn syrup, white-sugar-led formulas, artificial sweeteners and added citric/ascorbic acid under the locked preference.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Jam aisle/farm stall; compare fruit percentage and actual sweetener, including concentrated juice. Jelly uses juice rather than fruit pieces; “fruit spread” alone does not describe formulation. Pectin is a gelling ingredient, not proof of a poor jar. Prefer small glass and refrigerate opened jars according to their own directions.
SHELF STEP: Compare two jars of the same fruit for fruit content, sweetener and full list; ask a market seller for ingredients and opened keeping instructions, and never alter preservation acid just to shorten a recipe.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Fruit butter; freezer jam; honey preserves; no-added-sugar spread; “Is honey still raw after cooking?”; “Is pectin necessary?”; “Does juice concentrate sweeten a spread?”

## ketchup_mustard

GAPS: Retain the locked fermented/unsweetened lead; replace federal-standard discussion with ingredient/process checks. Vinegar does not prove fermentation, and seed texture/Dijon naming is not a quality grade.
FINDINGS: [Traditional ketchup method](https://traditionalcookingschool.com/food-preparation/recipes/lacto-fermented-homemade-ketchup/) uses organic tomato paste, vinegar, spices and a culture/fermentation step, followed by refrigeration; its honey/maple versions demonstrate that fermented need not mean unsweetened.
FINDINGS: [Traditional mustard methods](https://traditionalcookingschool.com/food-preparation/recipes/dips-spreads/3-fermented-mustard-recipes/) distinguish yellow/white seeds for mild flavor from brown/black for heat and describe cultured mustard refrigerated after fermentation.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic genuinely fermented ketchup/mustard, short list, unheated finished product; CLOSE SECOND organic unsweetened ketchup or organic seed/vinegar/water/salt mustard; FLOOR organic short-list ketchup modestly sweetened with named honey/maple, and plain organic prepared mustard; AVOID corn syrup, seed oils, preservatives, unspecified flavors and sugar-heavy honey-mustard dips.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Check refrigerated ferments as well as condiment shelves; read “unpasteurized” when live cultures are the goal. Whole-grain, stone-ground, Dijon and yellow describe style; inspect wine, sweetener and flavor additions separately. Keep live versions cold and follow each jar’s opened directions.
SHELF STEP: Compare ketchup and mustard separately; verify organic and every ingredient, then ask whether “fermented” describes the finished condiment and whether it was heated afterward.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Cultured ketchup; fermented mustard; honey mustard dressing; mustard powder; “Can fermented ketchup contain honey?”; “Does vinegar make mustard fermented?”; “Is stone-ground a health grade?”

## mayo

GAPS: Good oil hierarchy, including the affirmative pure/cold-pressed avocado option; restore organic and egg sourcing. “Mayonnaise” naming does not settle oil quality, and a universal one-to-two-month opened limit should give way to product-specific directions, especially for homemade mayo.
FINDINGS: [Traditional mayonnaise explainer](https://traditionalcookingschool.com/food-preparation/how-to-make-healthy-mild-tasting-mayonnaise-askwardee-128/) supports yolk/acid/oil preparation, organic traditional oils and cold-pressed avocado for a milder flavor; it distinguishes refining for mildness from choosing less-processed oil.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST freshly make a small batch with organic pastured egg yolks, organic extra-virgin olive oil, vinegar/lemon and salt; CLOSE SECOND organic short-list mayo using olive oil as its sole oil; FLOOR organic avocado-oil mayo when the maker documents pure, cold-pressed oil, with simple egg/acid ingredients; AVOID soybean/canola/sunflower blends, refined-oil formulas sold only on an olive/avocado front claim and sweetened flavor-heavy dressings.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Condiment and refrigerated cases; “made with olive oil” can still accompany another oil. Read every oil and the egg ingredients; “light” and “vegan” do not answer the fat question. Homemade and unopened shelf-stable mayonnaise require different handling; refrigerate opened product and use its stated limit, with a clean utensil.
SHELF STEP: Circle every oil in the ingredient list, verify organic and extraction details, then choose the smaller jar when turnover is slow; ask about pasture-raised eggs rather than inferring pasture from organic alone.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Olive-oil blend mayo; cold-pressed avocado mayo; aioli; vegan mayonnaise; “Why is this olive-oil mayo mostly another oil?”; “Can mild oil be refined?”; “Does homemade mayo keep like a shelf jar?”

## hot_soy_sauce

GAPS: The combined card barely answers hot sauce. Restore organic and raw-versus-pasteurized brewing rungs; remove “soybeans named first,” since water may lead. Tamari is not automatically gluten-free, and vinegar in hot sauce does not exclude fermentation.
FINDINGS: [Traditional brewer’s process](https://san-j.com/our-tamari/) describes koji, brine, months of aging, pressing and later pasteurization; [organic ingredient panel](https://san-j.com/product/organic-tamari-soy-sauce-for-food-service/) puts water before soybeans, disproving the current first-ingredient shortcut.
FINDINGS: [Traditional fermented hot sauce](https://nourishedkitchen.com/fermented-hot-chili-sauce-recipe/) starts with peppers/salt/water, can include vinegar after fermentation, needs refrigeration for the live version and loses live cultures with canning heat.
PROPOSED LADDER: SOY—BEST organic “raw/unpasteurized, naturally brewed” shoyu/tamari with disclosed aging; CLOSE SECOND organic naturally brewed pasteurized sauce, no hydrolyzed protein; FLOOR simple organic genuinely brewed sauce with less aging detail. HOT—BEST organic unheated fermented peppers/salt sauce; CLOSE SECOND organic vinegar/pepper short-list sauce; FLOOR organic cooked pepper sauce without sweetener/flavor additives. AVOID acid-hydrolyzed soy protein, liquid aminos as a brewed equivalent, caramel color, corn syrup and preservative-heavy hot sauces.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Asian/condiment aisles and cold ferments; water first is acceptable, “reduced sodium” does not establish method, and shoyu normally includes wheat. Read a gluten-free statement when needed; heat level is a taste choice. Keep live hot sauce cold and follow each sauce’s opened directions.
SHELF STEP: Choose the soy or pepper branch, read the process and full ingredients, then ask “Was it fermented, for how long, and heated after fermentation?”
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Nama shoyu; wheat-free tamari; liquid aminos; pepper mash; sriracha; “Can fermented hot sauce contain vinegar?”; “Does naturally brewed mean raw?”; “Can water be listed first?”

## canned_soup

GAPS: Keep homemade/bone-broth lead and additive/oil checks; restore organic and source of bones. “Most canned soups” is an unsupported current-market prevalence claim. Yeast extract and hydrolyzed protein should be named separately rather than universally relabeled MSG.
FINDINGS: [Traditional pantry critique](https://www.westonaprice.org/pantry-intervention-video-by-sarah-pope-2/) supports replacing additive-reliant canned soup with homemade broth-based food; its broad market assertions are not a current product census.
FINDINGS: [Traditional chicken-broth method](https://nourishedkitchen.com/fresh-chicken-broth/) demonstrates real poultry, vegetables and herbs as a soup base and distinguishes meat broth from longer-cooked bone broth; “broth” alone does not describe the sourcing or preparation.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST homemade soup from organic pastured poultry bones or organic 100% grass-fed beef bones and seasonal vegetables; CLOSE SECOND small-maker organic bone-broth soup, fresh/refrigerated or frozen, fully disclosed list; FLOOR organic canned bone-broth soup without MSG, yeast extract, hydrolyzed protein or seed oils; AVOID bouillon/powder-based flavor substitutes, natural flavors and additive-heavy condensed soups.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Check freezer/cold soup before the canned aisle. Read nested broth ingredients and any added cream, starch or oil; “bone broth,” “organic” and “low sodium” each leave other decisions unanswered. Water can legitimately appear in broth; “condensed” requires the stated dilution. Reject damaged containers and follow opened refrigeration/reheating directions.
SHELF STEP: Compare two full ingredient panels, find the actual broth and fat, and ask the small maker where the bones came from; buy or freeze meal-sized portions.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Ready-to-eat soup; condensed soup; boxed soup; stock-based soup; “Does bone broth on the front guarantee a simple list?”; “Is yeast extract the same ingredient as MSG?”; “What must be added to condensed soup?”

## olives

GAPS: Keep organic/brine preference; “water and salt, nothing else” needlessly excludes named herbs/olive oil. Brine packing does not prove lye-free curing, and lye treatment can precede fermentation. Universal two-week opened keeping is too rigid.
FINDINGS: [Traditional olive producer’s process explanation](https://olivesetal.co.uk/blogs/olive-history-geekery/why-are-olives-fermented) distinguishes natural brine fermentation, lye followed by fermentation and heat treatment after curing; finished processing determines whether cultures remain.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic grower-disclosed olives naturally fermented in salt brine without lye, unpasteurized; CLOSE SECOND organic traditionally dry-salt-cured olives, optional extra-virgin olive oil; FLOOR organic brine-fermented olives with fully disclosed curing/heat treatment, simple ingredients; AVOID color-fixed uniform black olives, ferrous gluconate/lactate and seed-oil or additive-heavy marinades for this preference.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Olive counter/cold deli and jars; whole versus pitted is a use choice, and green/black/Kalamata/Castelvetrano names do not establish curing. Glass supports ingredient inspection. Read stuffed fillings and marinade oils too; keep opened olives refrigerated as directed, brined fruit covered by its liquid, and use the producer’s opened limit.
COUNTER STEP: Ask “Were these cured directly in brine or first treated with lye, and were they pasteurized afterward?” Request the complete marinade/filling list and storage instructions.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Naturally fermented olives; dry-cured versus oil-cured; olive-bar mix; stuffed olives; “Does packed in brine mean brine-cured?”; “Can lye-treated olives still be fermented?”; “Are black olives always naturally ripe?”

## seaweed

GAPS: Keep named organic sea vegetables and restrained kelp use; cart_pick omits organic. Add harvest waters, drying/roasting, plain sheets versus oily snacks, metals information and species-specific preparation; an origin slogan cannot replace those checks.
FINDINGS: [Seaweed harvester FAQ](https://seaveg.com/pages/faq) distinguishes species, higher iodine in brown kelps, concentrated portions, dry airtight storage and dulse eaten directly versus kelp rehydration; species averages are not exact bag measurements.
FINDINGS: [Harvester’s published analyses](https://seaveg.com/pages/seaweed-testing-information) address harvest-related lead, cadmium, mercury and inorganic arsenic; supports asking for current species/harvest data rather than treating organic as a zero-metals promise. No certifier or generic testing badge is proposed as the pick.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic whole dried sea vegetables, species and harvest coast disclosed, small harvester explains drying and shares contaminant information; CLOSE SECOND organic plain nori/laver sheets or dulse with the same traceability; FLOOR organic species-named plain flakes/sheets with origin and use instructions; AVOID anonymous “seaweed,” seed-oil/flavored snack sheets, supplement-style kelp dosing and hijiki where inorganic-arsenic information is unavailable.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Asian/natural-foods aisle; sushi nori may be toasted, kombu/kelp is a broth ingredient, wakame is rehydrated, and dulse can be a plain snack. Do not substitute equal amounts across species; follow package portions, especially kelp. Reseal dry, away from heat/moisture; avoid routine refrigeration of dry seaweed that introduces condensation.
SHELF STEP: Locate species, coast, organic and ingredients, then ask the harvester for current iodine and inorganic-arsenic/metals information for that species/harvest if the label is silent.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: Laver; dulse; alaria; kombu versus wakame; roasted nori; seaweed snacks; “Does organic mean no metals?”; “Can kelp replace nori gram for gram?”; “Is white surface powder always mold?”
