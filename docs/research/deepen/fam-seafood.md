### F12 Wild-first seafood

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Small local seasonal fishery, with the actual species, harvest water and “wild-caught” disclosed; plain seafood, promptly chilled or frozen. Seasonal fresh and well-handled frozen can share this rung. The wild preference is editorial; prompt freezing is a documented way to retain harvest quality. [Traditional selection][trad]; [freezing evidence][frozen].
- **CLOSE:** A sealed “wild-caught” pack naming species and harvest origin, with seafood alone, optionally salt; select usable portions. “Flash-frozen” is useful processing information, while the case word “fresh” is insufficient to establish the catch timeline. [Frozen supply][frozen]; [portioning][thaw].
- **FLOOR:** Plain frozen wild seafood with identifiable species/origin and no added solution. If the desired species cannot clear those checks, change species; canned alternatives route to F13. No shared automatic farmed fallback survives the round-two corrections. [Traditional selection][trad]; [treatment specification][shrimp-spec].
- **SKIP:** Anonymous “fish/white fish,” unexplained treatment, or “farm-raised” without an acceptable item-specific decision. Under the locked sourcing preference, pass over heavily branded, long-distance, year-round “fresh” supply when the pack supplies no fishery or harvest history; those shelf traits are not proof of a health defect. [Source/method fields][shrimp-spec]; [fresh-distribution distinction][frozen].
- **Condition check:** Frozen packs should remain solid with intact seals; chilled fish should have firm flesh and a mild sea scent. Protective surface ice glaze is different from water absorbed through treatment and is not, by itself, a reason to reject a bag. [Condition][quality]; [glaze][glaze].

BEYOND THE STORE: A community-supported fishery or local seafood buying club can supply documented seasonal catch frozen for later use. This is a real route, not a requirement to locate a local fishery for every species. [Community fishery][frozen]; [buying-club model][species].

SEASON/STORAGE: There is no common F12 harvest calendar. Keep chilled purchases cold and frozen stock sealed; thaw meal-sized amounts in the refrigerator, following any instruction to remove/open vacuum packaging before thawing. Do not generalize a supplier's freezer-life number to all five members. [Regional calendars][species]; [thawing][thaw].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Apply the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list) to formulated seafood. The local distinction is **added solution versus protective glaze**, and traceable species versus a generic seafood name; “wild” does not establish absence of processing treatments. [Treatment fields][shrimp-spec]; [glaze][glaze].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Does “fresh” outrank a properly frozen wild pack? No; compare source and handling.
- What must the pack identify? Species, wild/farmed method, harvest origin and additions.
- Does every ice crystal mean a damaged product? No; protective glaze is distinct.
- If the preferred fish is unavailable, is any farmed fish the floor? No; follow its item decision or select another identifiable wild species.

- [Fishermen's freezing account][frozen] — seasonal supply, cold handling and community-supported fisheries.
- [Freezing/thawing guide][thaw]; [condition checklist][quality]; [glaze explanation][glaze] — physical selection and home handling, without invisible-contaminant guarantees.
- [Shrimp procurement specification][shrimp-spec] — independent fields for species, harvest/processing countries, count, preparation, glaze and treatment; process evidence only.

### F13 Tinned fish: species and packing medium

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Traceable small-fishery catch, species explicitly identified, plain fish “in own juices,” “in water,” or in explicitly named “olive oil/extra virgin olive oil”; optional salt. Prefer the shortest suitable recipe under the locked stance; both member cards can meet this recipe. [Actual tuna formula][skipjack]; [salmon's own juices][salmon-can]; [traditional medium preference][trad].
- **CLOSE:** Same acceptable species and recipe in a sound tin/jar when boat-level detail is unavailable; “hand-packed” alone does not establish species, medium or cooking history. Read the actual ingredient list and any disclosed process separately. [Pack/process example][skipjack].
- **FLOOR:** Plain named fish in an acceptable medium with readable storage instructions. If tuna species is unspecified, select an identifiable alternative rather than treating generic “light” as sufficient. Bones/skin are not required across this family. [Tuna label meaning][tuna-law]; [salmon forms][salmon-can].
- **SKIP:** Unnamed species, an “olive oil blend” whose remaining oils fail the global screen, and long-form recipes that fail that screen. Skip the heavily branded, year-round commodity pack when its front claims replace catch and ingredient disclosure; preserved seasonal catch itself remains eligible. [Pack-medium label meaning][tuna-law]; [traditional selection][trad].
- **Package check:** Choose intact containers; reject bulging or leaking tins. Read storage text before assuming a tin or jar belongs in the pantry; also read any lining declaration without treating it as a complete material specification. [Container/storage distinctions][preserves]; [lining disclosure][lining].

BEYOND THE STORE: No separate all-member route established. A buying club offering canned salmon does not establish a route for every tuna or small-fish tin.

SEASON/STORAGE: Follow unopened and after-opening instructions. Sterilized preserves and salt-matured semi-preserves have different storage needs; refrigeration after opening does not establish how the unopened product was meant to be stored. [Preservation categories][preserves].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Link the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list); inspect every packing oil and any broth's subingredients. “Vegetable broth” or an extra herb does not automatically mean hydrolyzed protein, and “water-packed” does not establish a fish-and-water-only recipe. [Permitted recipe/label distinctions][tuna-law].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Is own-juice fish acceptable alongside water and olive oil? Yes.
- Can water-packed tuna contain broth or other additions? Yes; read the full recipe.
- Must every fish tin have bones and skin? No; that belongs to particular species/forms.
- Does a tin or a BPA-related statement settle storage or all lining materials? No.

SOURCES:

- [Plain skipjack specification][skipjack] — species, separate pole-and-line/troll methods, own juices and disclosed once-cooked processing.
- [Traditional salmon pack][salmon-can] — edible skin/bones and self-produced liquid.
- [Preserver's FAQ][preserves] — sterilized versus semi-preserved foods and container checks.
- [Canned-tuna standard][tuna-law] — raw meaning of color, form, medium and recipe declarations only.
- [Can-lining disclosure][lining] — no intentional BPA addition is a specific formulation statement.
- [Traditional selection][trad]; [holistic fish discussion][fish-guide] — ingredient preference and species concern, not a packing-medium health guarantee.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `canned_fish_choosing`: **Whole sardines and traditional wild salmon with edible skin/bones are the form preference; do not extend it to all anchovy fillets or tuna.** Salmon's own liquid needs no added water/oil. Salt-cured anchovy fillets may be semi-preserves needing cool/refrigerated storage before opening: read that tin's instructions. [Traditional pack][salmon-can]; [anchovy processing][preserves]; [species/form preference][trad].
- `canned_tuna`: **“Skipjack/Katsuwonus pelamis,” then “pole-and-line” OR “troll”; delete “pole-and-troll.”** “Light” is a color designation, not a species guarantee; solid/chunk describes form. Own-juice/once-cooked is useful only when disclosed. The holistic source raises greater mercury concern for albacore/bigeye than skipjack; use the named species preference without a mercury-free or selenium-cancellation claim. ### F14 Moving live crustaceans

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Small local seasonal fishery, named species/harvest area; if choosing whole live crab or lobster, require observable movement and prompt cooking plans. This is a whole-food/form preference, not evidence that live always outranks properly frozen meat nutritionally. [Live condition][quality]; [whole/cooked crab forms][crab-source].
- **CLOSE:** Plain named-species whole cooked seafood or frozen portions, with source and “raw/fully cooked” status explicit. Select solid frozen packs with sound seals; the live-movement test does not apply. [Crab portions][species]; [lobster formats][lobster-forms]; [glaze][glaze].
- **FLOOR:** Actual picked/extracted meat, ingredients “crab meat” or “lobster,” optionally salt, with explicit “keep refrigerated/keep frozen” directions. A metal can alone is not a pantry instruction. [Chilled crab can][crab-can]; [lobster meat formats][lobster-forms].
- **SKIP:** Unresponsive animals offered as live when staff cannot establish condition, off-smelling flesh, unclear species/cooking status, or added moisture/flavor formulations that fail the simple-food screen. The commercial-pattern skip is a heavily marketed “premium” pack without species/origin/process details. [Condition][quality]; [process distinctions][lobster-forms].

BEYOND THE STORE: Regional seafood buying clubs can supply seasonal frozen crustaceans; source documentation still matters. No universal dockside live-sale availability is established. [Crab/prawn buying-club examples][species].

SEASON/STORAGE: No common crab-and-lobster season. Keep live purchases cold and ventilated; plan prompt cooking. Follow the actual chilled/frozen storage directions for other formats rather than a single live-animal use window. [Live handling][quality]; [lobster handling][lobster-live]; [chilled product][crab-can].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Apply the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list) to extracted meat and tails; do not confuse protective glaze with an absorbed solution. No automatic “eat the butter/tomalley” instruction: the traditional organ source itself raises source-water concerns and acknowledges missing organ nutrient analyses. That supports withholding a blanket recommendation, not diagnosing the contents of a particular animal. [Glaze][glaze]; [traditional organ caveat][crab-source].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Is movement required for cooked legs or frozen tails? No, only live-sale animals.
- Can a pasteurized metal can require refrigeration? Yes.
- Which words decide preparation? Actual species and “raw/fully cooked,” plus storage directions.
- Does whole-animal preference require eating all internal organs? No blanket instruction is supported.

SOURCES:

- [Fishmonger's live-condition checks][quality]; [live lobster handling][lobster-live] — movement, cold storage and ventilation.
- [Regional crustacean specifications][species] — precooked frozen crab portions and seasonal variation.
- [Lobster processor's formats][lobster-forms] — raw tails, cooked meat and raw HPP-extracted meat.
- [Picked-crab specification][crab-can] — species, SAPP and refrigerated pasteurized-can format; a label example, not a negative supplier judgment.
- [Traditional crab discussion][crab-source]; [glaze explanation][glaze] — limits on organ and ice-based judgments.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `crab`: **Choose real crab, not “imitation crab/surimi”; read picked-meat species and ingredients.** “Lump/backfin/claw” does not replace that identification. A pasteurized can may require refrigeration; SAPP can be declared for crystal control, so do not label every phosphate as water pumping. Delete shell redness as a sufficient freshness test and the unconditional butter instruction. [Picked-meat example][crab-can]; [condition][quality]; [traditional crab/organ evidence][crab-source]; [imitation distinction][roe].
- `lobster`: **Live clawed lobster: leg/claw movement and tail curl; frozen tails: species, solid condition and raw/cooked wording.** “HPP/cold-cracked” extracted meat can still be raw. Keep live lobster damp, refrigerated and ventilated, without tap-water immersion or freezing; cook the buying day where practical. [Live handling][lobster-live]; [HPP format][lobster-forms].

### F15 Tagged live bivalves

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Small seasonal local grower/harvester, live “clams/mussels/oysters,” with harvest water/date and shipper traceability. Marine-farmed bivalves can qualify; do not transfer the ration-fed-fish skip to this family. Read the sack label; at a staffed loose-shellfish counter, request the lot's tag. [Tag fields][tag]; [marine husbandry][mussels]; [grower practices][bivalve-faq].
- **CLOSE:** A traceable live lot from another disclosed growing area meeting the same condition checks. For live hard-shell stock, look for intact moist shells: oysters closed; slightly open clams/mussels responsive to handling/tapping. [Live selection][bivalve-handling].
- **FLOOR:** If live stock cannot qualify, use dated plain shucked meat in its own liquor for cooking, or plain frozen/canned bivalves under the appropriate storage/ingredient checks. This is a **format branch**, not permission to apply live-shell tests to shucked or processed products. [Shucked form][bivalve-handling]; [cooking use][bivalve-faq]; [canned alternative][trad].
- **SKIP:** Anonymous harvest water, damaged or unresponsive live hard-shell stock, sour-smelling shucked meat, and retail “fresh/premium” claims without lot traceability. Skip the heavily branded long-distance year-round offer when it obscures source and handling. [Tag fields][tag]; [condition][bivalve-handling].
- **Farming boundary:** Marine growers document natural plankton feeding; land-based systems can supply cultured microalgae. Do not write “all farmed bivalves receive no added food,” or treat filtering as proof of pristine flesh. [Marine grower][mussels]; [land-based counterexample][ras]; [water-source concern][bivalve-faq].

BEYOND THE STORE: A shellfish farm store is a documented direct route. Prefer local seasonal stock with the same harvest traceability, rather than an untagged informal source. [Farm-store route][bivalve-faq]; [tag meaning][tag].

SEASON/STORAGE: No universal “R months” calendar: growing region and current harvest conditions matter. Refrigerate loose live shellfish under a damp cloth in a ventilated container, not submerged in freshwater or sealed airtight; keep out of meltwater. Processed sealed packs follow their own directions. [Regional/handling distinctions][bivalve-faq]; [live storage][bivalve-handling]; [pack distinction][mussels].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Live stock's decisive exclusions are lost traceability and failed condition. For canned, smoked or sauced formats, link the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list). “Live,” a tag and a named grower do not establish suitability for raw consumption. [Tag meaning][tag]; [raw/cooked distinction][bivalve-faq].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Can farmed mussels, oysters and clams clear the family? Yes; check the actual growing system and water.
- What does the tag contribute? Lot-specific harvest and shipper identification.
- Must every live shell be treated identically? No; the listed closure checks concern hard-shell stock, with species distinctions above.
- Can loose live shellfish sit in a sealed bag or water? Use ventilated damp cold storage instead.
- Are “R months” or a sealed processed pack substitutes for the actual storage instructions? No.

SOURCES:

- [Shellstock tag fields][tag] — government source used only for raw tag/label meaning, not nutritional judgment or a new legal-compliance checklist.
- [Grower-association buying/handling][bivalve-handling] — hard-shell live condition, shucked condition and storage.
- [Shellfish farmer FAQ][bivalve-faq] — regional seasons, farm store, shucked cooking products and water-source caveat.
- [Marine mussel grower FAQ][mussels]; [land-based farm FAQ][ras] — feed-system and package exceptions.
- [Traditional seafood categories][trad] — plain canned bivalve fallback.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `clams_mussels_oysters`: **NONE.** All shared shopping findings sit in this single-member family. Soft-shell/steamer closure behavior remains outside the verified hard-shell rule; avoid expanding “any shell that stays open is dead” to every clam variety.

### F16 Dry-packed scallops

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Small local seasonal dayboat catch with boat/harvest-area/date disclosure, no freshwater soaking or additives, ingredients “scallops.” “Dayboat” should describe an actual short trip, not merely a large scallop. [Dayboat handling][dayboat].
- **CLOSE:** The same documented catch promptly frozen, sealed and sold as plain scallops. Off-season frozen supply can preserve the preferred source. [Seasonal frozen supply][dayboat-season].
- **FLOOR:** Identified sea or bay scallops with harvest origin and explicit no-added-water/no-phosphate information. Read the package first; for loose counter stock, request the original tub and treatment/soak information. [Dry-term limitations][dayboat].
- **SKIP:** “Water added,” “sodium tripolyphosphate/STPP,” or another declared phosphate treatment. Do not let “dry,” “natural,” “diver,” or heavily branded year-round supply stand in for actual source/treatment disclosure. [Dry-term limitations][dayboat]; [treatment process][stpp].
- **Size/color are separate:** Sea/bay describes species, not a health rank. Count labels indicate pieces per pound; natural orange meat and seasonal milky exudate are documented in the indexed processor account, but that page could not be opened here: **[unverified]**. Do not diagnose treatment solely from whiteness or liquid. [Species distinction][dayboat]; [processor excerpt][scallop-terms].

BEYOND THE STORE: Direct seasonal dayboat orders are a documented route; use the supplier's catch/date/handling disclosure rather than requiring a staffed retail counter. [Direct supply][dayboat-season].

SEASON/STORAGE: Maine inshore winter supply differs from spring offshore or other-region fisheries; there is no universal scallop winter season. Keep cold; use promptly or preserve frozen portions and follow thawing instructions. The no-soak preference concerns absorbed freshwater, not a universal ban on protective frozen glaze. [Regional calendar][dayboat-season]; [thawing][thaw]; [glaze][glaze].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Point to the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list). Scallops uniquely need **added phosphate/water and soaking history** stated locally because those are the decisive wet-versus-dry checks; no toxicity inference is needed. [Handling distinction][dayboat]; [process explanation][stpp].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Does “dry-packed” alone prove no soaking? No.
- What additional information matters at the counter? Original ingredients, source and soak/treatment history.
- Can frozen dayboat scallops qualify? Yes.
- Does “diver” or unusually large size establish the top rung? No.
- Is milkiness enough to prove treatment? No such visual diagnosis is supported; the optional seasonal explanation remains unverified this run.

### F17 Cured seafood: method and format

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Small source-transparent fisher/curer, actual species and harvest/farm origin, simple disclosed cure, and explicit storage directions. Prefer seasonal source information even when preservation extends availability. The exact wild requirement and recipe differ by member; neither “raw” nor “smoked” is universal. [Cured-fish formats][smoke-glossary]; [roe forms][roe].
- **CLOSE:** Sealed packaged seafood with the same species/source/cure information when direct-curer detail is unavailable. Read species, full recipe and required temperature together; “traditional/artisan” alone cannot supply those fields. [Fish processing example][fisher-faq]; [roe processing distinctions][caviar].
- **FLOOR:** A plainly formulated preserved product with its heat treatment/storage category explicit: pasteurized salt-only roe or shelf-stable canned/retort smoked fish, as appropriate to the item. There is no shared raw-over-heated health ranking. [Pasteurization][caviar]; [smoked storage formats][fisher-faq].
- **SKIP:** Undisclosed cure, additions failing the member's simple-formula requirement, or “keep refrigerated” products displayed warm. Pass over heavily branded, long-distance year-round supply when source and preservation method remain hidden; preservation or distance alone is not a health diagnosis. [Storage dependence][fisher-faq]; [roe preparation/storage][caviar].
- **Read the storage category:** Vacuum-sealed, jarred, tinned, dried or cured appearance alone does not establish shelf stability. Buy a size usable within the product's stated after-opening window. [Smoked formats][fisher-faq]; [roe storage variation][caviar].

BEYOND THE STORE: A fisher-direct range can include both smoked fish and salt-cured roe. Where offered locally, apply the same ingredient and cold-chain checks; no blanket local availability is established. [Fisher's product range][fisher-faq].

SEASON/STORAGE: No shared cured-seafood harvest season or after-opening duration. Use the actual refrigerated/frozen/ambient and opening instructions; do not transfer salmon freezing advice to every delicate roe. [Smoked/roe windows][fisher-faq]; [roe freezing qualification][caviar].

SHARED SKIP LIST: Link the [global ingredient screen](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list), then apply the member's cure rule. Actual wood smoking differs from added smoke flavor; a small sugar/honey component of a salmon cure is not permission to add it to salt-only roe. [Traditional smoking preference][trad]; [cure examples][fisher-faq]; [roe preparation][roe].

SHOPPER QUESTIONS the family answers:

- Does a sealed pouch or jar mean pantry storage? No; read the category and instructions.
- Must every cured seafood be raw, smoked or wild? No; follow the member's method and sourcing rule.
- Do “malossol,” “nova” and “kippered” all describe the same preservation? No.
- Can one opening window or freezing rule cover salmon and all roe? No.

The latter are scope limits, not missing permission to invent a rule. [Unopened processor page][scallop-terms]; [bounded hard-shell checks][bivalve-handling].
