## grassfed_butter

- NEW FINDINGS — independent ladder check: [traditional shopping guide](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) corroborates raw/pasteurized ordering and spread/flavor/whipped exclusions.
- NEW FINDINGS — [craft creamery FAQ](https://www.vermontcreamery.com/pages/faq) independently confirms cultured versus sweet-cream production, seasonal color, covered refrigerated storage and well-wrapped freezing; its four-month freezing advice is product-specific. Higher butterfat changes baking behavior; match the recipe rather than automatically upgrading every pastry to European-style.
- NEW FINDINGS — [raw-butter process panel](https://rawfarmusa.com/raw-butter/) independently confirms cold-churned cultured cream, optional salt, seasonal color and refrigeration; it does not establish organic or a no-grain diet.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1: Keep the color/softness correction. Whipped avoidance has a traditional-guide source, but aeration alone has no demonstrated health penalty here. A [farm listing](https://dutchmeadowsfarm.com/store/product/5-lb-raw-cultured-butter-bundle) illustrates why a raw/organic/feed claim must also be checked against the intended-use statement; a non-human-consumption listing cannot substantiate an edible retail rung.
- QUESTIONS: Should cultured butter replace sweet cream in pastry? How should surplus butter be frozen without absorbing odors? Does a higher butterfat percentage change the recipe's water balance? Is the raw product actually labeled for human consumption?
- LADDER CHANGES: Best “raw,” “organic,” “100% grass-fed,” cream ± cultures/salt, human-food sale confirmed; second same sourcing, pasteurized; floor organic short-list butter; avoid oil-blended spreads and added flavors/colors. Cultured/sweet cream and salted/unsalted are choices within a rung. At shelf, verify process/feed before color.

## whole_vs_reduced_fat_milk

- NEW FINDINGS — independent ladder check: [holistic milk guide](https://www.foodrenegade.com/healthy-milk-what-to-buy/) also ranks raw grass-fed whole milk before lightly pasteurized cream-top milk; its breed-based A2 and disease assertions are not adopted.
- NEW FINDINGS — [small-creamery process disclosure](https://kalonasupernatural.com/simple-methods/minimally-processed/) independently separates vat heating from homogenization; [another creamery](https://www.strausfamilycreamery.com/faqs/) confirms pasteurized cream-top milk and warns that freezing changes its texture and can break glass bottles.
- NEW FINDINGS — [raw-milk farmer FAQ](https://www.creambrookfarm.com/faq) recommends the back of the refrigerator, explains that opaque cartons block light, and reports separation after freezing. This adds a quality-preserving packaging/storage decision absent from the card.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1: Retract COMPLETE. It retained government/extension-led prose, a shortest-date shortcut and conflicting A2/breed wording. “Cream-top” does not necessarily mean pasteurized; raw milk can also be non-homogenized. UHT's suitability for a particular yogurt starter needs that starter's instructions, not a universal failure claim. No independent support for A2 as a separate rung overriding feed/heat.
- QUESTIONS: Where in the refrigerator should cream-top milk go? Can it be frozen in its original glass bottle? Should an opaque carton lose to glass when it protects milk from light? How should a cream plug be redistributed for an even pour?
- LADDER CHANGES: Best organic “100% grass-fed,” “raw,” named farm with handling documented; second same sourcing, “vat/low-temperature pasteurized,” “whole,” “non-homogenized”; floor organic plain pasteurized whole milk; avoid UHT/flavored formulations. A2 is an additional protein descriptor. Read actual process words rather than rank by the nearest date.

## cheese_real_vs_processed

## yogurt_plain_vs_flavored

- NEW FINDINGS — independent ladder check: [traditional guide](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) corroborates plain whole raw/cultured ordering; [farm creamery](https://butterworksfarm.com/creamery/) independently demonstrates organic 100% grass-fed yogurt with active cultures.
- NEW FINDINGS — [traditional yogurt explainer](https://nourishedkitchen.com/raw-milk-yogurt/) independently confirms that scalding raw starting milk makes a heated finished yogurt; raw yogurt can be thin, and straining or cream can thicken it without gums. This supports a process check, not a claim that every thin yogurt is raw.
- NEW FINDINGS — [Swedish filmjölk](https://nourishedkitchen.com/filmjolk/) is a traditional pourable ferment made with a particular starter; [Middle Eastern labneh](https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-bites/labneh-cheese/) is whole yogurt drained longer. Drinkability and very thick texture are not, by themselves, signs of dilution or additives.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1: Keep removal of automatic Greek priority and culture-seal certainty. The [ultrafiltration disclosure](https://www.maplehill.com/faq) establishes one maker's Greek method, not all Greek/skyr production. Regional names do not establish organic/feed claims. No independent evidence here makes raw yogurt clinically superior.
- QUESTIONS: Can plain filmjölk qualify despite pouring like a drink? Is labneh simply longer-drained yogurt? Was raw starting milk scalded before culturing? Can raw yogurt be thin without being diluted?
- LADDER CHANGES: Best organic 100% grass-fed plain whole farm yogurt, genuinely raw process confirmed; second same sourcing pasteurized cream-top/live-culture yogurt; floor organic plain whole milk + cultures; avoid sugar, gums, isolates and post-culture heat. “Greek,” “skyr,” “filmjölk” and “labneh” identify style questions, not higher sourcing rungs. Ask how it thickens.

## cream_vs_creamer

- NEW FINDINGS — independent ladder check: [traditional guide](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) supports raw cream before additive-free pasteurized non-homogenized cream; [farm creamery](https://butterworksfarm.com/creamery/) independently demonstrates organic 100% grass-fed heavy cream with limited weekly production.
- NEW FINDINGS — [craft freezing instructions](https://kalonasupernatural.com/new-24-oz-whipping-cream-and-half-half/) advise shaking non-homogenized cream, leaving expansion room and refrigerator-thawing; cube portions are an option for that product. Do not promise that every thawed cream will whip identically.
- NEW FINDINGS — [clotted-cream method](https://www.strausfamilycreamery.com/recipe/homemade-clotted-cream-recipe/) shows a traditional British-style use requiring non-UHT cream and prolonged cooking: clotted cream is not raw or interchangeable with liquid whipping cream. [Holistic creamer review](https://drcate.com/what-is-the-best-non-dairy-milk/) independently objects to seed oils and isolated proteins.
- CORRECTIONS to round 1: Keep the UHT-fallback correction. “Non-dairy” cannot be expanded to “contains no milk protein”: the creamer review discusses casein/whey-containing formulations. Organic/100% feed, whipping performance and heat treatment are separate checks; one clean label does not establish all three.
- QUESTIONS: Can surplus cream be frozen in portions? Will thawed cream still whip? Is clotted cream an unheated alternative? Can a non-dairy creamer contain a milk-derived protein?
- LADDER CHANGES: Best organic 100% grass-fed raw cream; second same sourcing pasteurized non-homogenized cream; floor organic additive-free pasteurized cream, or milk + cream half-and-half for coffee; avoid UHT, oils, sugar/flavors and gums. Read the actual carton's process/list; buy the fat/form the recipe requires.
