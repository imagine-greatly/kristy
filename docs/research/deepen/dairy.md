## grassfed_butter

- GAPS: Decision leads with color rather than the raw/organic/feed ladder; cultured butter and storage are missing. “Skip tubs” excludes packaging without checking ingredients; color and softness cannot establish a no-grain diet.
- FINDINGS: [Craft butter explainer](https://saxelbycheese.com/blogs/cheese-blog/grass-fed-butter-is-better) — pasture carotene influences color, while hay and season change it; color is a supporting observation, not verification of the locked 100% feed standard.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional butter-making guide](https://cheesemaking.com/pages/butter-cultured-butter-recipe-instructions) — sweet cream and cultured cream both make butter; cultures add tang, and refrigeration/freezing preserve flavor and limit absorbed odors.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: named-farm raw butter, organic, “100% grass-fed”; close second: pasteurized butter with the same organic/feed claims, sweet-cream or genuinely cultured; floor: organic butter with a short list when feed is unspecified; avoid: oil-blended spreads, natural flavors, added color and whipped formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for cream, optional salt and, for cultured butter, cultures; do not mistake cultures for flavoring. “European-style” describes a butterfat/style choice, not the feed or organic rung.
- AT SHELF: Compare the ingredient lists and feed statements on two refrigerated packs before comparing yellowness; ask whether winter feed includes grain if “grass-fed” lacks “100%.”
- HANDLING: Choose salted or unsalted for the recipe; protect opened butter from light, air and refrigerator odors, refrigerate and freeze surplus well wrapped. Summer color is not a reason to reject a documented winter churn.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: cultured butter, sweet-cream butter, butter in a tub, winter butter, can butter be frozen, does yellow mean 100% grass-fed?

## cheese_real_vs_processed

- GAPS: Good block preference, but missing rennet sourcing and an edible-rind check. The blanket “process cheese product needs 51% cheese” claim should not be carried forward across differently named products without a matching standard; packaging form alone cannot classify cheese.
- FINDINGS: [Craft rennet explanation](https://help.cheesemaking.com/article/346-chymosin-rennet-what-is-it) — fermentation-produced chymosin uses engineered microbial hosts; “enzymes” does not disclose animal, microbial or fermentation-produced origin. Ask the maker rather than infer it from “vegetarian.”
- FINDINGS: [Cheesemonger handling FAQ](https://www.nealsyarddairy.co.uk/pages/faqs) — cheese paper inside a refrigerated box balances moisture and ventilation; some rinds/coverings are inedible, so “keep every rind for soup” is too broad.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic, verified 100% grass-fed raw-milk block with traditional animal rennet or disclosed non-GMO plant/microbial coagulant; close second: equivalent pasteurized whole-milk block; floor: organic plain block, short list, feed unspecified; avoid: processed cheese formulations, oil-based imitations, coated shreds and smoke-flavored versions.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for milk, cultures, salt and rennet/enzymes; ask what the enzyme is. Skip starch/cellulose coatings and natamycin when an uncoated block is available; cheese sliced from that block retains its rung.
- AT COUNTER: Ask for a small piece cut from a named wheel; confirm milk treatment, feed, coagulant and whether the rind is edible before having it wrapped in cheese paper.
- HANDLING: Refrigerate in its appropriate wrap; grate what is needed. Aged, fresh, brined and mold-ripened styles require different handling—use the maker’s instructions rather than a universal mold-trimming rule.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: animal rennet, microbial rennet, fermentation-produced chymosin/FPC, are deli slices processed, can the rind go in soup, what does “enzymes” mean?

## yogurt_plain_vs_flavored

- GAPS: Decision gives Greek an automatic lead despite the simpler cream-top option; Greek/skyr does not prove traditional straining. Current claims that unnamed cultures mean a dead product, or that a seal alone establishes viability, are too categorical.
- FINDINGS: [Farm-creamery process FAQ](https://www.maplehill.com/faq) — Greek yogurt can be ultrafiltered rather than cloth-strained, and plain cream-top and Greek versions can both have zero added sugar; ask how thickness is achieved.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional yogurt producer FAQ](https://bellwetherfarms.com/knowledge-base/) — unstrained whole-milk yogurt can be thick without gums, and separated whey can be stirred back in; texture and thickness alone do not identify additives.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic, 100% grass-fed plain whole-milk farm yogurt, raw only if the maker confirms the milk was not pasteurized during production; close second: equivalent pasteurized cream-top yogurt with live cultures; floor: organic plain whole-milk yogurt, milk and cultures; avoid: sweetened/light cups, gums, starches, protein-isolate formulations and products heat-treated after culturing.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “plain,” “whole milk,” “cream-top/non-homogenized,” culture information and zero added sugar; choose genuinely strained Greek only when that texture is wanted. A longer ingredient list is not required for thickness.

- HANDLING: Buy refrigerated with an intact seal and usable date; keep cold, use a clean spoon and stir ordinary whey separation back in. Follow the pack’s opening window; no universal “live count” follows from the date.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: pot-set yogurt, cream-top versus Greek, ultrafiltered yogurt, whey on yogurt, is skyr always strained, how can live cultures be confirmed without a seal?

## raw_milk

- GAPS: “Trusted farm” needs concrete questions; pasteurization is also a pathogen-control process, not merely shelf-life technology. Remove the casual sit-out/clabber instruction and the suggestion that cream line, glass or a short life demonstrates microbiological quality; raw describes heat treatment, not automatically homogenization.
- FINDINGS: [Raw-milk farmer checklist](https://www.rawmilkinstitute.org/updates/find-a-good-raw-milk-farmer) — asks about milking/bottle hygiene, water, actual bacterial counts and rapid chilling; its example target is roughly 38–40°F within 45–60 minutes, not “days, not weeks” as a quality test.
- FINDINGS: [Farmer production standards](https://www.rawmilkinstitute.org/common-standards) — farm-specific risk plans, regular coliform/SPC results and herd-health documentation reduce uncertainty but explicitly do not guarantee perfectly safe food; inference: spontaneous souring is no substitute for those controls.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic, verified 100% grass-fed raw whole milk intended for human consumption from a named farm with documented handling; close second: organic 100% grass-fed vat-pasteurized cream-top whole milk; floor: organic pasteurized whole milk; avoid: anonymous raw milk, absent handling information, broken cold chain and UHT/flavored formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for farm identity, bottling date, explicit raw status and feed details; ask about winter hay, any grain and field sprays. Prefer sound reusable glass where practical, without calling glass or a cream line a safety check.
- AT COUNTER: Ask when the batch was milked/bottled, how quickly it was cooled, what recent hygiene results show and how bottles are cleaned; use the pasteurized rung if answers are unavailable.
- HANDLING: Carry home cold and refrigerate promptly; follow the farm’s date and opening instructions. Keep the existing household-suitability cautions; do not add a home clabber recipe without a defined fermentation process.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: farm-gate milk, human-consumption raw milk, raw versus cream-top, milk cooling time, what questions to ask a farmer, is souring a quality test?

- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: named-farm organic 100% grass-fed raw cream; close second: organic 100% grass-fed pasteurized, non-homogenized cream; floor: organic pasteurized cream with no gums/flavors, or milk-and-cream half-and-half for coffee; avoid: UHT cream, sweetened creamers, added oils, carrageenan/gellan and emulsifier formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for cream alone, or milk plus cream for half-and-half; keep heavy/whipping cream for whipping and half-and-half for coffee. “Non-dairy,” “barista” and “zero sugar” do not establish a simple formulation.

- HANDLING: Buy from the cold case; keep cold and obey the opening instructions. Check milkfat and recipe requirements before substituting half-and-half for whipping cream; a shorter date alone does not identify processing.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: heavy versus whipping cream, coffee cream, half-and-half versus creamer, does cream-top mean raw, can half-and-half whip, clean barista creamer?

## raw_kefir

- GAPS: Missing organic/100% feed in the lead and traditional grains versus selected starters. Refrigeration and thickness do not prove raw status, live cultures or farm quality; a yogurt-style culture seal is not a universal kefir definition.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional kefir-culture guide](https://culturesforhealth.com/blogs/learn/milk-kefir-how-to-find-milk-kefir-grains) — grains and direct-set starters are different production choices; grains maintain a bacteria/yeast community. “Kefir” on a bottle does not disclose the starter method.
- FINDINGS: [Milk-kefir process FAQ](https://culturesforhealth.com/blogs/learn/milk-kefir-milk-kefir-frequently-asked-questions-faq) — flavor/effervescence vary with milk and fermentation, small alcohol amounts can occur, and non-homogenized kefir can develop a thick cream layer; none measures quality by itself.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: named-farm organic 100% grass-fed plain whole raw-milk kefir, traditional grains and no post-culture heat; close second: same raw sourcing with a disclosed selected culture; floor: organic plain whole-milk pasteurized-milk kefir with live cultures; avoid: sweetened/flavored bottles, gums and heat-treated finished drinks.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for milk, cultures, raw-milk confirmation and a culture/bottling date; ask about grains and heating before and after fermentation. Do not confuse dried shelf-stable starter grains with a finished bottled drink.

- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: grain-fermented kefir, starter-culture kefir, raw-milk kefir versus raw finished kefir, alcohol in kefir, separated kefir, dried grains versus bottled kefir?

- PROPOSED LADDER: Best overall when suitable: organic raw/100% grass-fed dairy; close second for dairy avoidance: freshly prepared organic almond-and-water milk or organic additive-free canned coconut milk; floor if buying almond: organic unsweetened almond/water drink, optional salt, no oils/gums/fortification; avoid: sweetened/flavored, seed-oil, thickener-heavy and non-organic formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic almonds and water; ask the maker about almond proportion and processing if undisclosed. “Original,” “barista,” “raw almonds” and refrigerated placement do not establish an unsweetened, unheated finished drink.
- AT SHELF: Compare the plain drink and organic almond-only base; read directions to see whether the base can make only the amount needed, then scan every oil, gum and flavor on a ready-to-drink pack.
- HANDLING: Shake natural separation back in; refrigerate prepared/opened milk and follow its specific window. Store concentrate according to its own label—paste and powder instructions differ; no package proves freedom from oxidation.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: almond concentrate/base, almond paste milk, homemade almond milk, almond percentage, why does almond milk separate, raw almonds versus raw almond beverage, how long after blending?

## soy_milk

- GAPS: Decision omits organic and its lower place among plant drinks; non-GMO-only is not a close second to organic under the locked floor. Missing whole-bean versus isolate-based drinks, cooking versus fermentation, and a useful shelf/storage check.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional-food producer’s soy-drink FAQ](https://www.edenfoods.com/faqs/view.php?categories_id=2) — distinguishes unsweetened from syrup/flavor/fortified variants and discusses organic soybean sourcing and aseptic packaging; the exact variety matters.
- FINDINGS: [Whole-food ingredient disclosure](https://support.addjoi.com/en-US/joi-ingredients-23159) — organic whole-soybean ingredients are possible, rather than a soy-protein-isolate formulation; [traditional guide](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) favors fermented soy foods over modern imitation milks.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best overall when suitable: organic raw/100% grass-fed dairy; close second for dairy avoidance: organic simple nut milk or additive-free canned coconut milk; floor if soy is chosen: organic unsweetened cooked whole-soybean-and-water drink, unfortified; avoid: non-organic/non-GMO-only substitution, isolates, oils, gums, sweeteners and flavor formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic soybeans plus water; “non-GMO” alone does not answer crop/spray practices. Ordinary cooked soy milk is not fermented soy; keep tempeh/miso/natto as food alternatives rather than pretend they replace a coffee drink.

## coconut_milk_beverage

- GAPS: Keep the locked can-over-carton preference, but add organic, full-fat versus lite/cream/cream-of-coconut distinctions, separation and opened-can handling. Fixed “one-eighth the fat” and universal carton-additive claims exceed what any one label establishes.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional coconut shopping criteria](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) — specifically prefers additive-free whole canned coconut milk over aseptic beverages; this is the sourcing standard, not proof that every can is additive-free.
- FINDINGS: [Coconut producer’s handling FAQ](https://store.edwardandsons.com/pages/faq) — fat can separate/solidify naturally, guar gum holds an emulsion, and opened milk needs refrigeration in a sealed container; this maker specifies four days and permits freezing, with possible texture change.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best overall when suitable: organic raw/100% grass-fed dairy; close second for dairy avoidance: organic whole canned coconut milk, coconut and water only; floor if a drink carton is required: organic unsweetened coconut/water beverage without oils/gums/fortification; avoid: lite, sweetened cream-of-coconut, flavored/thickened drinks and non-organic products.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic coconut and water, “whole/full-fat,” and no guar/carrageenan/gellan; distinguish unsweetened coconut cream from sweet cocktail “cream of coconut.” Check the panel instead of quoting a universal can/carton fat ratio.

- HANDLING: Reject damaged/leaking cans; stir or gently warm natural separation before portioning. Transfer leftovers to a covered container, refrigerate and follow the maker’s window; freeze portions if directed. Treat lining claims as packaging disclosures, not health guarantees.

## plant_butter

- GAPS: Dairy-first preference is buried and organic absent from the lead; “coconut or olive oil” does not ensure the rest of a blend qualifies. A qualifying commercial spread may not exist at the shelf; provide an executable single-fat alternative rather than imply any front-label oil passes.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional fats shopping criteria](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) — places actual butter/ghee ahead of margarine and blended spreads; apply the locked dairy-first stance without predicting harm from an individual product.
- FINDINGS: [Plant-fat maker’s process disclosure](https://nutiva.com/blogs/news/nutiva-organic-coconut-oil-buttery-flavor) — buttery coconut oil can be refined oil with separately added flavor and annatto; “coconut,” “organic” and “buttery” do not mean single-ingredient virgin coconut oil.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best when suitable: organic 100% grass-fed butter, raw first, or plain ghee; close second for dairy avoidance: organic virgin coconut oil or organic extra-virgin olive oil used directly; floor only if a formulated spread is needed: organic coconut/olive-based spread with every oil qualifying and a short list; avoid: seed oils, hydrogenated/interesterified fats, palm-based blends, flavors and emulsifier-heavy products.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for the complete oil list, optional water/salt and disclosed cultures; a named preferred oil first is insufficient if sunflower/canola follows. “Cultured,” “vegan” and “olive-oil spread” are not feedstock guarantees.

## dairy_case

- GAPS: Current answer describes categories without giving a buying order; organic/feed, heat and ingredients need to lead. Vitamin rules are product-specific, filtration is not pasteurization, and longer life cannot be attributed to filtration alone; “raw tops each type” also fails for cooked ghee/custard.
- FINDINGS: [Small-dairy processing explanation](https://kalonasupernatural.com/simple-methods/minimally-processed/) — vat pasteurization and homogenization are separate operations; even one creamery can use vat treatment for milk/yogurt and HTST for cottage cheese/sour cream.
- FINDINGS: [Creamery label/process FAQ](https://www.strausfamilycreamery.com/faqs/) — whole versus reduced-fat products can differ in added vitamins, and cream-top products still undergo pasteurization; verify the specific pack rather than infer the whole range from a farm image.
- FINDINGS: [Farm-creamery filtration disclosure](https://www.maplehill.com/faq) — yogurt can be ultrafiltered for thickness; inference: read filtration as a separate concentration step, then independently establish heat treatment and ingredients rather than treating “filtered” as a freshness promise.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: type-appropriate organic raw whole dairy from an identifiable 100% grass-fed farm, where available; close second: organic 100% grass-fed pasteurized whole dairy, non-homogenized and least processed for that type; floor: organic plain full-fat dairy with the shortest functional list; avoid: UHT fluid dairy, added sugar/flavors, gums/oil replacements and protein-isolate formulations.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Read in order: food type → organic and 100% feed → actual heat/homogenization → ingredients/cultures → date/handling. “rBST-free,” “A2,” “pasture-raised,” “natural” and “high protein” do not replace the organic/feed/process checks.
- AT SHELF: Turn over two packs within the same dairy type and follow that sequence; ask the dairy buyer for a small-maker organic cream-top or short-list option when the preferred rung is absent.
- HANDLING: Shop chilled/frozen dairy near the end, carry it cold and refrigerate promptly; use pack-specific dates and opening windows, not the shortest date as a quality ranking. Reusable glass is a packaging preference, not proof of the milk process.
- ALIASES / QUESTIONS: dairy shopping checklist, pasture-raised versus 100% grass-fed, filtered versus pasteurized, lactose-free versus A2, which dairy needs added vitamins, does a small dairy use one process for every product?
