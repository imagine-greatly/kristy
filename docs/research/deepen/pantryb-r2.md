## gluten_free_bread
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of the round-1 bakery, [a Saxon craft baker](https://www.baeckerei-spiegelhauer.de/Organic-Sprouted-Buckwheat-Bread-500-g) documents sprouted organic buckwheat/water/salt bread without flour, gums or yeast; its moist, dense loaf needs thin slicing, refrigeration and slice-by-slice freezing.
NEW FINDINGS: [A traditional bread developer](https://breadarchitect.com/real-bread-without-gluten/bread-formulas/basic-buckwheat/) independently demonstrates buckwheat starter, buckwheat flour, water, salt and psyllium with a lengthy fermentation; this corroborates the whole-flour sourdough alternative without making a binder count the quality test.
NEW FINDINGS: [A holistic cook](https://www.terigentes.com/buckwheat-groats-loaf---fermented.html) independently demonstrates soaked, blended and fermented buckwheat groats, with psyllium and optional additions; soaking, sprouting and fermentation are distinct steps, not interchangeable label claims.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1 has no independent comparative support for placing sprouted buckwheat above every well-made whole-flour GF sourdough, or declaring every starch-containing loaf inferior. Its [shared-equipment example](https://thebreadunderground.com/products/gluten-free-brown-rice-buckwheat-and-teff-sourdough-breadlf) remains a valid production question, not a recommended loaf for every gluten-avoiding shopper.
QUESTIONS: Is the buckwheat sprouted, fermented, or both? Does the bakery share wheat equipment? Can slices go straight from freezer to toaster? Why is this flourless loaf much denser than sandwich bread?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic GF whole-groat bread with disclosed sprouting/fermentation OR genuinely fermented whole-flour GF bread; SECOND the same simple base with less process disclosure; FLOOR organic GF whole-flour loaf, shortest functional list; AVOID starch/sugar-led additive-heavy formulas. Exact cues: “organic,” “gluten-free,” “sprouted buckwheat,” named starter, “psyllium husk”; “sourdough” alone answers neither gluten exposure nor fermentation time.

## sugars
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 Big Tree/WAPF, [a coconut-sap producer](https://www.organiccoconutblossom.com/process-organic-coconut-blossom/) documents tapping flower sap, traditional heating, crystallization, oven drying and sifting without additives; coconut sugar is processed by heat, not raw simply because it is brown.
NEW FINDINGS: [An organic maple farm](https://www.meridanhillmaple.com/shop/p/maple-sugar) independently documents its own syrup boiled to granules, one ingredient; [a traditional organic cane processor](https://en.rapunzel.de/organic-product-rapadura-whole-cane-sugar-hand-in-hand--1400140.html) documents pure cane juice retaining its molasses, heated/dried/ground, with dry airtight keeping to prevent clumping.
CORRECTIONS: These independently corroborate sap/cane processing and single-ingredient availability, not an established health advantage of coconut/maple over every whole-cane sugar. Preserve the locked lead as a nature-first editorial choice. “Jaggery” alone does not establish coconut rather than another palm/cane source or an unrefined method; no independent support found for ranking all such forms together.
QUESTIONS: Is palm jaggery made from coconut, another palm or cane? Was the molasses retained or added back? Does coconut sugar count as a raw food? Does powdered maple sugar contain starch or another sugar?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic “pure maple sugar” or “coconut blossom sugar,” sole ingredient and sap process disclosed; SECOND organic “whole cane sugar/rapadura/sucanat,” molasses retained; FLOOR organic named-crop sugar for occasional recipe needs, refining disclosed. AVOID unnamed-crop/non-organic sugar and starch/flavor/sugar blends. “Raw,” “turbinado,” “demerara,” “brown” and “jaggery” need process/crop questions; an organic seal does not mean unrefined or unlimited use.

## maple_syrup
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 Coombs, [a Vermont sugarhouse](https://henpeckermaplefarmvt.com/pages/faqs) documents color/flavor grades depending on harvest timing AND weather; [its product](https://henpeckermaplefarmvt.com/products/quart) demonstrates organic pure syrup in glass/plastic and refrigeration after opening. This corroborates purity/organic/storage/grade meanings, not a darker-is-healthier result.
NEW FINDINGS: Reopened [production disclosure](https://www.coombsfamilyfarms.com/maple-craft/organic-maple-whats-the-difference/) confirms defoamer use can occur during organic boiling; ingredient simplicity and processing-aid disclosure are different questions. No independent second producer source was found this run for the exact defoamer preference.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1's Dark BEST versus Golden SECOND has no comparative health support; darkness belongs to flavor choice. “All consumer maple is Grade A” is US-centric and should not become a universal regional label claim. The new farm product page mixes 2025 product-feature and 2026 harvest-order wording; do not infer a bottle's actual vintage from the page.
QUESTIONS: Does a lighter grade mean less pure syrup? Is a defoamer used despite a maple-only ingredient list? Which harvest/batch is actually in this bottle? Can a larger opened bottle be portioned for cold storage?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic “100% pure maple syrup,” disclosed sugarhouse/process, glass preferred, choose desired flavor; SECOND equally pure organic syrup with less production detail; FLOOR organic single-ingredient pure syrup with origin less explicit. AVOID “pancake/table/maple-flavored” blends and corn/glucose syrups. Read paired words “Golden/Delicate,” “Amber/Rich,” “Dark/Robust,” “Very Dark/Strong”; retain darker as the house taste preference, not a health rung. No “raw maple” premium.

## cooking_oils
NEW FINDINGS: Independent farm evidence from [an organic avocado grower/press](https://bellavado.com/products/usda-organic-culinary-oil) documents cold-pressed, unrefined, unfiltered, additive-free culinary avocado oil; it corroborates the locked affirmative avocado option. [A processor's distinction](https://chosenfoods.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/36711372077715-What-is-the-difference-between-100-Pure-Avocado-Oil-and-Extra-Virgin-Avocado-Oil) shows “100% pure” can also name refined oil.
NEW FINDINGS: [A traditional organic mill](https://www.emilenoel.com/en/products/huile-sesame-toaste-equitable) documents toasted sesame flavor and cooking/seasoning uses; “toasted” describes the seed's earlier heat treatment, so it must not be silently equated with raw even when extraction is cold. [A traditional-fat maker](https://fatworks.com/pages/fat-faqs) independently corroborates pasture/grass sourcing, rendering and differences between lard cuts.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1's [Nourished Kitchen](https://nourishedkitchen.com/my-favorite-minimally-processed-unrefined-fats-oils/) finishing-only sesame use is an author's preference, not a universal technical prohibition; the mill permits cooking. No independent basis establishes its whole fat-family ranking, a single best heat cutoff, or all antioxidant additions as harmful. The 100% grass-fed beef standard remains locked; do not infer finishing from “pasture.”
QUESTIONS: Can an oil labeled pure still be refined? Were sesame seeds toasted before cold pressing? Which oil is being bought for dressing versus frying? Does the pressing statement disclose later bleaching/deodorizing?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic “extra virgin” olive, “virgin” coconut or source-disclosed traditional fat matched to use; SECOND organic pure “cold-pressed/unrefined” avocado, documented by maker; FLOOR another disclosed plain traditional fat or organic cold-pressed sesame for occasional flavoring. AVOID generic blends, solvent-extracted/deodorized industrial seed oils and unwanted additives. Small light-protective bottles, fresh dates and cool/dark storage remain practical preferences; do not attach a universal frying recommendation to “cold-pressed.”

## lard_tallow
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 WAPF/Nourished Kitchen, [a craft renderer](https://fatworks.com/pages/fat-faqs) documents leaf fat's milder flavor and back fat's stronger pork character; both work for baking/cooking. It documents pasture-raised pork and 100% grass-raised beef sourcing, corroborating the feed/cut questions rather than merely the word “tallow.”
NEW FINDINGS: The same [renderer](https://fatworks.com/pages/fat-faqs) adds organic rosemary extract to some lard/poultry fats during rendering; craft/pastured is not synonymous with one ingredient. Read the actual tub, and distinguish an antioxidant addition from hydrogenation or a vegetable-oil shortening blend.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1 supplies no independent evidence that home rendering always beats an equally sourced gentle craft rendering, that only leaf lard makes acceptable pastry, or that every antioxidant addition merits a health rejection. Keep a one-fat-only preference explicit; do not copy the maker's CLA/trans-fat nutrition claims as sourcing proof. Organic and “100% grass-fed/grass-finished” remain separate purchasing requirements.
QUESTIONS: Can back-fat lard make pastry? Does rendering method change pork flavor? Is rosemary extract present despite craft/pasture wording? Is this tallow intended for cooking or only skin care?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic pastured-pork or organic “100% grass-fed/grass-finished” beef fat, gently rendered at home OR by a disclosed maker; SECOND equivalent plain rendered fat with less cut/process detail; FLOOR organic single-animal culinary fat without hydrogenation/oil blends, feed detail incomplete. AVOID “shortening,” “hydrogenated,” vegetable-oil blends and cosmetic-only tubs. Exact form cues: “leaf lard,” “back fat,” raw “suet,” rendered “tallow”; storage follows actual rendering/pack instructions, not an assumed universal shelf life.

## cocoa
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 Terrasoul, [an agroforestry cacao supplier](https://botanicaorigins.com/products/cacao-powder) documents organic single-origin fermented/sun-dried, non-alkalized, additive-free powder; [a wholefood specialist](https://www.forestwholefoods.co.uk/product/organic-cacao-powder/) independently documents organic natural cacao that is fermented AND roasted but not alkalized. “Cacao” therefore cannot promise unroasted.
NEW FINDINGS: [The round-1 producer page](https://www.terrasoul.com/collections/cacao-coconut/products/cacao-powder) reopened and confirms fermentation, sun drying, pressing, ingredient and dry storage; its narrative origin differs from its country-of-origin line. Ask for current batch origin rather than copying a permanent country claim.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1's King Arthur article is a mainstream corporate baking explainer, outside the requested holistic/niche judging set; replace it for ranking. [In On Around](https://www.inonaround.org/lead-cadmium-cocoa-cacao/) is holistic but explicitly leans on Consumer Reports, with government/mainstream references; treat it as a question generator, not independent corroboration of lot-specific metal levels. No independent comparison proves organic/raw/single-origin means lower metals.
CORRECTIONS: No independent support distinguishes round-1 SECOND from FLOOR when both are organic cocoa-only/non-alkalized with limited disclosure; merge them. Keep occasional use and no-alkali as locked stance, without a raw-as-healthier promise or numerical contaminant claim.
QUESTIONS: Can roasted powder still be called cacao? Is this lot's origin different from the website story? Is fat-reduced powder equivalent to nibs or cacao paste in a recipe? Does the maker provide current origin/batch information for metal questions?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST occasional organic single-ingredient “natural/non-alkalized” cocoa/cacao with fermentation/roasting/pressing and origin disclosed; SECOND/FLOOR organic cocoa-only powder confirmed non-alkalized, source detail limited. AVOID “processed with alkali,” “Dutched/black,” sugar-first drinks and added flavors. “Raw,” “unsweetened,” “single origin” or dark color alone cannot complete the process or metals read; match powder type and fat level to the recipe.

## seeds
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 guide/flax seller, [a flax-growing family](https://www.stevensfarm.com/pages/faqs) supports home grinding, cold keeping of ground flax, and brown/golden as broadly similar seed choices; whole-seed storage differs from ground-seed storage. Its natural/roasted offerings do not independently establish an organic rung.
NEW FINDINGS: [An organic sprouted-seed maker](https://goraw.com/products/sprouted-dry-roasted-pumpkin-seeds) documents sprouted pumpkin seeds plus salt, dry roasted without added oil; “sprouted” and “roasted” can coexist. [An organic chia maker](https://nutiva.com/products/organic-chia-seeds) documents whole/ground forms and gel formation, independently corroborating species-specific preparation rather than one soak/drain/dry routine.
CORRECTIONS: The round-1 WAPF shopping guide timed out; no independent second holistic source was found supporting categorical hemp exclusion. Round 1 correctly called it a guide preference but then promoted exclusion into its ladder; hold the hemp judgment instead of implying harm. No independent comparative evidence establishes raw above every plain dry-roasted seed or home-ground above equally fresh well-kept ground flax.
QUESTIONS: Is golden flax better than brown flax? Can sprouted seeds also be roasted? Are whole-seed and ground-seed storage instructions different? Can chia gel replace an egg without a soak-and-dry step?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic fresh whole flax ground as needed, whole/ground chia prepared for the dish, or plain prepared pumpkin/sunflower seeds; SECOND equally fresh sealed organic raw seeds prepared at home; FLOOR organic fresh ground flax or oil-free “dry roasted” seeds. AVOID stale, oil-roasted, sweetened/flavored formulas. “Sprouted/activated” needs the drying/roasting process; resolve the current hemp contradiction, but leave a categorical hemp rung unpublished pending independent support.

## dried_fruit
NEW FINDINGS: Independent of round-1 Blue Mountain/Nutstop, [an organic orchard's drying account](https://www.froghollow.com/blogs/news/dried-fruit-a-delicious-treat-and-a-solution-to-food-waste) documents variety selection and peeling peaches for drying without sulfur; early juicy/stringy peaches may be unsuitable. Harvest variety and slice preparation matter more than a generic sun-dried halo.
NEW FINDINGS: [Its current apricot page](https://www.froghollow.com/collections/dried-fruit/products/organic-dried-apricots) documents seasonal varieties, brown/mahogany unsulfured fruit, cool/dry keeping and longer frozen storage, BUT also citric/ascorbic acid. “Organic/unsulfured” does not guarantee fruit-only; this is formulation evidence, not a product recommendation.
NEW FINDINGS: [A traditional-food supplier](https://healthytraditions.com/pages/raw-freeze-dried-fruits) independently documents organic fruit-only freeze-drying, no sugar/color/preservatives and moisture/oxygen-proof packaging. It corroborates that form's availability; it does not establish freeze-dried fruit as nutritionally below sun-dried fruit.
CORRECTIONS: Round 1's lowest placement of freeze-dried fruit lacks independent comparative support; sun/gentle drying is a craft preference. Nutstop is a general retailer, not an independent holistic authority for health ranking. No support found for the current card's blanket China exclusion. Failed guessed route: `https://www.froghollow.com/products/dried-apricots`; the correct collection/product URL above opened.
QUESTIONS: Can unsulfured fruit still contain acids or oil? Are different peach/apricot varieties chosen for drying? How does freeze-dried texture change once a bag absorbs moisture? Should soft dried fruit be frozen for longer keeping?
LADDER CHANGES: BEST organic fruit-only whole/sliced fruit with grower and drying disclosed; SECOND organic fruit-only unsulfured dried OR freeze-dried fruit, chosen for intended texture; FLOOR organic fruit-only mixed fruit with less method detail. AVOID “sulfur dioxide/sulphites,” sweetening syrups/juice concentrates, oil coatings and fried chips. Read “ingredients” beyond “no added sugar/unsulfured”; acid-treated fruit misses fruit-only without implying acids and sulfites are the same substance.
