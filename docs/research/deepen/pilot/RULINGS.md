# Produce rulings (Devon, 2026-10-25) — binding for the pilot

0. **SUPERSEDES 1-2 where they conflict (Devon, 2026-10-26: "we want as much organic as possible but not wasteful... kristy approved is a very very strict organic and natural, yes to organic pineapple and the rest"):** the default verdict for EVERY produce item is Organic, including peel-discarded ones (pineapple, banana, avocado, melon, mango, citrus, winter squash, onion). "Not wasteful" = a non-organic verdict ("Conventional OK") only when organic is genuinely not a thing on the shelf or adds nothing real (e.g. no organic version is sold; a wild/unfarmed product), and then say what is next-best. Zero "Conventional OK" is acceptable. Natural/closest-to-source still leads: wild, whole, unprocessed, ingredient list is the item alone. Lemon/lime zest and edible-skin squash are simply Organic. Pineapple, papaya, corn, zucchini/yellow squash: Organic (GMO reason in the Why tap).
1. (SUPERSEDED by 0 where it says Conventional OK) **Premise:** "Organic on anything where you eat the skin." Kristy is firmer than EWG's Dirty Dozen: carrots, potatoes, cabbage, mushrooms, asparagus, peas/pods, broccoli, cauliflower are ORGANIC (skin/surface eaten). Conventional OK only where the outer layer is discarded (banana, avocado, melon, pineapple, mango, kiwi, citrus flesh, onion/garlic skins, winter-squash shell).
2. (folded into 0) **Strict organic:** sweet corn, summer squash, papaya = ORGANIC (GMO exposure), even though the husk/peel is discarded. Say the reason in the Why tap, not on the cue.
3. **Frozen is a first-class path, not an afterthought.** Frozen wild blueberries (rarely sold fresh) beat farm-grown cultivated berries; frozen carrots/peas/spinach etc. are legitimate. Go deep: per item, say when frozen is the recommended buy, and the shelf cue (organic seal, ingredient list is the fruit/veg alone, no clumps/frost, "wild"/species word on the bag).
4. **Stance:** nature first, closest to source; organic a floor; holistic sources judge, government = raw fact. Organic is stricter here than the EWG lists; do not cite EWG as the reason for a verdict, only as raw data where useful.
5. **Shape:** one aisle premise (one sentence) + one row per item/group = verdict + ONE shelf cue (<=15 words) specific to that item; no cue may be a generic that fits several items (e.g. "firm, no soft spots" is banned as a cue). Group rows only where items shop identically.
6. **Hard rules still apply:** SHELF-BRIEF.md (shelf-visible only, no 'local'/'small farm'/'unsprayed' wording), no-treatment rule, no prices, no named-brand negatives, no first person. Any comparative claim ("better than farm-grown") must be a sourced composition/practice fact, not a health claim.
