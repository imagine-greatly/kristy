# label_terms — T2 findings, 2026-10-23

All 19 cards’ decision, why, detail and sources reviewed; none has a `cart_pick` field. URLs below were opened and their supporting text read this run. Ladders/actions are proposals applying the locked stance, not claims that a source establishes a universal health ranking. Label cards inherit the relevant food’s freshness, storage and preparation rules. Only this findings file is written, per the single-file instruction; no archive files are created.

## label_natural

- GAPS: Useful warning, but no organic floor or concrete replacement; “barely defined” collapses different product categories. Missing: “Does natural mean unsprayed, organic feed or no added hormones?”
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Natural Versus Organic](https://www.cornucopia.org/natural-versus-organic/) — distinguishes organic production requirements from the much weaker natural claim.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Organic Poultry](https://www.cornucopia.org/organic-chicken-turkey/) — natural poultry does not supply organic feed and farming assurances; pasture/foraging needs separate attention.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food from an identifiable small grower with spray/feed answers; close second: organic plus explicit pasture or 100% grass-fed claims where relevant; floor: organic, minimally processed; avoid: choosing by “all natural,” “farm fresh” or pastoral pictures alone.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for the actual food, organic status and specific production words; skip flavor/color blends and refined oils even beneath a natural headline.
- SHELF STEP: Turn over one “natural” package and locate its organic claim, complete ingredients and producer before comparing it with the plain organic version.

## label_made_with_real

- GAPS: Remove “fourth or later = too small to matter,” “past third is a trace” and “the claim is never a lie.” Order cannot establish a percentage or serving. Missing: real fruit versus concentrate, actual cheese versus cheese flavor, “made with olive oil” versus an oil blend.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Misleading Food Labels](https://chriskresser.com/misleading-food-labels/) — a “made with olive oil” claim can coexist with other oils; the whole ingredient list matters.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Five-Ingredient Rule](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/why-five-ingredient-rule/) — ingredient-count cutoffs are arbitrary; a short list alone does not settle the food’s quality.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole fruit, real cheese or the named food itself; close second: plain organic base with that food added at home; floor: organic preparation containing the named food with a simple list; avoid: sugar/refined-flour/oil formulations sold by one “real” ingredient.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for named fruit pieces/purée, cheese or the specific oil and any disclosed percentage; skip using front pictures or ingredient rank as a quantity measurement.
- SHELF STEP: Find the advertised ingredient, then scan every other ingredient; compare with the unflavored base in the produce, dairy or oil section.

## label_no_added_hormones

- GAPS: “Only third-party verification backs it” wrongly excludes direct farm evidence and conflicts with the brief’s framing. The beef/dairy distinction needs feed and pasture priorities; “hormone-free” must not imply absence of naturally occurring hormones. Missing: rBST/rBGH versus grass-fed; no hormones versus no antibiotics.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Organic Beef](https://www.cornucopia.org/organic-beef/) — animals naturally produce hormones; organic production excludes added hormones and is broader than that single claim.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Organic Meat](https://chriskresser.com/is-organic-meat-better/) — discusses beef growth hormones, dairy rBGH and why organic still needs a separate finishing/feed question.
- FINDINGS: [Food Labels Exposed, via Farmers Guild](https://www.farmersguild.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Food-Labels-Exposed-LOW-RES-v11.pdf) — explains that added hormones are prohibited for pork/poultry, so that absence claim does not distinguish their raising practices.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic beef labeled “100% grass-fed” with finishing confirmed by its rancher; close second: packaged organic + “100% grass-fed”; floor: organic beef/dairy with feed read separately; avoid: making “no added hormones” the entire buying criterion. Keep the locked raw/100% grass-fed milk ladder on dairy cards.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “from cows not treated with rBST” when decoding dairy; skip treating it as an organic, pasture or antibiotic claim. Keep the existing pork/poultry species distinction without using it to recommend conventional products.
- COUNTER STEP: Ask the butcher which farm supplied the beef and whether any grain finishing or growth hormones were used; choose the organic option with the fullest answers.

## label_nonGMO_vs_organic

- GAPS: “Non-GMO Project Verified a close second” drops the organic floor. Remove absolute zero-contamination implications; do not imply every non-GMO statement describes the whole production system. Missing: “Need both seals?” and “Can non-GMO crops still be sprayed?”
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Plant-Based Beverages](https://www.cornucopia.org/plant-based-beverages-in-a-nutshell/) — organic excludes GMO production inputs; non-GMO conventional ingredients do not acquire organic growing requirements.
- FINDINGS: [Real Organic Project, Standards](https://realorganicproject.org/get-certified/standards/) — adds soil and pasture practices beyond the organic baseline.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food from a small grower with explicit soil/spray practices; close second: organic + relevant soil/pasture add-on; floor: organic with a simple food list, without requiring a second non-GMO seal; avoid: using a lone non-GMO badge to upgrade a refined or conventionally grown formulation.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic status on the actual item; skip equating “non-GMO,” “bioengineered disclosure absent” or a butterfly image with unsprayed or minimally processed.
- SHELF STEP: Compare two versions of the same food and locate organic status before considering any additional GMO badge.

## label_cage_free

- GAPS: “Pasture only means anything with a certifier” excludes a farmer who can describe the flock. “Free-range = a door” is too categorical across eggs, meat and programs. Soy/corn-free must not displace organic. Missing: pasture versus pasteurized; shell color, yolk color and grade versus husbandry.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Egg Labels](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/egg-labels-whats-to-look-for/) — distinguishes cage-free, free-range and pasture; encourages finding a grower and asking about the birds’ diet.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Egg Criteria](https://www.cornucopia.org/egg-scorecard-criteria/) — vegetation, outdoor timing, winter housing and flock management matter beyond space slogans.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic pasture eggs from a small flock with rotation and feed disclosed, plus explicit soy-free/corn-free feed if sought; close second: organic + pasture-raised carton with farm details; floor: organic eggs; avoid: cage-free/free-range/vegetarian-fed alone as a complete raising claim.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for real vegetated pasture and organic feed; skip using a dark yolk, brown shell or “Grade AA” as proof of either.
- SHELF STEP: At the refrigerated egg case, read the carton’s farm/feed statement and date; inspect for cracked shells, then ask how outdoor access changes in winter.

## label_grass_fed_term

- GAPS: Keep the locked 100% bar, but the withdrawn-standard/enforcement story is not enough to judge a particular package. Organic and finishing questions need integrating; hay is not grain. Missing: “Does grass-finished alone cover the whole diet?” and “What happens in winter?”
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Organic Beef](https://www.cornucopia.org/organic-beef/) — recommends organic plus “100% grass fed” and separates organic from grass finishing.
- FINDINGS: [WAPF, Splendor From the Grass](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/farm-ranch/splendor-from-the-grass/) — describes pasture-based husbandry and winter hay/silage, supporting a forage question rather than a fresh-grass-only test.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic “100% grass-fed” beef from a rancher confirming forage through finishing; close second: packaged organic “100% grass-fed and grass-finished”; floor: organic beef with diet disclosed, with grain-finished or unqualified grass-fed a lower rung; avoid: assuming any pasture photo or “grass-finished” alone proves the full feeding history.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “100% grass-fed,” named farm and finishing details; skip grain-finished as the lead beef pick. Apply the same 100% bar to dairy feed.
- COUNTER STEP: Ask “Any grain or grain byproducts, including during finishing, and what forage is fed in winter?” Check plain fresh and frozen cuts before seasoned preparations.

## label_pasture_raised_feed

- GAPS: Organic must remain the floor beneath soy/corn preferences; soy-free and corn-free are separate statements. Remove “only meaningful with certifier.” Missing: vegetarian-fed versus insect foraging; grain-free versus pasture; what replaces soy/corn in the ration.
- FINDINGS: [WAPF, Eat Your Eggs](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/childrens-health/eat-your-eggs-and-have-your-chickens-too/) — shows pasture, insects and supplemental feed together; its home ration includes organic corn, so pasture is not a corn-free guarantee.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Egg Criteria](https://www.cornucopia.org/egg-scorecard-criteria/) — treats feed sourcing and soy-free status separately from outdoor conditions.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic, rotated-pasture eggs with a disclosed ration and printed “soy-free” and/or “corn-free” matching the shopper’s preference; close second: organic pasture eggs with ration known; floor: organic eggs; avoid: nonorganic soy-free as an automatic upgrade, or “vegetarian-fed” as evidence of pasture.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for both the pasture and feed statements; skip inferring “corn-free” from “soy-free,” or an all-grass diet from “pastured.” These are preference refinements, not established universal nutrient rankings.
- COUNTER STEP: Ask the egg supplier “What is the complete supplemental ration, is it organic, and do the hens rotate onto vegetation?”

## label_organic_scope

- GAPS: Needs “organic” versus “made with organic” percentages and a whole-food/process ladder. Do not let the soil preference reverse the locked unsprayed hydroponic/greenhouse lettuce pick. “No routine antibiotics” is too loose for describing organic meat production. Missing: organic cookie, permitted sprays, organic versus regenerative.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Bar Report Summary](https://www.cornucopia.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Bars_Report_exec-summary.pdf) — distinguishes ≥95% “organic” from ≥70% “made with organic”; neither turns a formulated snack into whole food. Its [organic-label discussion, printed p. 19](https://www.cornucopia.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Toothpaste_Report_web_reducedsize.pdf) also explains “100% organic” and the exclusion of water/salt from organic-content calculations.
- FINDINGS: [Rodale, Pest Management](https://rodaleinstitute.org/why-organic/organic-farming-practices/pest-management/) — organic-approved pesticide sprays may be used after other controls; organic is not a universal no-spray promise.
- FINDINGS: [Real Organic Project, Standards PDF](https://realorganicproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/21224_2024UpdatedROPStandards-3.pdf) — documents additional soil/pasture practices beyond the baseline organic claim.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food with grower-confirmed unsprayed practices and soil/pasture details; close second: organic whole food with relevant additional practices disclosed; floor: organic, simply prepared food; avoid: “made with organic” or an organic ultra-processed snack as the top pick. Retain unsprayed living greenhouse/hydroponic lettuce alongside local soil-grown lettuce, per the locked exception.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for the organic category on the product itself; skip “pesticide-free,” “100% grass-fed” or “unprocessed” inferences from the seal alone.
- COUNTER STEP: Ask the produce supplier “What sprays were used on this crop, and how was it grown?” Choose seasonally fresh produce or plain frozen organic produce when appropriate.

## label_multigrain_vs_whole_grain

- GAPS: “Whole grain means kernel intact” confuses whole composition with physical form: whole-grain flour is milled. Whole-grain-first cannot prove all grains are whole. Missing: sourdough authenticity, sprouted/stone-ground versus refined flour, freshness after milling.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Understanding Grains](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/understanding-grains/) — multigrain counts grains; the word “whole” and the ingredient list establish the useful distinction.
- FINDINGS: [WAPF, Be Kind to Your Grains](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/food-features/be-kind-to-your-grains-and-your-grains-will-be-kind-to-you/) — favors organic grains prepared by soaking, sprouting or sour leavening and discusses deterioration during flour storage.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole kernels, soaked/cooked, or freshly milled organic whole-grain bread with long sour fermentation confirmed; close second: organic sprouted whole-grain bread with a simple list; floor: organic “100% whole grain,” every grain ingredient checked; avoid: enriched flour-led “multigrain,” coloring and sweetened extruded grain products.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “whole wheat flour,” “whole rye” and actual starter/sprouted grains; skip treating “wheat flour,” “seven grain,” “stone-ground” or brown color as whole-grain proof.
- SHELF STEP: Read every flour in the bakery/packaged-bread list and ask the baker about fermentation time; choose a fresh loaf or freeze portions rather than storing milled bread indefinitely.

## label_lightly_sweetened

- GAPS: Has no holistic sourcing and overstates the panel as a number that “cannot be spun.” Serving basis and sweetener identity still matter. Missing: unsweetened versus no-added-sugar, honey/maple/concentrate, and non-sugar substitutes in a low-sugar product.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Ingredient Cheat Sheet](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Ingredient_Cheat_Sheet_List_-_100_Days_of_Real_Food.pdf) — decodes cane juice, syrups, dextrose and other sugar names; natural-sounding wording does not make them disappear.
- FINDINGS: [Cornucopia, Plant-Based Beverages](https://www.cornucopia.org/plant-based-beverages-in-a-nutshell/) — recommends plain/unsweetened choices while still checking labels.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food with its own sweetness; close second: plain organic base with whole fruit added; floor: organic “unsweetened,” ingredients checked for all sweeteners; avoid: letting “lightly,” “naturally” or “reduced” sweetened substitute for reading the list and serving amount.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for plain yogurt/applesauce and unsweetened nut/coconut fallback drinks; skip syrups, concentrates used for sweetness and sweetener blends when an unsweetened equivalent exists.
- SHELF STEP: Put the plain and “lightly sweetened” versions together; compare ingredients, total/added sugar and equal gram or milliliter amounts before choosing.

## label_no_artificial_flavors

- GAPS: “Natural flavors are still made in a lab” is too sweeping; named extracts and physical extraction are not all the same process. Needs an organic, flavor-free pick and the organic-flavor distinction. Missing: vanilla extract versus natural vanilla flavor; organic natural flavors versus real fruit/spices.
- FINDINGS: [OMRI, Natural Flavors](https://www.omri.org/omri-articles/natural-flavors) — ingredient/process explanation: organic flavor restrictions address solvents and carriers; permissible extraction includes pressing/filtration. Natural flavor and organic flavor are not interchangeable formulations.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: plain organic food flavored with actual fruit, herbs, spices or vanilla bean at home; close second: organic product naming those food ingredients; floor: plain organic product with no added flavor blend; avoid: both “natural flavors” and artificial flavor blends when the same food is available plain.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “cinnamon,” “lemon peel,” “vanilla bean” or a named extract with its ingredients disclosed; skip assuming “no artificial flavors” means no flavor formulation, no carriers or no sweeteners.
- SHELF STEP: Read the flavored product beside its plain counterpart and locate the actual flavor source; move to the spice/fruit shelf if the list supplies only “flavors.”

## label_front_vs_back

- GAPS: “Back had to be checked” implies universal premarket review; neither panel location nor law guarantees perfect accuracy. Front organic/feed claims remain useful when specific. Missing: counter foods without a package, recipes changing, and short-list products that still fail the food ladder.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Read Ingredient Labels](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/video-read-ingredient-labels/) — ingredients reveal formulations that front claims can distract from.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Five-Ingredient Rule](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/why-five-ingredient-rule/) — five is a heuristic, not a quality threshold; food identity still matters.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food from a small producer with practices known; close second: plain organic food in a package; floor: organic simple preparation whose list, serving basis and process fit the food’s ladder; avoid: buying by front adjectives or a five-ingredient badge alone.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for food identity → organic/feed claims → whole list → serving basis → date/storage directions; skip flavor blends, refined oils and split sweeteners that a positive headline obscures.
- SHELF STEP: Turn over two competing packs, read the current labels completely, and ask for the ingredient sheet on deli/bakery foods sold without one.

## label_ingredient_order

- GAPS: Remove “first three are most of the package” as a guarantee and “2% = rounding error.” Small quantities can matter to an avoid list; rank gives no exact percentage. Missing: water first, ingredients inside parentheses and several separately named sugars.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Misleading Food Labels](https://chriskresser.com/misleading-food-labels/) — descending ingredient order and separately named sugars explain why a food-led first ingredient cannot settle the whole formulation.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Read a Nutrition Label](https://chriskresser.com/why-you-should-know-how-to-read-a-nutrition-label/) — numerical ingredient-count/line-length rules are arbitrary; inspect actual ingredients.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic single-ingredient whole food; close second: organic simple recipe of recognizable foods; floor: organic packaged food checked through the last ingredient and subingredient; avoid: refined-flour/sugar/oil-led formulations and unwanted additives anywhere in the list, including a “2% or less” group.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for the named food and all parenthetical ingredients; skip assuming water-first broth/milk is inferior solely from order, or that a late ingredient is absent or insignificant.
- SHELF STEP: Circle mentally every sugar name, read nested lists, then compare the panel’s added sugar at the same serving weight; do not calculate undisclosed ingredient percentages from rank.

## label_third_party_seals

- GAPS: “All use outside auditors” needs program-specific substantiation; “paid for several audits” is demonstrably too broad. Keep scope, but remove testing-as-quality framing and badge stacking. Missing: farm practices without a seal; regenerative versus organic; whether an add-on is required.
- FINDINGS: [Real Organic Project, FAQs](https://realorganicproject.org/faqs/) — its add-on requires existing organic certification and farm visits, with no fee to farmers; supports scope and corrects the blanket paid-audits claim.
- FINDINGS: [Real Organic Project, Standards](https://realorganicproject.org/get-certified/standards/) — the additional questions concern soil and actual pasture, not whether every finished food is minimally processed.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food from a small farm with direct spray/feed/pasture answers; close second: organic whole food with a relevant, understood soil/pasture add-on; floor: organic plus a suitable ingredient/process list; avoid: self-created badges or seal count as the deciding quality measure.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for the program name, specific practice and actual farm; skip inferring soy-free, 100% grass-fed, unrefined or flavor-free from a seal covering something else.
- SHELF STEP: Pick one badge on the pack, identify the practice it covers, and ask the supplier the most important question it leaves unanswered.

## label_cold_pressed_expeller

- GAPS: Decision wrongly makes expeller olive oil interchangeable with extra virgin. Extraction and later refining need separate checks; “cold” does not mean fresh or unrefined in every product. Missing: virgin coconut, cold extraction/centrifugation, harvest/storage and oil blends.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional Cooking School, Coconut Oil](https://traditionalcookingschool.com/food-preparation/which-coconut-oil-should-you-buy-aw072/) — distinguishes virgin/unrefined coconut from mechanically pressed coconut that is subsequently refined; expeller does not mean no later processing.
- FINDINGS: [Traditional producer’s process sheet](https://www.facetas.net/oliveoil) — demonstrates useful disclosures: organic fruit, harvest, prompt milling, cold extraction and protection from heat/light; cited for method, not a brand pick.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic extra virgin olive oil with harvest/origin/process disclosed, or organic virgin unrefined coconut; close second: those same oils with clear cold-extraction/pressing information and fewer freshness details; floor: organic mechanically extracted oil of a suitable type, with any refining disclosed; avoid: solvent-extracted oils, refined seed oils and blends promoted by a token cold-pressed ingredient.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “extra virgin,” “virgin/unrefined,” single oil, harvest/lot and dark glass/tin where appropriate; skip treating “pure,” “light,” “expeller-pressed” or “cold-pressed” alone as a complete quality grade.
- SHELF STEP: Compare type, refining, harvest and storage before the pressing slogan; take a quantity that will be used promptly and store sealed away from heat/light.

## label_sugar_free_substitutes

- GAPS: Sugar-free does not prove a replacement sweetener exists: an inherently unsweetened food may contain none. Remove blanket sugar-alcohol-panel guarantees. “Pure allulose” is not automatically closest to source. Missing: stevia/monk-fruit bulking agents, net carbs, no-added-sugar versus unsweetened.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Erythritol](https://chriskresser.com/erythritol-the-safe-sweetener-thats-anything-but/) — stevia/monk-fruit products can be mostly erythritol; supports checking the blend rather than the headline, without importing disease claims.
- FINDINGS: [Wellness Mama, Allulose](https://wellnessmama.com/health/allulose-sweetener/) — describes commercial starch/fructose conversion, distinguishing an isolated sweetener from the whole foods containing small natural amounts.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food or naturally unsweetened food; close second: plain organic base with whole fruit if sweetness is wanted; floor: organic unsweetened preparation with the full list checked; avoid: routine sugar-free candy/desserts and artificial/sugar-alcohol blends as the default replacement for whole food. Isolated sweeteners are a separate fallback, not the lead pick.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “unsweetened” plus no listed sweetener; skip erythritol, maltitol, xylitol, sucralose/aspartame or hidden blend ingredients when seeking an unsweetened version.
- SHELF STEP: Read every ingredient on the “monk fruit,” “stevia” or “zero sugar” package; choose plain yogurt, fruit or an unsweetened drink instead of solving only for the sugar number.

## label_wild_vs_farm_raised

- GAPS: “Origin can mean where processed” oversimplifies distinct catch/farming and processing information; do not repeat it as a universal legal rule. Country alone does not describe feed/water. Missing: previously frozen, seasonal catch, farmed shellfish versus salmon, and clean-water small-farm exceptions.
- FINDINGS: [WAPF, Is Something Fishy Going On?](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/modern-foods/is-something-fishy-going-on/) — favors wild salmon and frozen wild fish outside season; explains feed pigments in farmed salmon rather than assuming post-harvest dyeing.
- FINDINGS: [WAPF, Redefining Farm-Raised Fish](https://www.westonaprice.org/podcast/redefining-farm-raised-fish/) — a traditional hatchery operator describes why water, feed and management must distinguish farm systems.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: plain wild fish with species, catch area, method and fisherman/supplier known, whole or skin-on when practical; close second: plain frozen wild fish with those details; floor: traceable plain wild canned fish, or a disclosed clean-water farm system evaluated by species; avoid: opaque farmed-salmon sourcing, breading, oil sauces and generic “ocean fresh.” Wild seafood uses a catch/method floor, not an invented organic-wild requirement.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for species + wild/farmed + harvest area + frozen/thawed status; skip treating pinkness, “Atlantic,” processing country or sustainability wording as the full origin story.
- COUNTER STEP: Ask “Where was it harvested, where processed, when frozen/thawed, and what feed if farmed?” Compare the freezer with the thawed counter display; follow the pack’s thawing/storage directions.

## label_serving_size

- GAPS: Only government sources; “every number per serving” ignores per-package columns. Same-category products still need actual weights checked; “large packages” do not universally have dual columns. Remove intent claims about small type. Missing: dry/as-prepared, drained weight and a worked calculation.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Read a Nutrition Label](https://chriskresser.com/why-you-should-know-how-to-read-a-nutrition-label/) — introduces servings, serving size and nutrients, while prioritizing whole-food ingredients over favorable-looking panel numbers.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole food portioned for the meal; close second: plain organic packaged equivalent; floor: organic simple preparation compared at the amount actually eaten; avoid: choosing a refined sweetened formulation because a tiny serving makes its numbers look low.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for gram/milliliter serving size, servings per container and column heading; skip comparing one slice with two, dry with prepared, or a per-package column with a per-serving one.
- SHELF STEP: If the chosen portion is 2.5 labeled servings and added sugar is 6 g per serving, calculate 15 g; compare both foods per 100 g using panel amount ÷ serving grams × 100. These are arithmetic examples, not serving recommendations.

## label_artificial_color

- GAPS: Current card is mostly regulatory detail and lacks a no-color organic ladder. Do not infer titanium dioxide from an umbrella term, or imply every exempt color follows an exception-free naming rule. Current US/EU legal-status details and the precise titanium-dioxide rule were not independently reverified in this holistic-only pass; do not expand them.
- FINDINGS: [100 Days, Artificial Food Dyes](https://www.100daysofrealfood.com/real-food-tips-7-reasons-i-hate-artificial-food-dyes/) — supports avoiding cosmetic coloring and choosing food-based alternatives; no clinical or current regulatory claims adopted.
- FINDINGS: [Kresser, Read a Nutrition Label](https://chriskresser.com/why-you-should-know-how-to-read-a-nutrition-label/) — explains named colors and umbrella ingredient terms; supports scanning the whole list, not identifying an unnamed substance by guesswork.
- PROPOSED LADDER: Best: organic whole/plain food with its own color; close second: organic preparation colored by actual berries, beet or turmeric; floor: organic product with no added color; avoid: numbered dyes/lakes, titanium dioxide and unexplained “artificial color”/“color added” in otherwise comparable products.
- LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for no added color, or an explicitly named food ingredient; skip assuming “no artificial dyes” excludes every added coloring, or that a named plant-derived color makes a sweetened snack a whole food. Route salmon’s feed-pigment question to the seafood card.
- SHELF STEP: Compare the colorful product with its plain organic counterpart and scan to the end of both lists; ask the maker which coloring is present if a group term leaves it unclear.
