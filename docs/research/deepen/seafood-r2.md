## salmon_wild_vs_farmed

NEW FINDINGS — second source for the canned floor: [traditional-food shopping list](https://www.thehealthyhomeeconomist.com/resources/) supports wild salmon and plain olive-oil canned seafood; [traditional-pack canner](https://www.purealaskasalmon.com/why-our-canned-salmon/whats-in-a-can-of-wild-alaska-salmon/) documents salmon with optional salt, edible skin/bones and its own liquid.
NEW FINDINGS — second source for vacuum-pack quality/thawing: [fishmonger's freezing guide](https://fultonfishmarket.com/blogs/articles/how-to-freeze-and-thaw-fish-safely) corroborates Sitka: portion before freezing, exclude air during storage, introduce air before overnight refrigerator thawing; follow the actual pack instructions.
NEW FINDINGS — color exception: [canner FAQ](https://wildplanetfoods.com/pages/faqs) documents naturally white king salmon; [aquaculture producer](https://cookeseafood.com/divisions/cooke-aquaculture/) confirms farmed coho. White flesh does not disqualify wild king, and red coho does not prove wild origin.
CORRECTIONS to round 1: No source establishes fresh small-fisher salmon as healthier than equally well-handled frozen wild; keep them on the same top quality rung. Retain removal of nationwide antibiotic comparisons; no corroborated universal feed formula or nutrient ratio. Sitka's [thawing article](https://sitkaseafoodmarket.com/blogs/culinary/from-the-test-kitchen/how-to-thaw-vacuum-packaged-fish) explicitly leans on government safety guidance: use its handling instructions, not a holistic health verdict.
QUESTIONS: “Can ivory/white king salmon still be wild?” “Should a family buy a whole side or separate portions?” “Does shore-frozen mean worse than frozen at sea?” “Which pink/keta/coho preparation stays moist?”
LADDER CHANGES: BEST “wild-caught” + species + named fishery/boat and prompt chilling, seasonal fresh OR promptly frozen; CLOSE SECOND equivalent origin-named sealed wild portions when boat detail is unavailable; FLOOR “wild Alaska salmon,” ingredients salmon, optional salt, traditional canned pack; AVOID opaque farmed origin and unwanted oil/glaze additives. “Skin-on,” “PBO/pin-bone out,” and “portion” describe preparation, not health grades.
LADDER CHANGES — action: Ask the counter for species, actual catch/freeze/thaw dates and pin-bone status; choose the quantity/form usable without another thaw cycle.
