# pantrya — T2 deepen findings

Research date: 2026-10-23. Reviewed decision, why, cart_pick, sources and supporting copy for all 23 requested IDs. Ladders below are editorial synthesis under the locked brief, not measured rankings of health outcomes. Organic is the floor for agricultural ingredients; it does not apply to water. Small, identifiable growers, mills, bakers and roasters add traceability, not an automatic quality guarantee. Producer links document methods, not brand recommendations.
Every cited URL was opened during this run. Source text was read live; no archive files were written because the task explicitly authorizes only this file.

## rice_arsenic
GAPS: Decision/cart_pick omit organic; the brown-rice soak sounds more decisive than the cited traditional source warrants. Separate growing practices, arsenic exposure and phytate preparation; none is a proxy for the other.
FINDINGS: [Holistic rice discussion](https://chriskresser.com/arsenic-in-rice-how-concerned-should-you-be/) supports California white basmati and cooking in abundant water; it also reports arsenic in organic products. Its older product results cannot establish the contents of today's bag.
FINDINGS: [Traditional grain preparation](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/vegetarianism-and-plant-foods/living-with-phytic-acid/) identifies brown rice as low in phytase and corrects its recipe to discard soaking water; avoid promising that one overnight soak removes its phytate or arsenic.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “organic,” “white basmati” and where the rice was grown, rather than only its packing country; skip flavored rice mixes with added oils and flavorings. Brown/sprouted brown remains a separate, lower rice option, not a demonstrated arsenic solution.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Turn over two plain bags and compare grower/origin information; buy a manageable sealed bag, keep dry and airtight, and rinse, cook in excess clean water, then drain rather than absorb that water.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic California-grown white basmati with a named grower/lot; CLOSE SECOND the same organic variety/origin from a traceable packer; FLOOR plain organic white rice with disclosed growing origin, without a lower-arsenic promise; AVOID non-organic rice, additive-heavy mixes and making brown rice/rice syrup products the everyday grain default.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Does organic rice have arsenic?”, “Does sprouted brown rice solve arsenic?”, “Grown in versus packed in?”, “Does soaking replace draining?”

## oats_steelcut_rolled_instant
GAPS: “Sprouted oats have the soaking step done” conflates soaking with germination; an acidic overnight soak alone is oversold for oat phytate. Steel-cut is not necessarily unheated, and instant need not be flavored.
FINDINGS: [Sprouting producer's oat FAQ](https://onedegreeorganics.com/sprouted-oats-faqs/) describes steam stabilization for its steel-cut, rolled and quick oats; these forms differ in particle size, texture and cooking time, not automatically in retained whole-grain content.
FINDINGS: [Traditional phytate discussion](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/vegetarianism-and-plant-foods/living-with-phytic-acid/) says oats have little phytase and soaking without added rye removes relatively little phytate; rye introduces gluten, so it is not a universal instruction.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic oats as the sole ingredient, optionally “sprouted”; choose the cut for the dish. Skip sweetened/flavored sachets, added oils and a claim that “steel-cut” means raw.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Compare the ingredient lists of a plain quick-oat bag and a flavored packet; choose a resealable bag, keep airtight and dry, and follow its cooking instructions after any traditional soaking step.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic plain sprouted whole-grain oats, steel-cut or rolled; CLOSE SECOND organic groats/steel-cut oats for deliberate soaking/fermenting and cooking; FLOOR organic plain rolled oats, with plain quick oats a convenience fallback; AVOID flavored/sweetened packets and non-organic oats.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Oat groats,” “Irish versus Scottish oats,” “Are steel-cut oats steamed?”, “Is plain instant oatmeal different from a flavored packet?”, “Sprouted versus soaked?”

## nuts_raw_vs_roasted
GAPS: Missing a usable soaked-and-dried top rung, cold storage and package-size guidance. “US almonds sold raw are pasteurized by law” is too broad; “nobody sells a truly raw cashew” is an unnecessary absolute.
FINDINGS: [Traditional crispy-nut preparation](https://www.westonaprice.org/crispy-nuts/) describes salted-water soaking followed by complete drying, and singles out walnuts for refrigeration; [the phytate discussion](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/vegetarianism-and-plant-foods/living-with-phytic-acid/) explicitly says nut phytate reduction from soaking is uncertain.
FINDINGS: [Organic farming advocate's almond explanation](https://www.cornucopia.org/faq/) distinguishes steam/PPO treatment of California almonds and direct-farm exceptions. “Raw” on a grocery bag does not identify the treatment; ask rather than infer.
FINDINGS: [Nut seller's storage instructions](https://blog.nuts.com/safely-store-your-nuts/) support airtight, cool storage and refrigeration/freezing for longer keeping; a warm open display sacrifices that control.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic whole nuts, a sealed bag and “soaked and dehydrated” or “dry roasted,” with nuts and optional salt only; skip seed-oil coatings, sweet glazes and stale bulk-bin nuts.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask how “raw” almonds were treated; steam-treated organic almonds are a practical shelf pick. Buy small, refrigerate walnuts and keep longer-held shelled nuts sealed in the freezer.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST fresh organic soaked-and-completely-dried nuts in sealed packs; CLOSE SECOND organic whole raw nuts for home preparation, with almond treatment disclosed; FLOOR organic dry-roasted nuts with optional salt only; AVOID PPO-treated/non-organic choices, added roasting oils, coatings and rancidity.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Activated nuts,” “Crispy nuts,” “Does raw mean unpasteurized?”, “Steam versus PPO?”, “Do soaked nuts still need drying?”

## honey_adulteration
GAPS: Named local sourcing is useful but does not prove authenticity; “raw means never heated” is too absolute. Organic, hive/harvest traceability, coarse straining and misleading home purity tests are missing.
FINDINGS: [Beekeepers' processing FAQ](https://vermonthoney.org/faq) distinguishes coarse straining from fine filtration and describes raw as avoiding excessive heat; crystallization varies naturally and is not spoilage.
FINDINGS: [Beekeeper's authenticity guide](https://www.artvinbali.com/en/honey-guide/how-to-identify-real-honey) explains why water/flame/texture tests cannot prove purity, and supports checking producer, origin, lot and harvest, then asking where the hives were and whether honey was heated.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic raw honey, a beekeeper/region and lot, and “unfiltered” clarified as coarsely strained; skip honey syrup, honey-flavored blends and vague blends without accountable origin. Local provenance does not replace the organic floor.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask the seller “Where were the hives, when was this harvested, and how warm did it get during extraction/bottling?” Choose a well-sealed jar; accept normal crystals rather than using liquid appearance as a purity verdict.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic raw, coarsely strained honey from an identifiable local beekeeper; CLOSE SECOND the same from a named regional apiary; FLOOR traceable organic raw honey with disclosed origin/packer; AVOID heated/ultrafiltered choices, syrup blends and authenticity claims based only on appearance. Keep it an occasional sweetener; retain the infant exclusion.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Raw versus unpasteurized,” “Strained versus filtered,” “Is crystallized honey fake?”, “Does the water test work?”, “Is local honey automatically organic?”

## beans_dried_vs_canned
GAPS: Remove the price-based “nothing competes” language and “only cooking yourself controls salt” absolute. Add harvest age, sprouted dried beans, split lentils as a deliberate form, and a kidney-bean qualification to the blanket slow-cooker recommendation.
FINDINGS: [Bean grower's FAQ](https://www.ranchogordo.com/pages/faq-frequently-asked-questions) supports asking crop age and storing dry beans cool/dry/dark; heirloom and non-GMO are not synonyms for organic.
FINDINGS: [Grower's cooking instructions](https://www.ranchogordo.com/blogs/recipes/cooking-basic-beans-in-the-rancho-gordo-manner) specify an initial hard boil before simmering and flag kidney beans in slow cookers; soaking alone is not cooking.
FINDINGS: [Sprouting producer's process](https://secondspringfoods.com/sprouting-process/) separates soaking, germination and drying; [traditional shopping tiers](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) put organic dried beans above canned convenience choices.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic dry beans with crop/lot information, or plain organic cooked beans listing beans, water and optional salt; skip sauces, added seed oils and sweetened baked-bean mixtures. Intentionally split lentils are not damaged whole beans.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask which crop the dry beans came from; otherwise compare two plain organic jars/cans for ingredients and “no salt added.” Keep dry beans airtight; freeze portions after thorough cooking. Glass is a packaging preference, while “BPA-free” alone does not describe the replacement lining.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST recent-crop organic dried beans, soaked then thoroughly cooked; CLOSE SECOND organic dried sprouted beans, cooked as directed; FLOOR plain organic cooked beans in glass, or an intact can with disclosed lining, water and optional salt only; AVOID non-organic beans, additive-heavy sauces and treating a soak/low slow-cooker setting as complete preparation for kidney beans.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “New-crop beans,” “Heirloom versus organic,” “Sprouted dried beans,” “Split red lentils,” “Can kidney beans go straight into a slow cooker?”

## flour_basics
GAPS: The “keeps only days” rule is too rigid; bread/all-purpose/pastry names are not whole-grain claims. Clarify that sprouting is a different process from an acidic soak and that “unbleached” alone is an incomplete ingredient check.
FINDINGS: [Small mill's FAQ](https://haydenflourmills.com/pages/faq) describes both stone milling and sifting, identifies untreated flour separately from the milling method, and recommends refrigeration/freezing to protect natural oils; its shelf-life guidance is far longer than a few days.
FINDINGS: [Traditional pantry guidance](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/wisely-stocked-kitchen/) favors fresh organic whole flour and airtight freezer storage; [sprouting producer's process](https://secondspringfoods.com/sprouting-process/) shows germination, drying and whole-kernel milling as separate steps.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for “organic,” “whole wheat/whole grain,” a milling date and one named flour ingredient; select bread or pastry strength for the recipe. Check unbleached white flour separately for bromate, enrichments and other additives.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask the mill “Is any bran/germ sifted out, and when was this milled?” Buy a small sealed bag and keep whole flour airtight and cold; ferment/soak unsprouted flour in a suitable recipe before baking.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST freshly milled organic whole-grain flour for sourdough/soaked baking; CLOSE SECOND fresh organic sprouted whole-grain flour; FLOOR sealed organic whole-grain flour with a preparation/storage plan; limited-use organic unbleached, unbromated white flour sits below the whole-grain preference; AVOID bleached/bromated flour, stale flour and additive-heavy mixes.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Whole white wheat,” “High extraction,” “Bread flour versus whole wheat,” “Does unbleached mean unbromated?”, “What does the milling date change?”

## bulk_bins_buying
GAPS: A full bin does not establish turnover; “bulk spices are usually fresher than jars” is unsupported. Add organic identity, restock/lot visibility, covered dispensing, shared scoops and cross-contact questions.
FINDINGS: [Traditional shopping guidance](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) favors sealed nuts, brown rice and hot cereals; [traditional grain discussion](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/food-features/be-kind-to-your-grains-and-your-grains-will-be-kind-to-you/) flags rancidity in milled flour left in open bins.
FINDINGS: [Spice sourcer's FAQ](https://www.burlapandbarrel.com/pages/faq) points to harvest/packing information and aroma, not an open-bin format, as freshness clues. Its advice does not justify ranking every busy spice bin over a sealed jar.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for an organic item identity, ingredient/allergen information and a clean, closed dispenser; skip flour/nut bins, damp clumps, insects, stale smells and unidentified refills. For allergy-sensitive shopping, ask about shared equipment rather than assuming a dedicated scoop proves separation.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask “When was this lot opened, and do you top up old stock or empty and clean the bin?” Record the item/lot and ingredients with the purchase; keep dry foods airtight and spices cool/dark after returning home.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST traceable organic dry beans/lentils from a clean covered dispenser with credible restock information; CLOSE SECOND the same in a covered scoop bin with clear labeling; FLOOR sealed organic bags/jars when bin history cannot be established; AVOID exposed oily/milled foods, unlabeled bins and using fullness as proof of freshness.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Gravity dispenser,” “Can old and new stock be mixed?”, “Are bulk spices fresher?”, “How do bulk allergen warnings work?”, “Can a jar be tared?”

## whole_spices
GAPS: Six months is not a universal ground-spice deadline; the card needs harvest/packing clues, dampness checks and storage away from steam. Organic does not mean a spice has received no heat treatment.
FINDINGS: [Direct spice sourcer's FAQ](https://www.burlapandbarrel.com/pages/faq) supports checking packing information, aroma/color and dry, dark storage away from heat/humidity; useful life varies by spice and plant part.
FINDINGS: [Spice producer's processing explanation](https://www.frontiercoop.com/blog/all-about-our-spices) describes steam treatment instead of irradiation/ethylene oxide; a disclosed steam step should not be confused with chemical fumigation or a stale product.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic whole peppercorns/seeds/bark, named origin and a small sealed container; for powders such as turmeric, choose a recent small jar with one spice ingredient. Skip flavors, fillers, added oils and damp/caked stock.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Compare packing dates and ask when the whole-spice lot was harvested; use a store-provided sample for aroma rather than opening retail jars. Grind only the portion needed, cap promptly and store away from the stove, sink and fridge condensation.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST recently harvested organic whole spices from an identifiable grower/sourcer, sealed; CLOSE SECOND dated organic whole spices from a traceable regional source; FLOOR small sealed organic single-spice powders; AVOID irradiated/fumigated choices, additive blends, stale odorless stock and bulk purchases that outlast use.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Steam-treated spices,” “Harvest date versus packed date,” “Is caked spice damp?”, “Do powdered spices expire at six months?”, “Whole turmeric versus powder?”

## rancidity_check
GAPS: The decision reads only as an olive-oil card despite nuts/flour aliases. “Nothing on the label predicts it” dismisses useful age clues, and “bitter” alone wrongly catches fresh olive oil. Two months is a buying target, not a universal spoilage threshold.
FINDINGS: [Olive-oil sensory educator](https://www.oliveoiltimes.com/world/good-olive-oils-gone-bad/8900) distinguishes crayon/putty/stale-nut defects from fresh bitterness/pepperiness and treats harvest age, light, air and temperature as relevant clues.
FINDINGS: [Traditional pantry guidance](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/wisely-stocked-kitchen/) recommends airtight freezer storage for flour; [nut seller's guidance](https://blog.nuts.com/safely-store-your-nuts/) supports cold, sealed nut storage. Neither supports one fixed deadline for all fatty pantry foods.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for dated, protected organic oil and sealed organic whole nuts/flours; skip leaking packs and paint/crayon/stale-fat odors. Smell flour rather than prescribing a raw-flour taste; mold, moisture or infestation are separate reasons to reject it.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: At an oil counter, request a fresh sample and learn the difference between grassy/peppery and stale/greasy; at home compare a suspect bottle with a fresh one. Mark opening dates, cap tightly, keep oil cool/dark and nuts/flour cold.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST fresh organic foods with process dates and protective packaging; CLOSE SECOND the same with reliable storage history but no process date; FLOOR an intact organic package followed by an opening-time smell check; AVOID rancid stock regardless of date/organic claim. A sealed small bottle sized for roughly two months is a practical target, not a guarantee.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Peppery olive oil versus rancid,” “Can flour go rancid before its date?”, “Is freezer storage useful?”, “Can a printed date guarantee freshness?”

## olive_oil_grades
GAPS: “Extra virgin uses no heat” is inaccurate; remove the price flag under the brief and dated imported-oil failure percentages as today's shopping shortcut. Add actual harvest season, filtered/unfiltered distinction and opened-bottle size.
FINDINGS: [Olive-oil specialist Richard Gawel's FAQ](https://www.aromadictionary.com/oliveoilfaq.pdf) defines extra virgin through mechanical extraction without excessive heat, additives or solvents; modern centrifugation is compatible with extra virgin, so “first pressed” is not required.
FINDINGS: [Small producer's process disclosure](https://www.stonehouseoliveoil.com/products/select-evoo) explicitly describes cold extraction, filtration and a harvest window. Filtration is not refining; cloudiness is not a required sign of a closer-to-source oil.
FINDINGS: [Olive-oil sensory guidance](https://www.oliveoiltimes.com/world/good-olive-oils-gone-bad/8900) supports harvest-date scrutiny and tasting; [producer storage advice](https://www.stonehouseoliveoil.com/pages/faq) supports dark glass, cool storage and a quantity used promptly after opening.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic “extra virgin olive oil,” a named grove/mill or traceable growing origin, recent harvest and dark glass/tin; skip “light,” “pure,” refined/pomace oils and interpreting “packed in Italy” as olive provenance. Do not reject a disclosed blend merely for having several origins.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Find the actual harvest date and growing origin on the back; compare the latest available crop, not a fixed calendar-year promise. Buy a size used in roughly two months, cap tightly and keep away from the stove/light.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic EVOO from an identifiable small grower/mill, recent harvest and protected package; CLOSE SECOND organic EVOO with traceable olives/harvest from a larger packer; FLOOR organic EVOO in dark glass/tin with clear provenance and best-before information, accepting weaker age visibility; AVOID refined/light/pomace choices and stale oil.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Filtered versus refined,” “Cold extracted versus cold pressed,” “Which harvest is newest?”, “Does a country blend mean fake?”, “Is cloudy olive oil better?”

## nut_butter_ingredients
GAPS: Needs soaked-nut butter above ordinary raw/roasted jars, explicit organic in the pick and a distinction between ingredient evidence and visible separation. “No oil layer means additives” is not a reliable reverse inference.
FINDINGS: [Traditional nut/butter tiers](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/) favor organic soaked/sprouted nut preparations; [maker's nut-butter FAQ](https://www.maranathafoods.com/health-facts/nut-butter-faqs/) explains separation, palm-oil stabilization and stirring before refrigeration.
FINDINGS: [Maker's separation explanation](https://www.nuttzo.com/pages/separation-faq) supports keeping and stirring the nuts' own oil rather than pouring it away; separation is normal behavior, not a complete ingredient audit.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic nuts and optional salt only, ideally “soaked and dehydrated”; read every crunchy/smooth variant independently. Skip added palm/seed oils, sweeteners, flavors, protein isolates and reduced-fat spreads; this is an ingredient preference, not a claim that palm is a seed oil.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Read the ingredients before inspecting the oil layer; choose a manageable sealed jar, stir through the bottom after opening, then refrigerate according to the maker. A grind-in-store machine still requires nut freshness, organic identity and cleaning information.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST fresh organic soaked-and-dried nut butter, nuts plus optional salt; CLOSE SECOND organic raw nut butter with treatment disclosed; FLOOR organic dry-roasted peanut/other nut butter with no added fat/sweetener; AVOID stabilized/sweetened spreads, unidentified machine mixes and stale butter.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Activated almond butter,” “Is fresh-ground always better?”, “Should the oil be poured off?”, “Does no separation prove palm oil?”, “Crunchy versus smooth ingredients?”

## grains_beyond_rice
GAPS: The existing variety/gluten map is useful; add hulless barley, raw groats versus roasted forms, actual crop/lot freshness and a preparation plan by grain instead of one universal overnight-soak promise.
FINDINGS: [Traditional mill's farro instructions](https://ansonmills.com/recipes/624/download) explain pearling as removal of bran after hulling, and use an overnight soak for intact farro; [organic grain producer's hulless-barley explanation](https://shilohfarms.com/hulless-barley-organic/) describes a loosely attached hull without equating it to pearling.
FINDINGS: [Sprouting producer's process](https://secondspringfoods.com/sprouting-process/) says different seeds require different soaking/germination times; [quinoa grower's FAQ](https://britishquinoa.co.uk/faqs/) shows that some non-bitter varieties lack the usual saponin-rinsing requirement. Rinse ordinary quinoa unless its producer explains otherwise.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic “whole farro,” “hulled/hulless barley,” “buckwheat groats,” millet or sorghum, with one grain ingredient. Ask whether farro is semi-pearled; buckwheat is not wheat, while barley/farro remain gluten grains. Roasted groats are a cooking choice, not the form to buy for sprouting.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Choose one intact organic grain and read its actual preparation directions before buying; keep a sealed manageable quantity cool/dry and rotate crops rather than replacing every rice meal with one new grain.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST traceable organic sprouted whole grains with disclosed preparation; CLOSE SECOND recent organic intact whole/hulled grains to soak/ferment and cook appropriately; FLOOR sealed organic whole grains from a packer with clear grain/form information; AVOID non-organic or flavored instant grains, refined/pearled forms as the default and treating ordinary parboiled bulgur as sprouted.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Hulless versus hulled barley,” “Semi-pearled farro,” “Buckwheat groats versus kasha,” “Do all grains need the same soak?”, “Prewashed quinoa?”

## bottled_water_buying
GAPS: Preserve named-spring/glass preference, but remove “purified is whatever came out of the tap” as a universal fact. Glass is not a promise about the cap; “unpainted cap” lacks an executable, maker-verified shelf check. The mineral-water definition also needs separation from the spring-water category.
FINDINGS: [Source-bottler's disclosure](https://www.mountainvalleyspring.com/pages/spring-water) identifies its spring region and glass formats; this supports checking actual provenance and available packaging, not inferring water quality from bottle shape.
FINDINGS: [Spring producer's sourcing/handling FAQ](https://www.evian.com/en_us/faqs/) describes protected-source collection, direct piping, naturally present minerals and storage away from heat/odors; a named spring can have a disclosed handling process without becoming an anonymous source.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for plain “spring water” or “natural mineral water,” an actual source/place, intact seal and disclosed handling; glass when available. Skip flavored/vitamin/alkaline marketing substitutes and generic mountain imagery without provenance. Naturally sourced minerals and added “minerals for taste” are different ingredient stories.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Turn the bottle around and locate the source rather than the distributor address; for sparkling, read for water and carbon dioxide only. Check bottle/cap integrity, buy from stock stored out of sunlight and keep it cool; ask the bottler about closure materials instead of promising a particle-free package.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST traceable source-bottled spring/natural mineral water in glass with handling/closure information; CLOSE SECOND the same plain source water in glass with less closure detail, still or sparkling; FLOOR sealed plain water from an identifiable spring in the available container; AVOID untraceable sourcing and sweetened/flavored/enhanced substitutes. Organic is inapplicable here.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Natural minerals versus minerals added for taste,” “Bottled at source versus distributed by,” “Is sparkling mineral water the same as flavored seltzer?”, “Can glass have a plastic-lined cap?”

## sandwich_bread
GAPS: The grain/process/additive checks are already strong; add a realistic bakery/freezer route, ask about processing aids and avoid treating a long list of recognizable whole foods as equivalent to a long additive list.
FINDINGS: [Traditional grain guidance](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/food-features/be-kind-to-your-grains-and-your-grains-will-be-kind-to-you/) supports organic whole-grain sourdough/sprouted bread; [Real Bread Campaign's ingredient definition](https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/what_is_real_bread/) separates additives/processing aids from optional ordinary food ingredients.
FINDINGS: [Traditional sourdough shopping discussion](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/make-your-own-sourdough-bread/) names conditioners, added gluten and seed oils to inspect; [sprouting bakery's FAQ](https://www.foodforlife.com/about_us/faq) documents frozen/cold retail distribution and package-specific storage instructions.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic whole grain leading the grain list, genuine starter fermentation or sprouted whole grain, and flour/grain, water, salt plus culture. Skip added gluten, isolated bran, soy flour, seed oils, syrup and conditioners such as DATEM/calcium propionate; seeds themselves do not establish a whole-grain loaf.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Check bakery and freezer cases, then ask “Which grains are whole/sprouted, how is the dough fermented, and are conditioners or processing aids used?” Buy a loaf sized to use fresh or slice/freeze promptly; follow its thaw/storage directions.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST fresh organic whole-grain starter bread from a baker/mill who explains the process; CLOSE SECOND organic sprouted whole-grain bread without additives; FLOOR simple organic whole-grain yeast bread; AVOID non-organic/refined-flour loaves, added seed oils/gluten and bread sold on “wheat,” brown color or seeds alone.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Seeded versus whole grain,” “Malted loaf,” “Dough conditioners,” “Processing aids,” “Can good sandwich bread come frozen?”, “Can a small bakery use a premix?”

## sourdough
GAPS: Organic is absent from the headline/cart_pick, and the eight-hour phytate statement reads as a universal retail-loaf guarantee. The starter's presence, flour composition and final-dough fermentation are three separate checks.
FINDINGS: [Real Bread Campaign's sourdough guide](https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/sourdough/) defines its genuine-sourdough standard around live starter alone and explains why added acid/powder/yeast cannot establish that process; sourness, holes and a rustic crust do not answer the process question.
FINDINGS: [Traditional sourdough discussion](https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/make-your-own-sourdough-bread/) supports checking whole flour and additives; it does not establish a fixed fermentation time that guarantees a particular phytate result in every purchased loaf.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic whole wheat/rye, water, salt and a live starter; skip sourdough flavor/concentrate, vinegar/acid used to mimic the process, added commercial yeast for the traditional top rung and conditioner-heavy premixes. Starter can be implicit in flour/water labeling, so absence of that word is a reason to ask.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask “Does live starter raise the final dough by itself, for how long, and is that time warm or refrigerated?” Choose a fresh loaf with a storage plan; slice/freeze the portion that will not be used promptly. Retain that wheat/rye sourdough contains gluten.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic freshly milled whole-grain sourdough with explained starter-only fermentation; CLOSE SECOND organic whole-grain starter-only loaf from bagged flour; FLOOR an organic mixed whole/refined-grain starter-only loaf with proportions disclosed and no additives; AVOID acidified yeast bread passed off as traditional sourdough and non-organic/additive-heavy loaves.
ALIASES / QUESTIONS: “Levain,” “Naturally leavened,” “Sourdough powder,” “Does sour taste prove fermentation?”, “Does a 24-hour claim include the final dough?”, “Is sourdough yeast-free?”

## bagels
GAPS: Much of the current answer concerns chew rather than the healthiest purchase. Organic needs to reach the decision; add a whole-grain fallback, an ingredient-sheet request and freezing guidance. “Sourdough needs fat” is not a defining rule.
FINDINGS: [Traditional sourdough-bagel recipe](https://www.westonaprice.org/sourdough-bagels/) uses starter, organic whole/refined flours and a boil before baking; it demonstrates that a sourdough label or boil alone does not establish 100% whole grain.
FINDINGS: [Real Bread Campaign](https://www.sustainweb.org/realbread/what_is_real_bread/) distinguishes starter-only sourdough from ordinary yeast bread and additives from ordinary optional ingredients. Apply those process questions before judging color or a shiny crust.
LOOK FOR / SKIP: Look for organic whole wheat first, starter or sprouted whole grains, and a short comprehensible list; “whole wheat” on the sign still needs flour proportions. Skip added vital wheat gluten, soy flour, bleached/enriched flour and seed oils; “everything” describes toppings, not the dough.
SHELF STEP / STORAGE: Ask for the dough ingredient sheet, whole-grain proportion, leavening method and whether the bagels are boiled; buy fresh or frozen with an intact bag, slice extras and freeze. Boiling is a traditional texture step, not a substitute for these checks.
PROPOSED LADDER: BEST organic whole-grain starter-only, boiled bagels without conditioners; CLOSE SECOND organic sprouted whole-grain bagels with no added gluten/oils; FLOOR simple organic whole-grain yeast bagels; AVOID refined/additive-heavy choices and selecting solely by “bakery,” dark color or boil claims.

Nut flour is a separate branch, not a reason to replace every grain food.
