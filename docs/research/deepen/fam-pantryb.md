Common sourcing instruction for every RULE below: prefer the small grower or maker with origin, harvest or production details printed on the pack or shelf; use seasonal local ingredients where the crop permits. Skip the anonymous large-scale commercial pattern—year-round uniform supply, distant origin and heavily branded packaging that supplies no useful grower/process information—in favor of the disclosed source. Those visible traits implement the requested sourcing stance; they do not establish a contaminant or health finding. Stored grains, tropical crops and traditionally imported foods do not acquire a fictitious local growing season.

### F38 Fermented soy foods

RULE (true for all members):
- BEST: plain **organic soybeans**, an identified fermentation process and a small maker's origin/batch disclosure; inspect the actual culture, grain and seasoning ingredients. Soybeans plus culture can be sufficient for some members; salt/koji is not a universal recipe. [38a] [38b] [38c]
- CLOSE: equivalent plain organic ferment with less grower/aging detail; choose the product's documented chilled, frozen or other instructed format, rather than ranking all frozen food below fresh. [38b] [38c]
- FLOOR: organic fermented soy food with a fully named formula and clear storage/preparation directions. Pasteurized products remain fermented; **unpasteurized** does not establish ready-to-eat status. [38a] [38d]
- SKIP: flavored substitutes whose sauce obscures the simple ferment, and **non-GMO** as a replacement for the required organic wording; inspect separate seasoning packets as well as the main pack. Apply the global ingredient screen to additions, not to whole soybeans or fermentation cultures. [38a] [38b]

BEYOND THE STORE: A small regional fermentation maker or co-op cold case can supply plain organic soy foods; the cited producer documents organic tempeh and natto. Read its product panel rather than assuming all variants share one recipe. [38b] [38c]

SEASON/STORAGE: No common harvest-season purchase rule. Follow each pack's storage, thawing and cooking directions; neither one refrigerator lifespan nor “eat all ferments uncooked” belongs in this family. [38a] [38c] [38d]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Link the [global ingredient avoid list](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list). Family-specific check: inspect grain and sauce subingredients; a fermentation claim does not erase added syrup, oil or flavoring. [38b]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Which soy food is this? Is the soybean ingredient organic? What grain or culture is included? Is this plain food or a seasoned kit? Does this particular product require cooking?

SOURCES:
- [38a] — Traditional miso process, organic ingredient panel, barley disclosure, aging and late addition in cooking.
- [38b] — Plain organic tempeh ingredients, cold storage and materially different seasoned variants.
- [38c] — Organic natto ingredients and product-specific chilled/frozen handling.
- [38d] — Unpasteurized tempeh requires cooking; normal black spores versus slimy/off-quality condition. Used for process/handling, not certification of the maker's finished product.

ITEM DELTAS:
- `miso`: Prefer **unpasteurized**, naturally aged paste; identify soybeans, salt, koji and any rice/barley. “Hatcho” alone does not establish grain-free: an opened panel includes barley. Use the actual salt panel rather than making red color a universal saltiest ranking; follow storage instructions and add near cooking's end when retaining the unheated character matters. [38a]
- `tempeh`: Choose a firm, cohesive cake and read cooking directions. White fungal binding and occasional black spores are compatible with normal tempeh; remove the current blanket “noticeable mold” rejection. Whole/small/large bean choice is a form preference, not an independently demonstrated health ladder. [38c]

### F39 Vinegar: source, process and acidity

RULE (true for all members):
- BEST: organic fermentation with source/process disclosed by a small maker; choose the appropriate **raw/unpasteurized** table-vinegar branch or the **traditionally aged balsamic** branch. These are parallel culinary routes. [39a] [39b]
- CLOSE: organic vinegar with the same relevant process claim and named fermented source, but less orchard or barrel detail. Read **apple cider vinegar**, **wine vinegar**, or **grape must** as applicable. [39a] [39b]
- FLOOR: plain organic food vinegar with ingredients and acidity disclosed; raw status is an additional requirement for the specifically raw-cider purchase, not for every member. [39a] [39c]
- SKIP: replacing the intended vinegar with sweetened shots, **glaze**, undisclosed flavor/color mixtures or cleaning products. For preservation, buy the type and acidity required by the tested recipe; mother/cloudiness does not supply an acidity specification. [39a] [39c]

BEYOND THE STORE: Orchard-direct vinegar and a traditional vinegar maker are real routes; inspect the bottle's organic and process declarations rather than relying on an orchard picture. [39a] [39b]

SEASON/STORAGE: No shared requirement to buy a newly harvested product: aging is intentional in balsamic. Keep the bottle closed and follow its storage instructions; the opened cider-farm source specifies cool storage away from sunlight. [39a] [39b]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Link the global ingredient screen. Keep vinegar itself and named culinary acids outside a blanket “no acid” exclusion. Rawness, age, acidity and added sweetening are separate checks. [39a] [39b] [39c]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Is this for dressing or preservation? What was fermented? Is this vinegar raw, aged, or both explicitly documented? What acidity is printed? Is a thick balsamic actually a sweetened glaze?

SOURCES:
- [39a] — Orchard vinegar's raw/unfiltered/unpasteurized process, acidity and storage; medicinal claims and dosing excluded.
- [39b] — Traditional balsamic starts with cooked must and undergoes barrel aging; older alone is not the entire quality test.
- [39c] — Craft vinegar maker distinguishes acidity levels and culinary/preservation uses; not a substitute for a tested preserving recipe.

[39c]: https://supremevinegar.com/vinegar-acidities-levels-usage/

ITEM DELTAS:
- `raw_cider_vinegar`: Require the combined **organic**, **raw/unpasteurized**, **unfiltered**, **with the mother** disclosures for the preferred raw version. Whole-apple source and glass are preference details; “no added water” is not independently established as healthier. Read the actual acidity; an opened organic raw example is below 5%. [39a]
- `vinegar`: Aged balsamic cannot inherit a never-heated requirement because its must is cooked. Keep simply formulated must/wine-vinegar balsamic distinct from traditional must-only production. Replace “5 percent for all pickling” with recipe-specific vinegar type and acidity; no universal raw-vinegar requirement for preservation. [39b] [39c]

### F40 Bone-broth bases and soups

- CLOSE: a corresponding small-maker chilled/frozen broth or soup with the recipe disclosed; read inside the **broth/stock** sublist, including every animal species. [40b]
- FLOOR: organic actual bone broth, or soup built on it, with a short fully named formula. Read ingredients independently of whether the container is a jar, can or carton. [40a] [40c]
- SKIP: flavor-powder substitutes presented as equivalent to simmered bones, and formula additions excluded by the global list. **Water** first is not a rejection cue; **bone broth**, **collagen** or a cold gel cannot establish the farm/feed source. [40a] [40b]

BEYOND THE STORE: Source suitable bones through a pasture-based farm or butcher; a home stock pot is the practical alternative to packaged bases. Meaty joints, poultry frames and feet are useful selections, rather than marrow bones alone. [40a]

SEASON/STORAGE: No common vegetable season or unopened storage temperature. Read the pack's chilled/frozen/shelf-stable instructions and opened-use limit; do not transfer a shelf-stable unopened condition to opened broth. [40b] [40c]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Link the global soup/prepared-food ingredient screen. Inspect **MSG**, **yeast extract** and **hydrolyzed protein** as separately named ingredients; do not describe them as identical. Cube shape alone is not a defect: actual frozen concentrated stock exists. [40b] [40d]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Is the flavor from bones or a substitute? Which species are in the broth? Is water-first acceptable? Is this cube frozen stock or powdered bouillon? Does the source disclose the animals' raising method?

## AISLE BRIEFING

1. **Pantry/bulk and drinks:** start with the actual grain, botanical, cocoa form or broth base; organic is the floor, and useful source/process disclosure determines the preferred rung. Use the specific family rather than one freshness rule. [40a] [43a] [44a] [48a]
2. **Ferments and condiments:** choose the food/process first, then read grains, acids, fats and sweeteners. Fermented soy, vinegar, salsa and oil-based sauces do not share one rawness or refrigeration rule. [38d] [39b] [41b] [42e]
3. **Bakery, breakfast and snacks:** distinguish prepared whole-grain baking, plain muesli, home-popping kernels and occasional cocoa foods; their sweetening and preparation allowances differ. [45a] [48a] [49b] [50a]
4. **Deli and frozen:** read the full dressing/recipe and follow the particular product's cold-chain and use instructions; do not infer the formula from a cold case or freezer placement. [42b] [42e] [50b]
5. **Across these aisles:** use the one [global ingredient avoid list](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list) with its exceptions, and retain the printed source/ingredient/handling evidence rather than replacing it with front-panel claims.
