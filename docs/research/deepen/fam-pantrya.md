# fam-pantrya — T2 family findings

2026-10-23. Scope: F25–F33, F07, F34–F36; 34 member cards. Findings for the separate card builder, not finished card copy. Read against FAMILY-BRIEF.md, FAMILIES.md, both research rounds for pantrya–pantryd and dairy, the locked completeness decisions, and the current member entries in the KB.

**Evidence convention.** Best/close/floor/skip are the brief's editorial preferences, not demonstrated comparative health outcomes. Organic remains the floor; small seasonal local growers and makers lead where the crop exists locally. The commercial-pattern skip means anonymous long-distance sourcing, year-round standardized supply and prominent pack branding **without** useful crop, source or process disclosure; it is not a contamination claim. A preserved pantry food need not disappear outside harvest season. Producer pages establish their disclosed methods, not the quality of every comparable product. These preferences broadly align with the [traditional shopping framework][P]; the stricter organic floor and local-first ordering come from the brief.

**Shared screen.** All families refer to the [global ingredient avoid list](FAMILIES.md#one-shared-ingredient-list-avoid-list), with its exceptions. Family-specific exclusions below are formulation preferences, not diagnoses. Whole seeds are not the same as extracted seed oils; traditional lime treatment, starter cultures and intentional sweeteners have scoped exceptions. A source link beside a proposed rung supports the food/process distinction; it does not independently prove the entire editorial ranking.

### F25 Grains for soaking, sprouting or cooking

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Look for “organic,” the actual whole-grain name, a grower/harvest or lot, and a stated preparation route. Prefer a small local seasonal grower where applicable; documented sprouting and suitable home soaking/fermentation are alternative routes, not a universal ranking against each other. [Germination and drying][G1]
- **CLOSE:** The same plain organic grain from a disclosed regional source; read “whole,” “unpearled,” or the appropriate hull-removal description, plus preparation instructions. Do not infer whole grain from an ancient-grain name. [Mill's whole-kernel distinctions][G2]
- **FLOOR:** A sealed plain organic grain, identified by species and form, with usable cooking directions; preparation and crop detail may be less complete. The pack must still meet any identity-specific delta below. [Grain storage][G3]
- **SKIP:** Sweetened/flavored grain mixtures, refined forms passed off as whole, and the anonymous commercial pattern above. Do not turn “quick cooking” into a universal exclusion: oat cuts and traditionally finished wild rice need separate interpretation. [Oat forms][G4]; [wild-rice forms][G5]

BEYOND THE STORE: Regional grain farms/mills and their co-op shelves can expose whole-kernel and milling information; wild-rice harvesting communities are a distinct sourcing route, not evidence of local availability everywhere. [G2], [G5]

SEASON/STORAGE: No shared harvest month or soaking duration. For the **dry pantry formats**, keep sealed, cool and dry; whole flour benefits from airtight cold storage. Fresh wet sprouts require a separate handling route and should not inherit pantry storage. [G1], [G3]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Global screen plus undeclared refinement and seasoning systems. Do not transfer the standalone rice or puffed-cereal decisions into this family. [G2], [G4], [G5]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Is the grain whole or pearled? Does sprouted mean merely soaked? Are plain instant oats allowed? Is wild rice actually wild harvested? Does every grain need the same soak?

SOURCES:

- [G1] https://secondspringfoods.com/sprouting-process/ — soaking, germination, drying and whole-seed milling are distinct; timing varies by seed.
- [G2] https://www.janiesmill.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions — whole kernel, sifting and extraction; farm/mill route.
- [G3] https://www.janiesmill.com/pages/how-to-store-our-flours — dry whole-grain versus milled-flour storage.
- [G4] https://onedegreeorganics.com/sprouted-oats-faqs/ — steel-cut, rolled and quick forms; steam treatment can precede all three.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `oats_steelcut_rolled_instant`: Plain organic steel-cut, rolled and instant/quick oats remain eligible; choose texture and cooking time. “Steel-cut” does not mean unheated. Soaking and sprouting are different, and a traditional soak does not replace the product's cooking directions. [G4], [G6]
- `grains_beyond_rice`: “Hulled/hulless barley” retains the whole-grain form; “pearled” removes bran. Read grain species separately: barley and wheat-family grains retain gluten, while buckwheat is a different ingredient. Do not equate roasted groats or ordinary parboiled grain with sprouted grain. [G7], [G8], [G1]
- `sprouted_grains`: Require the named **sprouted whole** ingredient and distinguish dry kernels from flour or fresh sprouts. A visible tail is not required; sprouting does not imply ready-to-eat. Avoid the KB's worldwide legal absolute that “sprouted” has no regulated definition. [G1]
- `wild_rice`: Look for “hand-harvested,” “lake/river grown,” “wildcrafted/manoomin,” region and finishing method; cultivated wild rice is a different disclosed fallback. Broken/soup-grade is a use choice; color, wood parching and quick cooking alone do not establish wild harvest. Replace the universal seven-hour soak with producer-specific preparation. [G5]

BOUNDARY: The source's wildcrafted top rung does not itself establish organic status. Preserve the locked organic floor; flag a wild-harvested pack with undisclosed organic status as an unresolved sourcing qualification, not an automatic exception. If the builder wants wildcraft to substitute for organic, split that sourcing ladder explicitly. [P], [G5]

### F26 Dried pulses

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** “Organic dried [bean/pea/lentil],” with grower, crop/lot and whole/split form stated; prefer a small seasonal local grower and a preparation plan appropriate to that pulse. Sprouted dried pulses are an alternative where documented. [B1], [G1]
- **CLOSE:** Plain organic dried pulses with region and form stated but less crop detail; select whole versus intentionally split for the dish. [B2]
- **FLOOR:** Plain organic cooked pulses for convenience, with the pulse, water and optional salt; choose “no salt added” when desired. This is the adopted convenience rung, not proof of inferior nutrition. [P]
- **SKIP:** Flavored instant soup or sauce mixtures, damp/infested stock, unidentified bulk refills and the anonymous commercial pattern. Do not reject intentionally split peas/lentils as damaged whole beans. [B1], [B2]

BEYOND THE STORE: Farm-direct pulse packs and regional grower networks can disclose crop and origin; the organic requirement still needs its own evidence. [B1], [B2]

SEASON/STORAGE: Dry stock belongs in sealed cool/dry storage. Cooked packs and opened leftovers have different instructions; no family-wide seven-hour acidic soak or universal cooking time. [B1], [B2]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Global screen plus sauce/seasoning systems that replace the plain-pulse purchase. A “BPA-free” claim alone does not describe every feature of a can lining; glass remains a packaging preference, not a health guarantee. The prior canned-process FAQ failed this run, so avoid expanding lining claims. [B3, unverified]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Dried or ready-cooked? Whole or intentionally split? Does a red lentil necessarily fall apart? Must every pulse soak overnight? Does “no salt added” exist in a canned option?

SOURCES:

- [B1] https://www.ranchogordo.com/pages/faq-frequently-asked-questions — dry-bean storage and varied soaking/cooking practice; grower route.
- [B2] https://hodmedods.co.uk/products/split-green-peas — organic split peas, named growing source and optional soaking; cooking texture varies.
- [B3] https://www.edenfoods.com/faqs/view.php?categories_id=5 — **[unverified]**, 403 this run; earlier rounds documented soaked/no-salt cooked beans and formulation details. No new lining conclusion drawn.
- [P] https://www.westonaprice.org/health-topics/abcs-of-nutrition/shopping-guide-categories/ — adopted dried/canned organic pulse tiers.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `beans_dried_vs_canned`: “Whole, unbroken skins” applies to whole dry beans only. Plain no-salt cooked beans are a usable fallback; delete the claim that home cooking is the only way to control salt. Do not repeat the blanket slow-cooker instruction: follow bean-specific thorough cooking directions. [B1], [B3, unverified]
- `split_peas`: “Split/dehulled” is intentional. Choose split forms for soft soups/purees, whole lentils for retained shape; color alone does not identify form. Retain soaking as a traditional option, not a universal necessity or a seven-hour protocol borrowed from a rice answer. [B2]

### F27 Nuts, seeds and their simple mixtures

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** “Organic” named nuts/seeds, traceable grower or lot, and preparation stated for that species; prefer small local seasonal sources where available. Appropriate soaking followed by complete drying is a traditional option, not a mandatory method for every seed or a proven universal nutrient ranking. [N1]
- **CLOSE:** Sealed organic raw or dry-roasted nuts/seeds, or simple mixtures/butters made from them, with treatment and freshness information. “Raw” alone is incomplete process information. [N2], [N3]
- **FLOOR:** A fresh sealed organic product whose actual list is nuts/seeds, optional salt and, for fruit mixtures, qualifying dried fruit. Native separated nut oil is not an added roasting oil. [N3], [N4]
- **SKIP:** Added roasting/stabilizing oils, sweet coatings, stale stock and anonymous warm open displays; apply the global screen. A no-oil formula is the family rule even when another family's cooking oil is acceptable. [N1], [N3]

BEYOND THE STORE: Direct nut growers or small grinders can offer treatment/source information; a co-op grinding machine is not automatically fresh, organic or additive-free. Choose only when the displayed ingredient/source information supports the same checks. [N2], [N3]

SEASON/STORAGE: No common harvest month or shelf life. Keep packages closed and follow the form's storage directions; ground seed, nut butter and moist fruit mixtures cannot inherit one fixed dry-nut deadline. [N1], [N3], [N5]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Global screen plus oil coatings, candy/yogurt coatings and concealed sweetening/oil in dried-fruit subingredients. Do not convert the whole-seed allowance into permission for added extracted seed oil. [N3], [N4]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Does raw mean unheated? Are activated nuts fully dried? Is separated nut-butter oil normal? Can dry-roasted nuts qualify? Do chia and flax need the same preparation? Are the fruit pieces sweetened or oiled?

SOURCES:

ITEM DELTAS:

### F28 Flour: ingredient identity and preparation

RULE (true for the member's grain, nut and root branches):

- **BEST:** Organic flour naming its actual grain/nut/root, with small-mill/grower origin, recent milling and a preparation suited to the ingredient and recipe. Grain flour's positive cue is “whole/100% extraction”; that wording cannot classify almond or cassava flour. [G2], [F1]
- **CLOSE:** The same identified organic flour in a protected pack with process disclosed; for grain flour, “whole sprouted [grain] flour” is an alternative preparation route. [G1]
- **FLOOR:** Plain organic flour of the named ingredient, with recipe and storage directions, rather than an unidentified baking blend. Freshness and a suitable recipe matter more than “ancient,” “stoneground” or “grain-free” alone. [G2], [F1]
- **SKIP:** Refined grain flour presented as whole, stale meal and starch/gum/isolate mixtures presented as a single flour; apply the global screen within the intended recipe's scope. [G2], [F1]

BEYOND THE STORE: A regional farm mill can provide milling/extraction detail; do not prescribe local sourcing for every nut or tropical root. [G2]

SEASON/STORAGE: No common season. Follow the particular flour's package; whole-grain flour benefits from small purchases and airtight cold storage. Do not invent an “only days” deadline for every flour. [G3]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Global screen plus misleading whole-grain identity. A deliberately chosen gluten-free baking blend is a separate decision, not automatically defective because it contains a functional binder. [F1]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Does stoneground mean whole? Is whole einkorn different from all-purpose einkorn? Is almond flour a grain? Is cassava flour tapioca starch? Can these flours replace wheat one-for-one?

SOURCES:

- [G2] https://www.janiesmill.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions — 100% extraction versus sifted flour, independent of stone milling.
- [G1] https://secondspringfoods.com/sprouting-process/ — dried whole sprouted flour.
- [G3] https://www.janiesmill.com/pages/how-to-store-our-flours — flour storage.
- [F1] https://www.ottosnaturals.com/ — dried ground cassava root versus isolated tapioca starch and different recipe functions.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `specialty_flours`: Grain branch: whole organic flour for suitable soaking/fermentation, or whole sprouted flour; fresh milling alone is not preparation. Nut branch: organic named nut, grind/skin status and freshness; root branch: named processed root flour, not isolated starch. Remove the rounded fat-percentage shortcut. [G1], [G2], [F1]
  Boundary: The current “whole grain” family name excludes part of its own card. Proposed split: grain flour versus nut/root recipe flours, or retain this broader identity rule. Spelt/einkorn gluten and nut-allergen distinctions remain ingredient-level; sprouting does not make wheat gluten-free. [G1]; [sprouting FAQ](https://secondspringfoods.com/faq/)

### F29 Fermented or sprouted whole-grain baking

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** Organic whole-grain baking from a small regional baker using disclosed grain/mill information and genuine fermentation **or** sprouting/appropriate soaking. Read all grain ingredients, not brown color, seeds on top or “multigrain.” [G2], [R1], [R2]
- **CLOSE:** The same whole-grain and preparation standard with less mill/crop detail; fresh, chilled or frozen formats may qualify. Neither fresh milling nor sourdough is proven universally superior to every well-kept sprouted product. [G3], [R2]
- **FLOOR:** A simple organic whole-grain product retaining its promised identity. Preparation may be unspecified for ordinary loaves, rolls, bagels, crackers or pretzels; a product sold as sourdough or sprouted must still establish that process. [R1], [R2]
- **SKIP:** Refined-flour-led commercial formulas, isolated gluten/bran used to simulate whole grain, conditioner-heavy lists and the anonymous commercial pattern. “Sourdough flavor” does not establish starter fermentation. [P], [R1]

BEYOND THE STORE: Small bakeries and co-op bread/freezer shelves can supply qualifying labeled products. Select from a printed ingredient sheet or pack; do not make a conversation with a baker the default shopping step. [R1], [R2]

SEASON/STORAGE: No universal shelf life: soft loaves/bagels and dry crackers/hard pretzels differ. Use each pack's fresh/thawed instructions and freeze suitable soft products for longer keeping; keep dry crisp products sealed. [R2], [R3], [R4]

SHARED SKIP LIST: Global screen plus misleading flour and fermentation claims. No family-wide “no yeast,” “no fat,” “no eggs/milk,” or “no sweetener”: the item standards differ. [R1], [R3], [R4]

SHOPPER QUESTIONS: Does multigrain mean whole? Was the final dough fermented? Does starter on a list establish sourdough? Is sprouted flour allowed? Can frozen bread qualify? Do toppings or an alkaline bath reveal the flour?

SOURCES:

- [R4] https://www.farmhouseonboone.com/the-best-soft-sourdough-pretzels/ — final-dough fermentation, alkaline bath, wash and freezing; its recipe does not itself establish a whole-grain retail rung.
- [R5] https://aberlehome.com/whole-wheat-sourdough-bagels/ — whole-wheat bagels, boiling, optional recipe ingredients and freezing.

ITEM DELTAS:

- `sandwich_bread`: Whole-grain sourdough or sprouted loaf first; simple organic whole-grain yeast loaf is the identity-preserving fallback. Inspect every flour; a whole-grain first ingredient does not mean every grain ingredient is whole. [G2], [P]
- `sourdough`: Require disclosed live-starter fermentation under the adopted craft standard; acid/flavor-only yeast bread is not its fallback. Starter contains yeast, so “no commercial yeast” is not “yeast-free.” Flour/water/salt labeling may include starter implicitly; a missing separate starter word is not conclusive. [R1]
- `bagels`: Whole-grain dough, no added gluten; toppings and boiling do not establish flour quality. A little malt/honey in a traditional method is not evidence of harm, although a no-added-sugar choice may still be preferred. Slice/freeze excess according to instructions. [R5]
- `sprouted_grain_bread`: Accept organic sprouted whole grains **or whole sprouted flour**; remove the blanket “no flour” ban. Inspect unsprouted additions without automatically rejecting a whole-grain starter. Retain sprouted identity at the floor; flourless does not mean gluten-free. [G1], [R2]
- `buns_rolls`: Whole grain and no added sugar are the house targets. Butter, milk or eggs can be ordinary recipe ingredients; “brioche,” “potato” and a pale color do not settle flour identity. [R6]
- `crackers`: Olive oil, butter or coconut oil/no added fat under the existing card's fuller list. Check topping oils too. A cracker made entirely from fermented discard needs no invented second overnight fermentation; newly added flour is a separate process question. [R3]
- `pretzels`: Whole-wheat flour and the actual fermentation process matter more than gloss or twist shape. Alkaline bath and egg wash are not automatically unwanted additives; hard/soft forms require different keeping. [R4]

BOUNDARY: Proposed split within F29: ordinary whole-grain bakery/snacks with a plain-yeast floor; starter-defined sourdough; and sprouted-defined bread. A single “ordinary yeast bread” floor would reverse the latter two identities. No new card IDs assigned. [R1], [R2]

### F31 Fresh egg pasta

### F32 Masa foods

RULE (true for all members):

- **BEST:** “Organic corn,” actual nixtamalization and a short formula from a small maker disclosing the corn source; fresh wet masa is the closest-process preference where available. Look for “nixtamalized,” “lime-treated corn,” or an explicit masa-process statement. [M1], [M2]

## AISLE BRIEFING

1. Start with the back label's decisive identity: whole grain/flour, named pulse/nut/fruit, every fat, or the actual sweetener; source and process are separate checks. [G2], [M1], [S4]
2. Organic is the floor; prefer disclosed small seasonal local production where real, then regional traceability. An anonymous year-round commercial pack with no useful origin/process detail is the stance's skip pattern, not proof of contamination. [P]
3. Sprouted, sourdough, masa, raw, cold and pure each answer a different question. Read ingredients and the specific process together; use the global avoid list with its exceptions. [G1], [R1], [M1], [S1]
4. Match storage to form: dry kernels, milled flour, soft bread, fresh pasta, frozen potatoes, rendered fat and opened fruit products do not share one temperature or deadline. [G3], [E1], [O3], [U3]
5. Keep the shopper on the shelf or printed product sheet. Where a crucial claim is missing, choose the qualifying disclosed fallback; do not replace missing evidence with obligatory counter questions or an invented available product.

- F25: wildcrafted does not establish organic; the source's wild-harvest preference and the locked floor require an explicit qualification. Plain instant oats remain eligible despite another guide's broad quick-grain rejection. The seven-hour soak is not universal. [G4], [G5], [P]
- F26: “unbroken skins” cannot cover intentionally split pulses; the split is by form, not color. The canned-process URL failed and is marked above. [B2]
