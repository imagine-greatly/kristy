# List Philosophy — The User's List, Kristy's Voice, Subtle Over Time

The List is a lightweight feature ON TOP of the main Counter/whole-store moat — but it's where Kristy's VOICE shows up in planning. Governing principle: the user knows themselves better than Kristy ever will. The List is THEIR list; Kristy sharpens it, never dictates it. She still speaks up (flag once, offer better, respect the choice), and she nudges goals SUBTLY over weeks, never in one exhausting overhaul. Build AFTER the Counter redesign — it's lower leverage, and the work is mostly restraint. Standing rules: claim lock, VOICE_SPEC (egoless, no first person, no em-dash, brief), no-treatment, one commit per block.

---

## THE PRINCIPLE

Them above everything. The user's list is the foundation — what they know they want and will actually eat. Kristy's job is to improve it gently and cumulatively, not to impose an ideal cart. Two things that are NOT in tension and must both be true:
1. The user's authority is absolute — their items stay, their choices are respected, never struck or nagged.
2. Kristy still has a voice — she flags a questionable item ONCE, offers a better option, then honors the choice and moves on.
A good friend does both: tells you the truth once, then lets you live your life. That's the tone.

---

## BLOCK 1 — Kristy's voice on the list: flag once, offer better, respect the choice

```
The List must carry Kristy's opinion without taking over. When a user adds a questionable item (Lucky Charms, a Snickers bag, soda), Kristy speaks up like a friend — once, briefly — then respects the choice completely.

1) THE ITEM ALWAYS STAYS. A user-added item is NEVER removed, struck, or replaced. They're buying it; that's final. Kristy adds a note/suggestion NEXT TO it, never touches the item itself.

2) FLAG ONCE, NOT EVERY TIME. When a questionable item is added, Kristy notes it on that action — not every time the list loads. Re-nagging the same item is the #1 way to make the app feel judgmental. One mention, then silence.

3) OFFER A BETTER OPTION, BRIEFLY, NO GUILT:
   - "Lucky Charms stays. If the kids are open to it, [cleaner cereal] is a solid swap."
   - "Snickers is on there. [Better option] if you want the same thing cleaner."
   One line. A door held open, not a correction. No paragraph about sugar/dyes, no guilt, no lecture. The tone is a friend mentioning, not a parent scolding.

4) RESPECT SIGNALS: if the user keeps the item (doesn't take the swap), Kristy drops it entirely — no re-flag, no passive-aggressive reminder. The choice is honored.

5) THIS IS KRISTY IN THE LIST: she always has an opinion (that's the brand), but on the List the opinion is OFFERED, never enforced. Egoless, no first person, brief.

Verify: a flagged item (Lucky Charms/Snickers) stays on the list, gets ONE brief swap offer, no guilt language, and is never re-nagged on reload or if the user keeps it; the item is never removed or replaced automatically.
```

---

## BLOCK 2 — Subtle goal weighting, not a rewrite

```
Goals should shape the list GENTLY and cumulatively, not overhaul it. Nobody hits their goals in one haul. Dial DOWN how hard the list pushes and dial UP how much it defers to the user.

1) THE USER'S LIST IS THE SPINE. What they entered is the foundation; goals weight the MARGINAL choices, they don't replace the list. If someone builds a list of what they want, Kristy specifies and gently improves it — she doesn't swap it for her ideal cart.

2) MARGINAL NUDGE, NOT OVERHAUL. Goals influence the specifics ("rice" → lean toward brown for a whole-food goal) and what Kristy OFFERS to add — but subtly. A high-protein user's list leans a bit more protein over time; it doesn't get every item rewritten to maximize protein.

3) SUBTLE = BARELY NOTICED, EFFECTIVE = DRIFTS BETTER OVER MONTHS. The goal is a list that feels like the user's own, that quietly gets a little better each week. Not a perfect cart every trip. If the coaching is loud, it's too much.

4) NEVER SCOLD. The List voice is a quiet suggestion, never a correction. Same tone as Block 1 — offered, not enforced.

Verify: a user-built list stays recognizably theirs after goal weighting (not replaced by an ideal cart); goals affect marginal specifics and offers subtly; the push is gentle, not an overhaul; no scolding tone anywhere.
```

---

## BLOCK 3 — Groundwork for over-time drift (the differentiator)

```
The List's real long-term edge: it remembers the user's actual patterns and nudges from THERE, cumulatively — not from a blank ideal each trip. This is what makes "subtle yet effective over weeks" real. Lay the groundwork; full personalization can grow over time.

1) REMEMBER REAL PATTERNS: what the user actually buys/keeps trip to trip (from haul_scans, kept list items, accepted/declined swaps). Store it as the user's real baseline.
2) NUDGE FROM THE BASELINE, cumulatively: each trip leans SLIGHTLY better than their actual last trip — not toward a perfect ideal. If they always buy white rice, maybe once brown is offered; mostly their pattern is respected and the drift is gradual.
3) LEARN FROM DECLINES: if a user consistently declines a swap (keeps their white rice), Kristy stops offering it. Respect over repetition — a declined suggestion is a preference learned, not a thing to keep pushing.
4) DON'T OVER-BUILD NOW: capture the patterns and respect declines; the sophisticated drift can deepen with real usage. The point is groundwork — memory of the user's real self, and gentleness that compounds.

Verify: the list can reference the user's real buying patterns; nudges lean from their actual baseline not a blank ideal; a repeatedly-declined swap stops being offered; nothing overhauls the user's established preferences.
```
