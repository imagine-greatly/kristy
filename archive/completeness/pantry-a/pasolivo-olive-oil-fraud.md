## **What Is Olive Oil Fraud?**

Olive oil fraud occurs when an olive oil is mislabeled, adulterated, diluted, or falsely marketed as extra virgin olive oil when it does not meet extra virgin standards.

Some sources even trace it all the way back to ancient Rome. Today, it happens on a massive scale—to the tune of the 260,000 counterfeit liters authorities seized last winter and deemed “unfit for consumption.”

The issue is so prevalent that Italian authorities recently launched a full investigation into seven of the country’s leading olive oil producers and their practices.

> According to **[Europol](https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/11-olive-oil-counterfeiters-arrested-following-operation-opson)**, factors like price inflation, reduced olive oil production, and increasing demand have created “the perfect breeding ground for fraudulent producers.”

It’s not just a problem that exists abroad; even in the U.S., there is a good chance phony olive oil might end up on your pantry shelves. With imported, uncertified, and largely unregulated oils lining grocery store shelves, it’s up to you to be a [smart shopper.](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/olive-oil-buying-guide-5-facts)

Understanding the most common types of olive oil fraud can help you [avoid misleading labels and make more informed purchasing decisions.](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/olive-oil-buying-guide-5-facts)

## **What Are the Most Common Types of Olive Oil Fraud?**

The most common types of olive oil fraud include mislabeling lower-grade oils as extra virgin olive oil, adulterating olive oil with cheaper seed oils, and misleading consumers about an oil’s true origin.

### **1\. Mislabeling Lower-Grade Olive Oil as Extra Virgin**

#### **What Does “Extra Virgin” Olive Oil Really Mean?**

[Extra virgin olive oil](https://www.pasolivo.com/collections/california-olive-oil "Pasolivo California Extra Virgin Olive Oil") is the highest grade of olive oil. To qualify as extra virgin, an olive oil must be mechanically extracted without excessive heat or chemicals and must meet strict standards for flavor, aroma, and free fatty acidity.

> Larry Olmsted, author of _Real Food Fake Food_, told **[_Epicurious_](https://www.epicurious.com/ingredients/seven-ways-to-tell-the-difference-between-real-and-fake-olive-oil-article)** that he defines fraud as “whenever you get something other than what you think you're buying.”

That can mean many different things across many different facets of the food industry. In the world of olive oil, this often means buying a product that claims to be extra virgin, but is actually either a low-grade oil or one that has been cut with filler seed oils.

With imported oils that pass under the radar of third-party certifying bodies, the line becomes even blurrier.

#### **How Some Producers Stretch the Definition of Extra Virgin**

To be considered extra virgin, an olive oil must meet several criteria, including being free of sensory defects and not having undergone chemical manipulation.

But new refinement technology has made it possible to [pass sub-par oil off as extra virgin](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/what-is-lite-olive-oil "What is Extra Light Olive Oil"), despite the heavy chemical manipulation it has undergone. This deceptive practice allows low-quality oils to slip under the esteemed extra virgin umbrella.

> Tom Mueller, author of _Extra Virginity: The Sublime and Scandalous World of Olive Oil_, told the **[_New Yorker_](https://www.newyorker.com/books/page-turner/olive-oils-dark-side)** that this is gradually stretching the definition of extra virgin “beyond all meaning.”

### **2\. Adulterating Olive Oil with Seed Oils**

Another common practice involves [cutting olive oils with cheaper and less heart-healthy seed oils](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/olive-oil-buying-guide-5-facts), like canola, grapeseed, and sunflower oil. In most cases, you won’t find these oils listed on olive oil labels, because their disclosure is not legally enforced.

That’s a major issue, as filler oils compromise the smoke point, flavor, nutritional content, and health properties of the product you’re purchasing. They also contain potential allergens, and not disclosing them puts you as a consumer at risk.

Despite the clear cause for concern, Mueller found this practice to be “widespread” in the U.S. In part, that’s due to a lack of product testing by olive oil’s primary regulatory body: the FDA.

### **3\. Misrepresenting an Olive Oil's Country of Origin**

Another form of olive oil fraud involves misleading consumers about where an olive oil was grown, harvested, or produced. Some products marketed as Italian, Californian, or Mediterranean may contain oils sourced from multiple countries.

Without [transparency](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) and [third-party verification](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/awards), it can be difficult for consumers to determine an oil’s true origin. Knowing exactly where your olive oil comes from is an important indicator of quality and authenticity.

## **How Is Olive Oil Regulated in the United States?**

Olive oil in the United States is regulated primarily by the FDA, but enforcement and testing are limited compared to some international markets. As a result, third-party certification programs often play an important role in verifying olive oil quality and authenticity.

Shoppers like you trust the FDA to enforce clear and honest olive oil labeling in the U.S. However, conscientious consumers and producers alike are pushing for more transparency and better protections against fraud.

The American Olive Oil Producers Association (AOOPA), North American Olive Oil Association (NAOOA), and the world’s largest olive oil producer, Deoleo, recently [petitioned the FDA](https://www.aboutoliveoil.org/olive-oil-industry-leaders-unite-to-petition-fda-for-first-ever-u.s.-standards) for stricter labeling standards. They also announced the U.S.’s largest-ever [olive oil testing study](https://518490.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/518490/NAOOA%20Testing%20Program%20Release%20FINAL%2011.13.23.pdf).

Both of these actions are steps forward in improving transparency between olive oil producers and consumers.

Beyond FDA guidelines, third-party organizations such as the [California Olive Oil Council](https://cooc.com/about-the-seal/) also provide confidence behind an olive oil’s quality. They conduct strict testing and give seals of approval to EVOOs that meet their high standards—including [California olive oils](https://www.pasolivo.com/collections/california-olive-oil) from Pasolivo.

Many of the oils you’ll find on grocery store shelves that claim to be Californian are actually imported and never receive certification from the COOC. Checking for that seal of approval in the COOC’s [online registry](https://cooc.com/certified-oils/) is a great place to start.

## **How Can You Tell If Olive Oil Is Real?**

To tell if olive oil is real, look for producer transparency, harvest information, clear origin details, and third-party certifications. Avoid relying on marketing buzzwords alone.

### **1\. Shop Small and Straight From the Source**

Choose [smaller producers](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/our-story), which typically provide more transparency into their production process and let you taste before you buy.

> As Mueller puts it, “In an industry where the label means so little, personal trust in the people who have made and sold it is important.”

### **2\. Outsmart Empty Marketing Terms**

Steer clear of buzzwords like “light,” which don’t officially ensure anything in terms of quality. (Read more: **_[What Is Extra Light Olive Oil?](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/what-is-lite-olive-oil)_**)

### **3\. Look for Harvest and Origin Information**

The more info you can find—the estate where the olives were grown and milled, the harvest date, or the olive varieties featured in the oil—the better. If you can’t find them on the bottle, try looking at the producer’s website or [speaking directly to the source](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/contact).

### **4\. Look for Third-Party Certifications:**

For California olive oils, the COOC seal of approval is the gold standard. All of Pasolivo’s EVOOs have received this certification. Furthermore, all of Pasolivo's flavored olive oils either use our [COOC certified EVOO as a base, or go through the same milling process as our certifed EVOO.](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest)

## **Does the Olive Oil Fridge Test Work?**

No, the olive oil fridge test is not a reliable way to determine whether olive oil is real, authentic, or high quality. You may have heard of the olive oil fridge test, popularized by Dr. Oz back in 2013 and revived on TikTok in recent months.

The test goes like this: Place a bottle of EVOO in the refrigerator overnight. If it solidifies, the oil is a true extra virgin olive oil. If not, it’s likely adulterated or cut with refined oils.

Industry experts quickly debunked this test when Dr. Oz’s episode first aired:

> Dan Flynn, former executive director of the University of California Davis Olive Center, told the **[_Olive Oil Times_](https://www.oliveoiltimes.com/world/olive-oil-fridge-test/32830)**, “While it is true that refined oils will not coagulate in the cold, it is also true that some olive oil varieties will not coagulate either.”
>
> The **[North American Olive Oil Association](https://www.aboutoliveoil.org/extra-virgin-olive-oil-fridge-test-myth)** denounced the test as “completely false and misleading,” noting that extra virgin olive oil will solidify at “a wide variety of time and temperature exposures.”

Factors such as olive variety, growing conditions, harvest timing, and more all play a role in the chemical makeup of an EVOO, affecting its solidification point.Ultimately, the fridge test is not a reliable way to check an olive oil’s authenticity or quality. Instead, stick to the guidelines above to make sure you’re buying the very best.

## **What Makes High-Quality Olive Oil?**

High-quality olive oil begins with healthy olives, careful harvesting practices, rapid milling, proper storage, and independent quality testing. Each step in the production process plays an important role in preserving freshness, flavor, and nutritional value.

### **1\. Freshly Harvested Olives**

The best extra virgin olive oils start with olives that are [harvested](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) at the optimal stage of ripeness and handled carefully to minimize bruising and oxidation.

### **2\. Rapid Milling**

After harvest, olives should be [milled](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) as quickly as possible. Prompt milling helps preserve antioxidants, polyphenols, and the vibrant flavor characteristics associated with premium extra virgin olive oil.

### **3\. Proper Storage**

High-quality olive oil should be [stored](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/spring-pantry-refresh-how-to-correctly-store-your-olive-oil) away from light, heat, and oxygen. Dark glass bottles and [temperature-controlled stainless steel storage tanks](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) help protect freshness and extend shelf life.

### **4\. Independent Quality Testing**

[Independent laboratory testing](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/awards) can verify important quality markers such as free fatty acidity and ensure an olive oil meets extra virgin standards.

## **Frequently Asked Questions About Olive Oil Fraud and Authenticity**

#### **1\. What Is Olive Oil Fraud?**

Olive oil fraud occurs when olive oil is mislabeled, diluted, adulterated, or falsely marketed as extra virgin olive oil when it does not meet extra virgin standards. Common examples include blending olive oil with cheaper seed oils, selling lower-grade oils as extra virgin, or misrepresenting an oil's origin.

#### **2\. How Common Is Olive Oil Fraud?**

Olive oil fraud has been documented for centuries and remains a concern in global olive oil markets today. Rising olive oil prices, limited harvests, and growing consumer demand can create opportunities for fraudulent practices. **Olive is Europe's #1 most adulterated and counterfeited food product.**

#### **3\. What Does Extra Virgin Olive Oil Mean?**

[Extra virgin olive oil](https://www.pasolivo.com/collections/california-olive-oil) is the highest grade of olive oil. To qualify as extra virgin, the oil must be mechanically extracted without excessive heat or chemical processing and must meet strict standards for flavor, aroma, and quality. Authentic extra virgin olive oil contains naturally occurring antioxidants, polyphenols, and heart-healthy monounsaturated fats.

#### **4\. How Can I Tell If Olive Oil Is Authentic?**

To tell if olive oil is real, look for transparency from the producer, harvest and origin information, and third-party certifications such as the California Olive Oil Council (COOC) seal. [Reputable producers](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) should be able to explain where their olives were grown, how the oil was produced, and how quality is verified.

> Read: [5 Surprising Olive Oil Facts That Will Change How You Shop.](https://www.pasolivo.com/blogs/blog/olive-oil-buying-guide-5-facts)

#### **5\. Does the Olive Oil Fridge Test Work?**

No, the olive oil fridge test is not a reliable way to determine whether olive oil is authentic or high quality. Different olive varieties solidify at different temperatures, and factors such as [harvest timing and growing conditions](https://www.pasolivo.com/pages/olive-harvest) can affect how an oil reacts in the refrigerator. Experts and industry organizations have repeatedly debunked this test.

#### **6\. Why Does Olive Oil Adulteration Matter?**

Olive oil adulteration matters because it can reduce the flavor, nutritional value, and health benefits consumers expect from authentic extra virgin olive oil. Adulterated oils may contain lower-quality ingredients, undisclosed seed oils, or fewer beneficial antioxidants and polyphenols. Consumers may also end up paying premium prices for products that do not meet extra virgin standards.

#### **7\. What Is the COOC Seal?**

The [California Olive Oil Council (COOC) seal](https://cooc.com/about-the-seal/) is a third-party certification that verifies an olive oil meets strict extra virgin olive oil standards. Certified oils undergo laboratory analysis and sensory evaluation to confirm quality and authenticity. For consumers, the COOC seal provides added confidence that the olive oil has been independently tested.

#### **8\. What Makes High-Quality Olive Oil?**

High-quality olive oil starts with carefully grown olives that are harvested at the right time and milled quickly after picking. Proper storage, minimal exposure to heat and light, and independent quality testing also help preserve freshness and flavor. Transparent sourcing and third-party certifications are additional indicators of a premium extra virgin olive oil.

#### **9\. Is California Olive Oil Better Than Imported Olive Oil?**

[California extra virgin olive oil](https://www.pasolivo.com/collections/california-olive-oil) often benefits from greater transparency, stricter quality standards, and third-party certification programs such as the COOC. The most important factor is not where the oil comes from, but whether the producer can verify its quality, freshness, and authenticity.
