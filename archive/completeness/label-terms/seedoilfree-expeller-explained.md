Seed Oil Education

A practical guide to what expeller-pressed oils are, how they are mechanically extracted, and how they differ from solvent-extracted and cold-pressed oils.

## Expeller-Pressed Oil Explained

Expeller-pressed oil is oil extracted through a mechanical pressing process rather than solvent extraction. In this method, seeds or plant materials are physically pressed to release oil.

The process uses pressure and friction generated by a mechanical press. Depending on the oil type and manufacturer, some heat may naturally develop during pressing.

## How Expeller Pressing Works

In expeller pressing, seeds or plant material are fed through a mechanical screw press that applies pressure to release oil. The oil is separated while the remaining solid material is removed.

After pressing, the oil may still be filtered, refined, deodorized, or otherwise processed depending on the intended final product.

### Mechanical Extraction

Oil is physically pressed from the seed or plant material without chemical solvent extraction.

### Commercial Production

Expeller pressing is used in both large-scale and specialty oil production depending on the oil type.

## Expeller-Pressed Vs Cold-Pressed

Both expeller-pressed and cold-pressed oils use mechanical extraction rather than solvent extraction. However, cold-pressed oils are generally associated with lower heat exposure during production.

Expeller pressing can generate more friction and heat during extraction depending on the equipment and production scale.

## Expeller-Pressed Vs Solvent-Extracted Oils

### Expeller-Pressed Oils

- Mechanical extraction
- No chemical solvent extraction
- Physical pressing process
- Can still be refined
- Common in specialty oils

### Solvent-Extracted Oils

- Industrial solvent extraction
- Common in commodity oils
- Designed for high oil yield
- Frequently heavily refined
- Widely used in packaged foods

## Where Expeller-Pressed Oils Are Commonly Used

Expeller-pressed oils are commonly found in specialty grocery products, natural food products, premium cooking oils, sauces, dressings, and products emphasizing production methods or sourcing transparency.

### Cooking Oils

Sold as bottled oils for home cooking, dressings, sauteing, and finishing applications.

### Natural Foods

Often used in products marketed as natural, minimally processed, or specialty grocery products.

### Dressings & Sauces

Used in salad dressings, marinades, dips, sauces, and premium packaged foods.

## Expeller-Pressed Oil FAQ

### What does expeller-pressed mean?

Expeller-pressed means oil was mechanically extracted through physical pressing rather than chemical solvent extraction.

### Is expeller-pressed oil cold-pressed?

Not necessarily. Both use mechanical extraction, but cold-pressed oils are generally associated with lower heat exposure during production.

### Does expeller pressing use hexane?

Expeller pressing itself does not use solvent extraction. However, some products may still undergo additional refining after pressing.

### Can expeller-pressed oils still be refined?

Yes. Some expeller-pressed oils are filtered or refined after extraction depending on the intended final product.

### What oils are commonly expeller-pressed?

Sunflower oil, canola oil, sesame oil, avocado oil, coconut oil, and peanut oil are commonly sold in expeller-pressed forms.

## Final Thoughts

Expeller-pressed oils are mechanically extracted oils that avoid chemical solvent extraction during the pressing stage. Understanding terms like expeller-pressed, cold-pressed, and refined can help consumers better understand oil production and food labeling.
