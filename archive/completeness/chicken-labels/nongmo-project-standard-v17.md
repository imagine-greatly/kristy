1 Introduction

veriﬁcation and trustworthy education that empowers people to care for themselves, the planet, 
and future generations. 
In support of our mission, the Non- GMO Project offers a Product Veriﬁcation Program (PVP) 
whereby Participants may enroll wholesale and retail Products for evaluation against, and 
determination of compliance with, the Non- GMO Project Standard. The PVP als o includes the Non-
GMO Project Program Rules and Procedures, the Non- GMO Project Trademark Use Guide, and the 
Non-GMO Project Trademark License and Program Participation Agreement, collectively referred to 
as Program Documents. Where applicable, a written agreement between the Participant and one or 
more Technical Administrators (TAs) is also required. If all elements of  the PVP are satisﬁed, 
including meeting the compliance requirements set forth by the Non -GMO Project Standard, 
Products may attain Non-GMO Project veriﬁcation.

programs. The surveillance program routinely tests Veriﬁed Products and Inputs and Ingredients 
thereof, for compliance with the Action Thresholds outlined in the N on-GMO Project Standard. The 
auditing program is in place to ensure that the appropriate supporting documentation associated 
with Veriﬁed Products is on ﬁle and fulﬁlls the requirements of the PVP .

1.1 Purpose 
The purpose of the Standard is to offer meaning and value to the marketing claim “ Non-GMO 
Project Veriﬁed” by creating, maintaining, and keeping publicly available, a set of rigorous 
requirements against which all Non -GMO Project Veriﬁed Products are measured. 
1.2 Deﬁning Genetically Modiﬁed Organism 
The Standard employs a deﬁnition of Genetically Modiﬁed Organism (GMO) that focuses on the 
application of Biotechnology rather than comparing the observable or measurable traits of an 
engineered organism to those of a “natural” organism. For purposes of the Standard, Biotechnology 
is deﬁned in Appendix A and further illustrated in Appendix C. Under this approach, any organism 
modiﬁed using Biotechnology, such as through recombinant DNA techniques or new genomic 
techniques (e.g., CRISPR, TALEN) is classiﬁed as a GMO, along with its derivatives. For instance, if 
corn is Genetically Modiﬁed u sing techniques such as recombinant DNA or gene editing and then 
processed into corn oil, both the corn and the resulting oil are classiﬁed as Genetically Modiﬁed. 
This deﬁnition ensures a rigorous evaluation framework, emphasizing transparency, complia nce, 
and alignment with consumer expectations. 
1.3 Methodology and Approach of the Standard 
The Project’s PVP is based on a practice-oriented and process -oriented Standard that uses both 
testing and Affidavits as key strategic tools to conﬁrm that practices and processes meet 
requirements. 
Continuous improvement on the part of Participants is required with the common goal of 
eliminating any Inputs and Ingredients derived from GMOs from their supply chains.  
A Product is a unique branded formula and process, where process could be either the 
manufacturing or facility process. 
The breadth and depth of Product evaluation is informed by the nature of the Inputs and Ingredients 
that are represented in or present in the Product formulation. Inputs and Ingredients are classiﬁed 
according to three attributes: 1) weight percentage as represented in or present in the Product, 
2) likelihood that they are derived from a GMO, and 3) whether a testable precursor exists at any 
point in the supply chain. These three attributes are termed Weight Percentage, Risk Status, and 
T estability, respectively. Compliance of all Inputs and Ingredients within the scope of evaluation is 
required for veriﬁcation.

Activities occurring along the chain of custody (CoC) for Products and their Inputs and Ingredients

Although requiring compliance of all Inputs and Ingredients within the scope of evaluation, the

3.3 Testability 
Inputs and Ingredients are either Testable or Non -Testable. Testable Inputs and Ingredients have a 
point in the supply chain where the Input or Ingredient contains sufficient intact deoxyribonucleic 
acid (DNA) or protein to return valid molecular or immunological test results. Additionally, 
molecular tests or immunological tests are publicly commercially available to cover all events for 
which the Project requires testing. Non -Testable Inputs and Ingredients either lack a point in the 
supply chain with suff icient intact DNA or protein for valid molecular or immunological test results 
or do not have commercially available testing methods for the relevant GM events as required by 
the Project. Some organisms and their derivatives may meet the criteria for being  both Testable and 
Non-Testable, depending on the speciﬁc circumstances. 
3.3.1 For Testable High-Risk Inputs and Ingredients (including for use in pet food) other 
than animal feed, the molecular method polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is the 
only acceptable testing methodology. 
3.3.2 For Testable High-Risk Inputs and Ingredients to animal feed (other than pet food), 
either the molecular method PCR or immunological methods may be used to 
demonstrate compliance with the Action Threshold.

Appendix C – Biotechnology Techniques 
Appendix C – Biotechnology Techniques 
Appendix C serves as a classiﬁcation resource by identifying some techniques falling under points 
(a) and (b) of the deﬁnition of Biotechnology in Appendix A . Point (a) encompasses both genetic 
modiﬁcation techniques and their associated delivery methods, as delivery methods facilitate 
technique implementation and are an integral component of in vitro nucleic acid techniques.  
The techniques speciﬁed in Appendix C are not exhaustive; all techniques falling under the 
Appendix A  deﬁnition of Biotechnology are covered under the Standard.  
C.1 In Vitro Nucleic Acid Techniques 
C.1.1 Engineered Molecular Techniques and Approaches  
• Recombinant DNA techniques 
• Gene editing techniques (e.g., CRISPR -Cas systems, including base editing, prime editing, 
and epigenome editing; TALEN, ZFN, meganucleases)  
• Synthetic genomics (construction of whole genomes and chromosomes)  
• Oligonucleotide-directed mutagenesis (ODM)  
• RNA interference (RNAi) and RNA -dependent DNA methylation (RdDM)  
• Directed evolution techniques, when involving in vitro nucleic acid techniques  
• Gene drives (e.g., CRISPR-based systems designed for super -Mendelian inheritance) 
• Transgenesis, cisgenesis, intragenesis, and reverse breeding 
• Synthetic biology (including precision fermentation)  
C.1.2 Nucleic Acid Delivery Methods  
• Transformation (e.g., heat shock, calcium chloride, electroporation) 
• Agrobacterium-mediated transformation  
• Biolistics (gene gun, nanobiolistics)  
• Protoplast transformation (e.g., PEG -mediated uptake of nucleic acids)  
• Transfection (e.g., chemical methods, lipofection, calcium phosphate)  
• Transduction via viral vectors (e.g., adenoviral, lentiviral, retroviral systems)  
• Microinjection 
C.2 Cell Fusion 
• Protoplast fusion between cells from different taxonomic families
