To promote the top-ten, the Aquarium writes:\n\n“…the Super Green List includes ocean-friendly and nutritious seafood. Simple and straightforward, you can eat these species in good conscience — for your body and for the planet. These foods are high in omega-3 fatty acids, low in mercury, and, above all, are rated green by the [Monterey Bay Aquarium](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/) Seafood Watch program for environmental sustainability. (Note: Seaweed omega-3 levels vary by species.) They’re good for you and good for the planet. They’re Super Green!”\n\n**Seven of the ten Super Green rated seafoods are U.S. farmed!**\n\n- [Catfish](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-catfish)\n- [Clams](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-clams)\n- [Hybrid Striped Bass](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-striped-bass)\n- [Mussels](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-farmed-mussels)\n- [Oysters](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/oysters)\n- [Trout and Steelhead](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-rainbow-trout)\n- [Seaweed](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/seafood-basics/sustainable-healthy-fish/nutritious-seaweed)\n\n**In addition to the Super Green rankings, the Aquarium recognizes more U.S. farmed seafoods as Green or Best Buy consumer choices:**\n\n- **[Abalone](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/abalone/abalone-35507?species=119)**\n- [Almaco Jack](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/amberjack/almaco-jack-29821?species=361)\n- [Red swamp crawfish](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/crayfish/red-swamp-crayfish-31560?species=290)\n- [Red drum](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/drum/red-drum-35520?species=314)\n- [Yellow perch](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/perch/yellow-perch-26501?species=264)\n- [Florida pompano](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/pompano/florida-pompano-26503?species=391)\n- White leg shrimp ( [RAS](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/shrimp/whiteleg-shrimp-29994?species=156) and [ponds](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/shrimp/whiteleg-shrimp-36077?species=156))\n- Sturgeon ( [Russian](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/sturgeon/russian-sturgeon-30016?species=4), [Siberian](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/sturgeon/siberian-sturgeon-30018?species=2), [White](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/sturgeon/white-sturgeon-30028?species=8) – California ( [raceways](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/sturgeon/white-sturgeon-30024?species=8), red](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/tilapia/hybrid-red-tilapia-30045?species=227), [Mozambique](https://www.seafoodwatch.org/recommendation/tilapia/mozambique-tilapia-30033?species=226),
