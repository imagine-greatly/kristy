# Label Truth — Teach the Principle, Not Brand Verdicts

Kristy teaches users to SEE the gap between what a label implies and what it guarantees — so they can evaluate any brand themselves. This gets the value of "know which brands are really good" without hardcoding legally-dangerous verdicts about named companies. More holistic anyway: a coach who makes you competent beats one who memorized a list. Uses the existing perimeter KB + label-decoding. Standing rules: claim lock, VOICE_SPEC, no-treatment, one commit.

---

## THE PRINCIPLE (why this shape)

Do NOT hardcode negative claims about named brands ("Brand X feeds soy"). Those are checkable factual assertions about real companies — commercial-disparagement risk, they go stale when brands reformulate, and they're the hardest claims to source and defend. Instead, Kristy teaches the LABEL-LEVEL truth: what a term legally requires vs. what shoppers assume, and what to look for to close the gap. This is educational, defensible, never goes stale, and makes users smarter at EVERY brand.

```
Extend the perimeter KB's label-decoding with the gaps between what labels IMPLY and what they GUARANTEE, so Kristy teaches users to evaluate any product/brand themselves. Educational, about the label CATEGORY, never a claim against a named brand.

1) FEED TRUTH (eggs, poultry, dairy, beef) — the big one:
   - "Pasture-raised" / "free-range" / "cage-free" tell you about the animal's SPACE, not its FEED. Most pasture-raised hens are still fed soy and corn.
   - What to look for if feed matters: "soy-free", "corn-free" on the label; grass-FINISHED (not just grass-fed) for beef.
   - Kristy's voice, teaching not brand-shaming: "'Pasture-raised' means she went outside — not what she ate. Most are still on soy and corn. If that matters to you, the word to find is 'soy-free.'"

2) "ORGANIC" SCOPE:
   - What organic certification does and doesn't cover; organic still allows certain inputs; organic ≠ automatically nutrient-dense or well-raised.
   - Where organic matters most (thin-skin high-residue produce) vs. least — you may already have the dirty-dozen-honest entry; align with it.

3) PRODUCE — buy by ripeness/season/handling, not brand or origin myths:
   - Avocados: judged by ripeness and variety, not a brand. (Do NOT claim a specific origin is "worse" — Hass is a variety grown in multiple regions; the CA-vs-Mexico difference is mostly season/ripeness, not quality. Teach how to pick a good one, don't rank origins.)
   - General: how to actually tell good produce (firmness, color, season) — the skill, not a brand list.

4) OTHER HIGH-CONFUSION LABEL TERMS (extend existing label_terms_decoded):
   - "Natural" (means almost nothing), "made with real X" (X can be a tiny fraction), "no added hormones" (already banned in poultry/pork — a marketing non-claim), "multigrain" vs "whole grain", "lightly sweetened", "no artificial flavors" (natural flavors still allowed).

5) HARD RULES:
   - NO negative factual claims about named brands. Ever. Teach the label category, not "Brand X is bad."
   - Where Kristy references what a term legally requires, it must be accurate and sourced (regulatory definitions), tiered honestly.
   - If POSITIVE brand guidance is ever wanted later, it's a separate, sourced, dated, maintained effort — and framed as recommendation ("brands that disclose soy-free feed"), never disparagement. NOT in this block.
   - No-treatment / claim-lock unchanged. This teaches label literacy, makes no health-outcome claims.

6) SURFACE IT: these teachings appear where relevant — on a scanned egg carton ("here's what pasture-raised does and doesn't tell you"), when a user asks about a category, and as education isms. On the list, an egg or beef item can carry the "what to look for" tip.
```

## Note for Devon

Your instinct — "some brands aren't as good as they market themselves" — is 100% right and it's exactly what a real holistic coach knows. The safe, better way to deliver it is teaching the LABEL truth: "pasture-raised doesn't mean soy-free, here's the word to look for." That's the same insight that made you skeptical of Vital Farms, but delivered as a skill the user keeps, applied to every brand, with zero legal exposure and nothing that goes stale.

Two reasons it's actually BETTER than a brand list:
1. It makes the user competent — they can now evaluate any egg carton, not just the ones you rated. That's the "teach to fish" depth from the VISION doc, and it's the stickiest kind of coaching.
2. It never goes wrong. A brand reformulates and your "Brand X is bad" becomes a false, liable claim. "Pasture-raised means space not feed" is true forever.

On the avocado example specifically: that one's a caution, not a feature. Hass is a variety grown in California AND Mexico; "Mexican better than Hass" mixes up variety and origin and is probably just wrong. It's a perfect illustration of why hardcoded specific claims are dangerous — teach how to pick a ripe avocado instead, which is true and useful.

Brand-level POSITIVE recommendations (sourced, dated, "these brands disclose X") can come post-launch as a real content effort if you want them. Negative brand claims — don't, at any point, without a lawyer.
